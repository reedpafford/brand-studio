# What the FOMO-Producer learned (harvest, 1 Nov)

From two batches of fomo videos made with Reed on the live site (29–30 Oct: 7 shorts, 4 song videos). One story's own logic (why a fall happens, the stakes, the payoff) is left out: Reed keeps that per video.

## 1. General craft (any brand)

**Script.** Open on the conflict, no setup. Every beat has something happening (a shove, a splash, things flying), not people standing and talking. Keep edgy physical comedy. The money climbs in figures that each beat states, with one dip before the payoff. Payoffs are invented for this story, never the same run of car, penthouse and jet. A time jump gets a white flash. Before the end line comes a short yes/no question to the viewer, asked straight with no set-up ("Would you take her back?"). Songs skip the question. 40–50 s keeps a video near $8–11.

**Song videos.** Every lyric is sung. The one call to action is spoken over the instrumental at the end, with the music still playing under it. Each clip is timed to its lyric window.

**Stills.** Check the cast in every still: no extra people, and nobody in a scene the script doesn't put them in. Redraw a wrong one ("Change this") before writing clip prompts; it's cheaper than a bad take.

**Clip prompts.**
- The camera is always moving. "Locked-off framing" gave stiff videos. Reed: "more movements, camera pans, or commotion", as general advice, not strict rules.
- Write feelings as face and body only. A feeling written as words ("he can't believe it worked") gets spoken aloud, even in a clip with no lines.
- Avoid "reply" and "answer" in actions: the check reads them as dialogue and blocks the take.
- Strong emotion words give glowing or red eyes. Add a Keep line: "normal human eyes, never glowing".
- One image per shot. "Two quick cuts" inside one second can come out as a split screen.
- At most one payoff per montage clip. Stacking unrelated ones (a helicopter, then a car) reads choppy.
- A line that runs across a cut loses lip-sync; keep each line on a shot where the speaker is talking.
- Look-alike refusals are free. Change the scene or the look (e.g. glasses and a bun), then retry.

**Screens.**
- Screens are always on a phone in the scene, never full-frame.
- A real screen is copied exactly, so each beat that names a figure needs its own copy with that figure. Otherwise every phone shows the same number (Reed caught this twice).
- The chart moves with the story: a dip at "don't sell", then the climb.

**Takes and the cut.** Review every take: transcript against the script, plus a frame strip (the take checklist is in `take-review-checklist.md`). The model adds unscripted lines: mute them in the cut, or redo the clip. Redo only the clip that failed. Rewatch the whole cut start to finish before sending.

**What Reed always asks for.** Visual interest ("something happening"), not more cuts. Continuity that makes sense: the order of events, and a flash for time jumps. He rewatches as director and notices boring or wrong parts. He gives creative notes as guidance, not hard rules.

**What Reed rejects.** Static, talky clips. A brand screen that doesn't match the script. Full-screen UI inserts. The same payoff over and over. Errors such as a character not speaking their line on camera, or the wrong UI.

**Cost habits.**
- Most spend is video takes (about 1 credit a second), with redos adding 30–50%.
- Before paying, check the prompts and stills.
- Fix for free in the cut when you can (mutes, timing).
- Keep each video under its estimate; ask before going over $15 for one video or past the cap.
- Log every paid call.

## 2. Per brand: fomo

- **Tone notes** are on the brand (copied to the test site 30 Oct). The core of them:
  - fomo is the thing that saves people, and is shown only winning.
  - Losses happen on plain unbranded apps, or on a screen we don't see.
  - Sell it in the flow of talk ("on FOMO you just copy the top traders"), never as an ad.
- **Say it right:** FOMO is "FOH-moh". Write it in capitals in every spoken line. Lower case gave "Fobo", "Fogo" and "Foho".
- **Real UI only:**
  - the real screens, with any numbers or coins;
  - custom coin pages with a big fomo header, the eyes logo, and the character's face as the coin icon;
  - any loading screen is dark, with only the fomo eyes and logo.
  - Every real screen is attached as itself (UI, logo and layout exact, since #1041), and each beat's figure is drawn onto its own copy (#1059).
- **Payoff:** a big balance on a rich Home screen, a different balance each video.
- **Chart dip:** fine when the story needs it (e.g. "Don't sell. Diamond hands."), as long as it recovers and fomo ends up winning.
- **End line:** "Want to trade like me? Download FOMO through the link in my bio." It can be adapted to the story, for example to the dad: "You can start by going to my profile and downloading FOMO through the link in my bio."
- **How people find fomo varies:** a friend (Kimchi), scrolling Instagram, watching someone's gains, researching how to win trades.
- **Cast:**
  - Goth GF: antagonist or girlfriend.
  - Mogging BF: boss, rival or robber.
  - Kimchi: the friend who shows the app.
  - Heroes: Wyatt, Carl, Clavicular, Jalen.
  - Song videos have male leads.
- **Look:** Pixar-style 3D, 480p. Captions and music are added in post by the team.
