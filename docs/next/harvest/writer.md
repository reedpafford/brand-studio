# Writer harvest: writing scripts for Reed

Drafted by the Writer session on 1 Nov 2026; committed by Next-Lead because the Writer's role doesn't open PRs. Text unchanged.

## 1. General craft (any brand)

**Pitching**
- Ask only what the brief and brand kit don't answer: brand, length, cast names.
- Pitch 2–3 hooks, one or two lines each, all clearly different (caught in the act, a shocking number, a public roast). Reed often says "run all three as their own scripts", so keep the middle shareable.
- Pick sensible defaults and say so in one line instead of asking.

**Hooks**
- The conflict is the first line, spoken at 0 s. Open on an accusation, a mocking line or a gross close-up, never a setup shot.
- Show the problem in close-up (a red chart, gunk on a cotton swab) so it reads with the sound off.

**Dialogue (Reed was explicit)**
- Short lines, 1–8 words, fired back and forth. Keep quick cuts between speakers and a cut to the listener's reaction. Never merge an exchange into one held two-shot.
- A reaction names the feeling and its reason ("embarrassed that everyone saw"), not just the face.
- The payoff line is short and quotable, under 8 words, and mirrors the opening ("Stopped picking. Started copying.").

**Scenes and continuity**
- Each scene is one whole moment in one place, as long as it needs. No fixed timings.
- Never end a scene on its last line with nothing after. Let the reaction land, then end on a beat the next scene picks up: a look, a sound, a dropped object, a door, a time card.
- Mirror the ending to the opening (the same tissue reach, now nothing to wipe). It's Reed's favourite payoff device.
- Montages can hop between places as long as they follow the story: one climb, one dip, celebration only at the payoff.

**Physical comedy**
- Make every fix fail visibly and get worse (it refills, cracks bigger, grows back like frost). Build to a frantic peak, like whack-a-mole or a fast-forward routine.
- POV-of-the-owner openings, marked "frantic, fast, exaggerated", worked well.

**Voiceover scripts**
- The words are locked. Never trim, reword or "improve" them, and check word for word before handing over.
- Split the voiceover into one phrase per line, each with an action line before it showing exactly what's said.
- A dog narrator's mouth never moves.

**Things Reed rejected**
- Changing his script words, even in song lyrics.
- Attaching a reference to an original script (it turns the script into a remake).
- Over-long or over-specified prompts.
- Calling dialogue cuts the problem when the real problem was abrupt scene endings.

**Things Reed approved**
- A CTA adapted to each story, as long as it names the brand and where to find it.
- A recurring visual villain and hero inside the body (gremlins, and workers in branded uniforms).

## 2. Per brand

**fomo**
- Voice: a teen underdog proves a doubter wrong. The money climbs to six figures. The doubter ends on a "WHAT?!" scream.
- Screens: Home, Home (rich), Token chart up, Token chart down, Desktop trading. Trades that aren't on fomo are on "a plain app with no fomo logo".
- The selling idea that landed: "Stop picking. Copy the people who win." (copying top traders).
- CTAs Reed approved: "Stop picking. Copy the winners on fomo. Link's in my bio." / "Done getting rugpulled? Copy top traders on fomo. Link's in my bio." / "fomo. I just copy the top traders. Link's in my bio." (The saved default is "Want to trade like me? Download fomo through the link in my bio.")
- Never say: guaranteed profit, can't lose.

**Iron Paws**
- Voice: the dog tells the story in first person over the pictures. Warm, a little funny, then tender at the recovery.
- Refrain: "That is my gut breaking down." Closing: "You just did not know what the signs meant. But now you do. The link is below. Your pup has been waiting on you."
- Claims to use only as briefed: human grade, vet formulated, 3000+ five-star Amazon reviews, 10,000+ owners last month. Ingredients: probiotics, chlorella, chicory root.
- The bag shows label to camera at the reveal, the scoop, the feeding and the final shot. Use the flavour each video picks (so far, Peanut butter).
- The breed changes per video (pitbull, French bulldog). Keep fur colour and body details in the action lines consistent with the breed, and avoid words like "golden" that pull the picture away from the sheet.
