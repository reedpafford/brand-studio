# Song-Producer: what I learned with Reed (song and narrated ads)

From four Iron Paws ads made by hand on 28–29 Oct: two song ads and two narrated ads. Reed called them "amazing". The details are in `docs/next/song-ad-workflow.md` and `docs/next/song-pilot-notes.md`.

## 1. General craft (any brand)

**Creative plan (what Reed liked most)**
- Read the script line by line and give every line its own vivid, *exaggerated* picture, so the story reads with the sound off. Every action the script mentions is shown.
- Play symptoms big and funny: a crust-glued eye, a tongue blur, a fur blizzard. Show a hidden cause visually: a translucent glowing x-ray of the character with hot spots, or a trip inside the body. Payoffs are quiet and warm.
- Structure that works for problem/solution ads:
  1. the symptom hook in the first seconds
  2. the daily routine
  3. a cause made visible
  4. the find, often on a phone
  5. the product in use
  6. "weeks later" with the reveal
  7. the apology or hug
  8. proof
  9. the end line with the product pushed to camera
- The same cast in every scene, and the same owner across a brand's ads when it fits.

**Song ads**
- The song is the clock. Make the song first, measure the real take, then plan shots to the measured lines. Never guess timing.
- The hook comes in the first 5 seconds, a symptom list works well as a chant. Make it a real song, not a jingle. Lines of 6–9 words, each a picture.
- If the editor brings a voice-over script, turn it into lyrics beat for beat. Keep its story, key lines and claims word for word, and only add the brand name to the last line. Once picked, the lyrics are locked.
- Style line: describe the sound, never name an artist (Suno rejects names). Reed's taste for an older audience: "laid-back, breezy pop", warm female vocal, about 100 BPM, strummed acoustic guitar. He turned down pop-rap.
- Add "vocals start immediately, no instrumental intro, no instrumental breaks, continuous singing, tight ending". The last sung line names the brand and where to find it.
- Suno takes vary by up to 20 s for the same words. Pick the tighter take: it's cheaper to film.
- About 365 sung words gives about 2:40. Show the expected length before the lyrics lock.
- B-roll only by default, with lip-sync only where asked. Nobody sings on screen.

**Narrated ads**
- One narrator track, recorded once and laid over silent clips. Clips that voice themselves drift between voices.
- Reed wanted a *realistic* voice: ElevenLabs "Sarah" won over the MiniMax voices.
- Time each scene to the sentences it shows.
- The clips' own sound effects (licking, swipes, the scoop) go under the narration for free.
- Music: a dark, mysterious "investigative" bed that ducks under the voice and turns hopeful in the last third. Reed asked for this ("conspiracy background music, like the reference").
- Dead space: Reed wanted it cut, then "loosen up a little" after words got clipped.
  - Trim only real silence, shortening pauses to about 0.25 s.
  - Never cut by the transcript's guess of where a word ends, which clipped "pit bull" and "the ears" and dropped a soft phrase.
  - Re-transcribe after trimming and compare letter by letter.

**Making it**
- Approve the looks before stills. Check stills and clips against the character sheets and product.
- Refusals ("looks like someone else's character") and "service busy" errors worked on a plain retry, and refusals are free.
- Long clips with people (over about 16 s) drift faces, so split them.
- Two versions of one ad (different dog) can reuse clips that show no person or animal (product shots, ingredient close-ups).

**Cost habits**
- Quote the price before every paid step and ask for a budget per video. Reed sets caps ($60 for the pair, $40 for the pair) and approves inside them.
- About $0.13 per second of finished video at 480p. The clips are about 88% of it, the songs and voices are cents. Log every paid call.

## 2. Per brand

**Iron Paws** (dog gut-health greens powder)
- **Product:** the **peanut butter** bag, shown exactly, at four moments: the find (on her phone), the feeding, the proof, and the end, with the dog nudging it to camera.
- **Claims in Reed's scripts** (use as written): human grade, vet formulated, probiotics, chlorella, spirulina, chicory root, burdock root, "plus 14 more ingredients", 3,000+ five-star Amazon reviews, 10,000+ owners last month.
- **Recurring cast that works:**
  - a fawn Frenchie with a red bandana and big glossy worried eyes
  - a blue-grey pit bull with a white chest blaze and a brown leather collar
  - the owner: a mid-30s woman, chestnut messy bun, cream knit sweater, light jeans, gold hoops
- **Story pattern:** the dog's symptoms (eyes, paws, skin, ears, shedding) are "the gut breaking down". The owner tries sprays and shampoos, then finds the gut fix. "We were good." The end line: "You just didn't know what the signs meant. But now you do. The link is below. Your pup has been waiting on you."
- **References that work:** PetLab Co. ads, both dog-point-of-view songs and the x-ray narrated style.
