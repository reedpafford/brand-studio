# Song-ad pilot notes (hand-run, for the Next team)

Reed's two Iron Paws song ads, made by hand while the song flow isn't built. What was awkward, what worked, and what Starpop does better. The newest entries are at the bottom.

## 2026-10-28/29: brief and song
- **The input was a voice-over script, not lyrics.** Reed brought two full narration scripts (about 580 words each) plus a reference ad each. The references turned out to be *songs* (PetLab Co., dog-POV pop-rap, 1:46 and 2:08). The real job was "turn my script into lyrics in the reference's shape". The song-directions prompt only covers "write fresh lyrics" or "the editor's lyrics are the song". **Next:** add a third case: a script or VO, adapted line by line into lyrics, keeping its beats, claims and key lines.
- **Listening to the reference is what made the directions good.** The ads were fetched free from the Meta Ad Library with Playwright (the ad's own card, not the popup), then transcribed locally with Whisper (word timestamps), with the BPM and voice pitch measured by librosa. Frame contact sheets showed the visual grammar: literal B-roll per line, exaggerated symptom close-ups, a trip inside the gut, the phone order, the box arriving, the hug, proof cards, the bag to camera. **Next:** the reel import should do the same for song references (transcript + tempo + sections), and pass it to song directions.
- **Reed changed the genre after the first draft** ("older audience → laid-back pop"). The genre map said pet owners → folk or indie pop, and Reed wanted pop in the feel of a famous song. Suno rejects artist names, so the style line has to *describe* that song. **Next:** let the editor give a "sounds like" reference and turn it into a style line (no names) before writing lyrics.
- **Reed's choice was really "full story vs 60 s cut".** Presenting it as a table (length, what's in, what's cut, cost) made it a one-line answer. **Next:** show the directions as a side-by-side with these columns.
- **Length surprise.** Locked lyrics of about 365 sung words means a song of about 2:15–2:30, longer than the references. Word count should be shown with an estimated length *before* the editor locks.
- **Suno via KIE.** Reed added a KIE key mid-session. A saved environment key only reaches new sessions, so a helper session made the songs. KIE's Suno V6 custom mode takes `lyrics` (5,000 chars), `style` (1,000), `negative_tags`, `vocal_gender`, an optional `duration` (10–360 s), and returns 2 takes per request. The price shows only when signed in (the public page says "from $0.002 per request").

## 2026-10-29: songs, timing and the app run
- **Suno ran long.** The 365-word lyrics came back at 2:38–2:59 (four takes), longer than the references. Takes differ by up to 20 s in pace with the same words. Picking the tighter take per video saved about 25 s of footage (about $2.50). **Next:** show each take's length and the cost of its video side by side when the editor picks the Preferred take.
- **Measuring is easy and free.** faster-whisper with word timestamps on the song, then a word-level alignment (difflib) against the locked lyrics, gave a start and end for every lyric line, even where Whisper mishears the singing ("iron pause", "Chico re-rooped"). librosa gave the BPM and beats. The singing starts at 0–1 s, as the "vocals start immediately" add-on asks.
- **The app has no song input, so the song timing went in by hand.** I wrote a dialogue-free script (one SCENE per planned clip, action lines, ★ bag lines), let the breakdown run, then re-timed every shot through `PUT /projects/{pid}/breakdown`: each scene was scaled to its piece of the song, and clips over 16 s with people were split in two. **Next:** the song breakdown (`song_breakdown.md`) should take these line times directly; this re-timing step is the core of "the song is the clock".
- **Cast read for a dialogue-free script works:** it found the dog, the owner, the old bulldog and his owner, each with a good description. I set the Frenchie and the owner to the same description in both videos, so the two videos match. **Next:** "the same cast as video X" for a pair of videos; looks made for a video can't be shared (rule 12), so they were drawn twice.
- **False "Check the cast" alarms on inserts** (a vacuum-canister close-up, the old dog's sparkle close-up) needed "Keep anyway".
- **Off-model stills:** 2 of 37 had the owner wrong (red hair, hair down). "Change this…" with a note fixed both first time (4 credits each quoted).
- **Clip-prompt flags were wording only:** "answer" reads as a spoken line; one audio line with an extra phrase failed the "same audio line" check; Advice on a bed + licking clip. These were fixed by hand-editing the prompt (the free fix only worked on one).
- **Network:** the site dropped for about a minute mid-run (connection resets). The jobs carried on server-side, and polling with retries picked them back up.
- **Costs before takes:** about 25 credits per video (cast read, breakdown, 2–4 looks, frame prompts, 17–20 stills, clip prompts, 2 still redraws).

## 2026-10-29: takes and the final cut (both videos delivered)
- **37 clips, 35 first time.** One was refused by Seedance as "too much like someone else's character" (video 1's end shot: the Frenchie nudging the bag to camera, nothing obviously borrowed). It went through unchanged on the retry. One failed as "service busy" and also worked on the retry. Neither was charged.
- **Off-model drift is rare and small:** the Frenchie's bat ears folded down like a pug's in an "embarrassed" moment (video 2's opener). A "Keep" line saying "upright bat ears, never folded" fixed half of it; the model still drops the ears for that emotion. The faces of the owner and the dogs held across all 37 clips, the longest 18 s. The bag read right in every shot.
- **The final cut is simple once the clips are timed:** each take is trimmed to its piece of the song from 0 s (every take was 0.1–1 s longer than needed, so nothing was slowed), the clips are joined, the take sound is dropped, the song goes on, and there's a 1.5 s fade. It runs in about a minute locally with ffmpeg. The app's own "Make video" keeps the take sound, so **Next** needs a "song as the only sound" option there.
- **Total cost:** 2 songs $0.12 + video 1 $20.37 (2:45) + video 2 $18.98 (2:28) = **$39.47**, about $0.13 per second of finished video. The breakdown, stills and prompts were about 12% of it; the takes were the rest.
- **What Starpop does better (for the Next team):** the song step is in-app with 2 takes and a Preferred mark, and its storyboard is timed to the track automatically. We did both by hand (KIE in a helper session; the line times put into the breakdown via the API). **What we do better:** looks approved before stills, reviewable clip prompts, exact product shots, and timing to the measured line times (not guesses).
- **Network:** the live site dropped connections about 4 times in 90 minutes, each for under a minute. Retries on GET and sign-in, plus downloads saved under a temporary name until complete, were enough.
