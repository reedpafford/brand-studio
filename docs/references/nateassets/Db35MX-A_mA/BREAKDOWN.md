## Why it works

- Injustice in the first second: a smug one-word 'Yep.' and '200 bucks' make the viewer take the son's side before any setup; the hook title 'DAD SAID I'D FAIL' promises the payback.
- Comic punctuation through camera: snap zooms and a whip-pan to the empty TV wall turn each line into a beat; reaction ECUs are cartoon-big (saucer eyes, giant jaw).
- The flash-insert burst (15 shots in 2.7 s) under 'That cost me 2,000' makes the loss concrete and the pace frantic without new dialogue.
- The eviction plays as one 10.6 s continuous take with an arc and whip-pan, so the fight feels real and the long shot contrasts with the hyper-cut hook.
- A quotable vow straight into the lens ('When this works, don't say you believed in me') and a lonely rain walk set up the arc in classic underdog grammar.
- Grind montage makes the method feel achievable (laptop, list one item, 'Is this still available?', ship, first sale) - perfect for a reseller audience and the 'Comment AI' CTA.
- Cross-cutting Dad watching through the window lets the antagonist witness the rise before the confrontation, so the apology lands.
- Payoff milks the win ('Say it louder') then flips to a money joke ($1,800) and tops it with '*10,000 ORDERS*' - three escalating buttons in 8 s.
- Cut to black then a 0.1 s-per-shot real-life flex montage ties the cartoon fable to the creator's actual life and loops back to the start.
