## Why it works

- The hook is a named doubter: the title card 'MOM SAID I'D FAIL' plus Mom bursting through the door makes the whole reel a 'prove her wrong' promise in the first second.
- The opening joke-answer ('How many have you sold?' - 'Two.') lands the stakes and the humiliation in one word, then a snap zoom into Mom's glare doubles it.
- Big, readable face acting (saucer eyes, gritted teeth, slow jaw drops) carries each beat on mute; captions are one word at a time so the eye stays on faces.
- The grind montage teaches the actual business (photo, list, sell, pack, print label) in 8 s with no words - it doubles as content for the reselling audience the CTA targets.
- Time passes through light, not titles: day/night flicker on locked-off frames and fast-motion packing with Mom at normal speed show weeks in seconds.
- The breakthrough is a single visual gag - sale prices reflected in his eyes - followed by a full-body jump; the payoff is physical, not explained.
- Mom's arc flips from opponent to partner ('You're doing it wrong' while packing next to him) - a warmer payoff than revenge, and very shareable with parents.
- The button is a comic reversal (closet of new boxes, 'You bought more?' - 'maybe') ending on a horror ECU, so the reel ends on a laugh, not a lecture.
- The live-action tag links the cartoon to a real lifestyle ('based on a true story' caption) and the fast cuts invite a loop/rewatch.
