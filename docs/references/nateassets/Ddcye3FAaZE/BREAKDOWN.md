## Why it works

- Hook packs title-card premise ('STEP MOM SAID I'D FAIL'), physical comedy (a bag of AirPods avalanching onto a bed) and a shouted money line into the first second - the viewer knows the stakes before the first cut.
- A clear ticking clock ('Three days') turns a hustle story into a deadline story; every later beat is measured against it.
- The fail is staged as rising hope then collapse (jump cut to 'YES!' -> 'Zero.' -> eviction), so the low point lands harder.
- The best-friend foil gives the hero someone to talk to: all exposition is quick back-and-forth two-word lines (4.7 words/s), never V.O. narration.
- Slapstick acting at the low points (falling off the curb, throwing AirPods, flopping on the bed) keeps the tone funny instead of sad.
- The pivot carries the real lesson for the audience ('Everybody sells these. They're saturated.' / 'Something people actually want'), which is what the 'Comment SHEET' CTA sells.
- Transitions are objects thrown into the lens (AirPods case, trash bag, red shirt, falling boxes), motion-blur whips and a mouth zoom - cuts feel kinetic and on-theme and hide AI-clip joins.
- Payoff is a mirror of the opening: the woman who doubted (stepmom look-alike) now asks him for the products, and his answer is the funnel line 'That's in my bio.'
- A 1 s '*actual vids*' live-action insert mid-story and the real-life tag at the end blur fiction and reality ('True Story btw'), selling the creator as the character.
- Word-by-word yellow captions make it watchable on mute; dialogue covers most of the runtime so there is no slack.
