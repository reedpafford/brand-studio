## Why it works

- The hook is a prop and a number: an empty wallet waved in the doorway and '$2,000' in the first 3 s - stakes are concrete and instantly readable.
- Escalation by one word: 'Actually, three.' cut to mom's clenched-teeth close-up is the reel's biggest laugh and makes Nate a cheeky, likeable underdog.
- A clean contract - 'One week. Make your $2,000 back.' 'Every dollar.' - sealed in a nose-to-nose profile ECU, so the viewer knows exactly what success looks like.
- Wordless locked-off montages (same frame, jump cuts, speed-ramp ghosting, day/night light flips) show time and effort with zero dialogue and are cheap to generate as one set-up.
- The emotional low is quiet: night, laptop glow, mom softening ('You don't have to prove anything to me') - the warm doorway light behind him is the visual for her support.
- The turn is a visual gag, not a line: notification glow in his pupils and a star-jump 'Yeah!' - big cartoon acting at the peak.
- Mom moves from antagonist to teammate (packing beside him, 'You're doing it wrong'), a more wholesome arc than revenge, widening the audience.
- The button is a callback reversal: he already bought more stock, snap zoom on her horrified face - ends on a laugh, not a lecture.
- The CG-to-real morph in the same room plus 'respect.' and 'Based on a true story' sell the creator as the character and feed the 'Comment SHEET' CTA.
