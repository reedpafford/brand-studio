## Why it works

- Instant stakes: a number ($2,000), a villain-ish parent with the wallet and a whip-pan across the stock - viewer knows who, what and why in 4.5 s.
- Rapid-fire bickering (lines of 1-3 words, 0.2-0.5 s apart) played on mostly static two-shots; the speed of the cutting matches the speed of the talk.
- Cartoon-big acting on the key beats only: Mom's ECU with bulging eyes, the chair flail, the screaming 'I am!' - between them the faces stay grounded.
- The night montage sells effort without dialogue: one locked-off frame, a clock caption ticking, light flipping warm/blue, then stutter jump cuts as fatigue sets in.
- A quiet low point ('You don't have to prove anything to me') gives the turn somewhere to come from, and the crash zoom into the eye makes '3 orders' feel huge.
- The antagonist becomes the partner: Mom grabs the shirt ('You're too slow'), then the match-cut team montage shows them packing in the same pose across days and places.
- Final line flips the opening blame into shared credit ('You did.' 'No. We did.'), an emotional button stronger than a revenge ending.
- The real-life tag puts the creator and, likely, his real mother in the same 'shocked' pose, linking the cartoon to real life and to the 'Check my bio' CTA.

## Method

Machine cut list (frame-difference spikes) checked by eye with frame strips at 1/24 s around every run of short shots and at 0.08-0.25 s across long shots, plus a per-frame difference listing to tell cuts (single spike) from camera moves (smooth run). Changes (74 auto shots -> 64): false cuts merged for whip-blur frames (4.23), screen-content changes on the laptop (15.33-15.75), morphs (14.38), handheld shake (54.58-54.80, 55.43), fast motion (8.10, 8.43, 41.38); cuts re-timed at 13.83 and 40.95 s (zoom/whip transitions). Audio: faster-whisper small; silencedetect for dead air.
