## The product in the story
Enters at about 30%, brought by the one witness who didn't laugh: put in the hero's hand, label readable, with one line on what it does, in the person's claims. The hero uses it on camera (an app: on the phone in the hero's hand). Named twice: at the hand-over, and by the hero at the end.

## Structure
0-8%: Mid-task, in front of people who matter, the hero fails through the exact problem the product solves; one person laughs and says a line that will come back. 8-30%: The cost: what is lost, and a date to try again. Alone, the old way fails once more, smaller and sadder. 30-45%: The witness blames the real cause, never the hero, and hands the product over; the hero doubts it once. 45-70%: First use, shown plainly, one small visible win; the laugher raises the bar for the retry. 70-92%: The same room, the same faces. Hold on the moment it works, then on the room. 92-100%: The laugher asks what changed; the hero, product in hand, gives the opening line back, kindly. Their call to action, if any, is the hero's last line, naming the brand; else end on the laugher's face. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
All adults. Hero: the buyers' age and sex, capable, proud, quiet when hurt. Laugher: a peer with standing, quick, smug. Witness: older, plain-spoken, has been there. The one who decides: fair, brisk.

## Tone, sound and look
Short lines that answer each other, most under eight words; silence after the failure and before the retry. The room's own sound: chairs, a cough, the thing failing, then working. Flat cool light for the failure, low warm sun in the same room for the return. One long hold when it works.

## Details you invent
The task, the room, the stakes, the laugher's line, the witness's reason: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, not the first stock choice (an office pitch, a talent show).
