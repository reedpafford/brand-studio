## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
Disney Pixar style, Narrated format: the hero's own diary voice over the scenes, almost without a gap; nobody speaks on camera. About 60-90 seconds in four short chapters. 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
There from about 10%: it arrives, the hero unpacks it, label readable, and it is in the hero's hand or in use in every chapter after (an app: on the phone in the hero's hand). Named on arrival and in the last line. Results and timings are only those the person's words give; where they give none, the weeks show the habit, the feeling and other people's reactions, never a measured result.

## Structure
0-10%: The problem, shown big and funny on the body or the day, and a first line that admits it and the doubt: they gave it a month and expected little. 10-28%: Week one. It arrives; a first use; honestly, nothing. A squint at the mirror, a sceptic's raised eyebrow. 28-48%: Week two. One small surprise, noticed in the middle of something else and nearly missed. 48-66%: Week three. A wobble: a bad day or a skipped one, and the choice to carry on. 66-88%: Week four. The change, shown on the same body in the same mirror and room as week one; the sceptic notices before the hero says a word. 88-100%: The hero looks into the lens, product in hand, and gives the verdict in one line. Their call to action, if any, is the last line, naming the brand. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
Hero: the buyers' age and sex, hopeful, a little embarrassed to be hoping. The sceptic at home: a partner, sibling or flatmate. One person outside the house who notices late on.

## Tone, sound and look
First person, plain and funny about itself, the voice covering nearly all of it; only the scenes' own small sounds under it. Each week opens on the same spot so the change is easy to see; time passes inside the picture (the light, a wall calendar), never on a card.

## Details you invent
The spot each week returns to, the small surprise, the wobble, who notices: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, never the first stock choice.
