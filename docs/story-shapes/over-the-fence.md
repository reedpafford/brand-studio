## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
Disney Pixar style, Dialogue format: a 3D cartoon rivalry, lines spoken on camera. About 60-90 seconds, four scenes either side of one boundary. 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
Glimpsed at about 45%: one rival using it in secret, label half seen. Shown in full at about 70% when the other catches them, product in hand, label readable; then passed across and used on camera (an app: the phone snatched, stared at, handed back open). Named twice: in the confession, and when the loser asks for their own.

## Structure
0-7%: Two neighbours (or grown siblings) either side of a fence, mid-boast, trading a compliment that is really an insult about the problem they both have. 7-40%: Three rounds in mirrored pairs: one does something, the other tops it, framed alike so the eye compares. The problem dogs both, and both hide it. 40-55%: Overnight, one side's problem is gone; its owner puts it down to luck. The other doesn't sleep. 55-72%: Spying: a hedge, a ladder, binoculars. The secret is caught in the act and the spy falls into the wrong garden. 72-90%: Nose to nose. The winner could gloat and instead passes it over the fence. The loser uses it and it works. 90-100%: The two lean on the fence, at peace for one line. Their call to action, if any, is that line, naming the brand. The button: a third neighbour appears with something grander and both heads turn as one. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
All adults. The loser, who is the hero: the buyers' age and sex, competitive, sweaty, lovable. The winner: neat, smug, lonelier than they let on. A third neighbour: one silent appearance.

## Tone, sound and look
Polite venom: smiling lines of two to seven words. Sprinklers, shears, a ladder's creak, a crash through a hedge. Bright suburban day; deep blue night for the spying, one warm window.

## Details you invent
What they compete over, the three rounds, the third neighbour's entrance: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, not the first stock choice (lawns, unless the product is for lawns).
