## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
Dr. Pickle style, Presenter format: the blunt cartoon doctor talks without a break from a bottom corner while a skeleton behind him acts out every line. About 60-90 seconds in one unbroken rant. 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
Enters at about 55%: in the skeleton's hand in a warm real room, real packaging, label readable, in the upper two-thirds, clear of the doctor's corner (an app: on a phone in the skeleton's hand). As he says what it does, in the person's claims and his own blunt words, the body changes on screen. Named twice: here and in the sign-off.

## Structure
0-7%: First frame, first word: he calls out the daily habit, straight at the viewer. Behind him the skeleton is caught doing it, the problem glowing. 7-30%: What it is really doing inside: a fast push into the body and two or three cutaways, each clearer and grosser than the last. He enjoys this too much. 30-45%: The usual fixes and why each is useless: a quick run, one shot each. 45-55%: The real cause, in one sentence, on one slow held picture. 55-80%: The product: how it deals with that cause, the bad glow turning good as he speaks. 80-92%: The after, in life: the skeleton in a bright, tidy room doing what it couldn't. 92-100%: The sign-off, rude and fond. Their call to action, if any, is his last line, naming the brand. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
The doctor: the style's own host, always a doctor. The skeleton: one recurring figure with one accessory from the buyers' life.

## Tone, sound and look
A rant with punchlines: short, hard sentences, no pause longer than a breath. He swears, as the style's guide says he does; tone it down only if the person asks. His voice and nothing else. Blue studio backdrop for explaining, warm lived-in rooms for life; orange-red glow for harm, green or gold for good.

## Details you invent
The habit he attacks, his insults, the cutaways, the useless fixes, the skeleton's accessory: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, never the first stock choice. He promises nothing the person didn't.
