## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
UGC style, Presenter format: one ordinary person talks to their phone in their own room, from the first frame to the last, with literal B-roll cut in. About 75-110 seconds, one talker, one room. 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
Late on purpose. At about 15% it is in B-roll in passing, unnamed, part of getting ready. At about 65% it answers the stranger's question: in B-roll, taken from the talker's bag in their own hand at its real size, label readable (an app: their phone turned toward the stranger). At their talking spot they only talk and hold nothing. Named twice, both after the twist.

## Structure
0-6%: To the lens: this happened at the weekend and they are still thinking about it. 6-25%: Where they were going and why it mattered; getting ready; what used to happen there because of the problem. 25-50%: Someone keeps looking at them. B-roll: the stranger's glance, then again, then again. The talker's guesses at why get worse each time. 50-65%: The stranger walks over; the talker braces. 65-80%: The twist: no judgement at all. The stranger has the same problem and wants to know what the talker uses. The talker shows them. 80-92%: Why it stuck: a year ago they would have been the one staring. 92-100%: The longest hold on the talker: one line turned to the viewer. Their call to action, if any, is the last sentence, naming the brand. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
The talker: the buyers' age and sex, a natural storyteller. In B-roll only: the stranger, an adult who reads at a glance, and a friend along for the day. Nobody else speaks.

## Tone, sound and look
A story told to a friend: quicker as the dread builds, one real stop before the twist. The room's own quiet under the voice; never a crowd's noise in B-roll. The talker fills about half the video, still, eyes on the lens. B-roll: one slow, simple action a shot, flat light for the dread, warm for the twist.

## Details you invent
The outing, the old fear, the talker's three guesses, the stranger, the closing line: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, never the first stock choice.
