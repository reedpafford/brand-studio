## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
Claymation style, Narrated format: one voice, the foreman of a tiny clay crew, reads the shift log over the scenes; nobody speaks on camera. About 40-60 seconds, two worlds: a clay person's room, and the hidden place where the product works (a body, a head, a house, a machine). 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
Enters at about 50%. Outside, the clay person's hands pick up the real product in its real packaging, label exact. Inside, it arrives as the delivery the crew has begged for and does the one job the person's words claim, shown as clay mechanics (an app: on the phone in the clay hands; inside, a new control panel). Named once, in the log.

## Structure
0-8%: Push into the worn-out clay person's face and cut inside: alarms, a tiny crew in chaos. The foreman logs the day's number and that it is still on fire. 8-30%: Working conditions: the problem as jobs the crew does by hand with the wrong tools, one log line and one gag each. 30-48%: Outside, the person innocently makes it worse. Inside, the crew watch it coming; the foreman files a complaint nobody reads. 48-68%: The delivery. Hats off, silence, then it goes to work. 68-90%: New conditions, the warmest and sunniest stretch: a crew with nothing to do; outside, the same room in gold light and the person at ease. 90-100%: The foreman signs off: nothing to report. Their call to action, if any, is the last entry, naming the brand. The button: one light blinks, a worker taps it, and it is only the kettle. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
The person outside: the buyers' age and sex, finely sculpted, never speaks. The foreman: a small goofy clay worker with a clipboard, a weary professional. Two crew: one who panics, one who naps.

## Tone, sound and look
Log entries: clipped, present tense, deadpan about disaster. Clay thumps, a tiny alarm, then a kettle. Low and cool for the problem, golden window light for the fix.

## Details you invent
The hidden place, the crew's hopeless jobs and tools, the day count, the false alarm: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, never the first stock choice.
