## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
Skeleton style, Narrated format, in the skeleton ad's own shape: a curiosity question, five to seven rising rungs, a payoff. One narrator speaks to the viewer as you, over one recurring cartoon skeleton; nobody speaks on camera. About 35-55 seconds, roughly 110-160 words. 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
Diagnose it first with the style guide's four angles, and say in a line which and why. By default it enters at about 45%, on one clean turn between a ladder going down and the climb back; if its angle is daily use, it is in the first rung instead. In the skeleton's hand, real packaging, label readable (an app: on a phone in its bony hand). Named at the turn and in the end line.

## Structure
0-8%: The question, on the first frame, over the problem already showing in the body: a glow, a swelling. The face can wait. 8-45%: Rungs one to three, counted one way only (days, amounts or stages): one line and one picture each, each more physical than the last. Dive into the part a line names. 45-55%: The turn, in one line: the product. 55-88%: Rungs four to six, the climb, each bigger, gold where it is working. Only what the person's words claim, on their timings; where they give none, count stages rather than promise days. 88-100%: The payoff answers the opening question: a triumph for a story of using it, a catastrophe for one of never fixing it. Their call to action, if any, is the end line, naming the brand. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
The skeleton: the style's glass-body figure unless the product points to another lane, with one accessory from the buyers' life, the same in every shot. One or two onlookers at most, for the payoff.

## Tone, sound and look
Present tense, short punchy sentences, one concrete physical picture a line, no sales words. The voice runs wall to wall; almost no effects. A slow creep in on most shots, a dive when a body part or object is named.

## Details you invent
The question, the ladder's unit, each rung's picture, the accessory, the payoff image: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, never the first stock choice.
