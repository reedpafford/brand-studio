## The person's words
Their words and attachments override this card; the rest follows it. A pasted script stays word for word: the card then guides only staging, pacing and where the product lands. Only their own claims, offer, tagline and call to action; invent none. No product named: say so and ask, never invent a real brand.

## Settings
Disney Pixar style, Narrated format: the hero's own voice reads a letter over the scenes, almost without a gap; nobody speaks on camera. About 50-70 seconds, the same two or three rooms then and now. 9:16, no captions.

## The product in the story
Enters at about 50% as the one thing the letter would hand over: in the present-day hero's hand, label readable, then in use in the very room where the past scenes went wrong (an app: on the phone in the hero's hand). Named once at the turn, and once more only if their call to action names it.

## Structure
0-8%: The earlier self, mid-disaster through the problem. The voice greets them by date and tells them to put that down. 8-45%: Three pieces of advice, a line or two each, each over the earlier self confidently doing the opposite, each a bigger physical gag. One has nothing to do with the product (a haircut, a message not to send): it makes the letter feel real. 45-65%: The turn: but mostly, this. The product, and what it does in the person's claims; the grey room turns warm around it. 65-88%: The same moments again, now: each put right in one picture. The unrelated advice is still ignored; the haircut happens anyway. 88-100%: The hero now, looking into the lens, signs off. Their call to action, if any, is the letter's P.S., naming the brand; else the sign-off ends it. These shares hold at any length.

## Cast
The hero, then and now: the buyers' age and sex, the same face and colours, worn out then, easy now. Someone who lives through both versions: a partner, a flatmate or a dog. Nobody else is needed.

## Tone, sound and look
Fond and teasing, never scolding; short sentences that run on, the voice covering nearly all of it. Under it only the scenes' own small sounds. The same rooms in two lights: cool and grey then, golden now. One picture a sentence; the lens met only at the sign-off.

## Details you invent
The date, the three mistakes, the unrelated advice, the sign-off: each made from the product, who buys it, where it is used and what goes wrong without it, never the first stock choice.
