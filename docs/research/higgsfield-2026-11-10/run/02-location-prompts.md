# Higgsfield run, step 1b: place pictures (seen 10 Nov)
3 items, Seedream 5.5, 16:9, 2.5 credits each: the parking lot, the store aisle, the kitchen. Each prompt is about 4,700 characters.
Each is "A two-panel location reference sheet of one and the same <place>: <which part, time of day, weather>."

## Shape of the prompt (in order)
1. The sheet: one 16:9 image, two panels side by side split by a thin black line, "two film frames of one place with identical materials, furniture, light, colour and scale; only the camera moves". Left = the master view. Right = the reverse angle (the camera turned around). "The two backgrounds never match."
2. A fixed camera-and-film paragraph (same in every place): a named film camera, anamorphic lenses and 35mm film stock (one for day, one for night), fine grain, soft highlight roll-off; level camera, no lens wider than 28mm indoors; camera height by posture (about 160 cm standing, 110 seated, 90 low); nearest object at least three metres from the lens; each panel's lower edge is open floor.
3. A fixed materials paragraph: surfaces "photographed for real: true texture, true colour, the true state they are kept in; mostly matte and satin".
4. A fixed forbidden list: cartoon/CG/plastic looks, mirrored panels, lens flares and streaks, blown highlights, hazy grey, letterbox bars, text, logos, watermarks.
5. The place itself, measured and laid out: size in metres ("about 60 metres wide and 45 deep"), what stands at the far end, the left side, the right side and the near end behind the camera, the floor, and one named "anchor" (the lamp pole in its island). "No people, figures, silhouettes or animals anywhere".
6. The light: time of day, the key light and where it falls, the brightest area ("bright but not blown out") and the darkest place.
7. Each panel's camera: lens (35mm), height, where the camera stands, what recedes, and where things sit in the frame (on the lower third line, on the right-hand third).

## Why it matters
The place is drawn once from both directions, so a conversation's two sides (shot and reverse shot) share one geography, one light and one anchor object. Story details are planted in the place picture (one lamp head dark, as the script says "a light that's half out").
