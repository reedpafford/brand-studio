# Current rules

Every agent reads **Current rules** first, before each task. A newer decision always overrides an older one on the same topic. When a decision changes a rule, the Producer updates that line here and marks the old History line as superseded, with a pointer to what replaced it. If two rules seem to conflict and it's unclear which is newer, ask Reed rather than guess.

These entries are extracted without rewriting. Their existing dates and pending-transition language are preserved; this extraction does not reapprove older V5 instructions over newer V6 decisions. Read the applicable newer user approval before acting when they differ.

## Current rules (confirmed by Reed, 2026-10-27)
**Precedence**
1. A newer decision overrides an older one on the same topic; if it's unclear, ask Reed.

**Costs**
2. 1 credit = $0.1028 = 1 second of 480p video. Videos stay at 480p by default; other models are priced in credits at their real Replicate cost (Reed, 2026-10-28).
3. The per-video cap is $75 (729 credits). The project budget is twice the 480p estimate; going over needs Reed.
4. Editors see credits and % of budget, never dollars. Editors may see Reed's name (Reed, 2026-10-28). Past an allowance they're declined with the reason, never "Ask Reed". *Changing (Reed, 2026-10-30), being built:* an editor's over-budget press (Quick clips past the weekly allowance, or a video press past its budget) is **held for the owner**, not declined: nothing is charged while it waits, the editor sees it's waiting for approval with a way to withdraw (never "ask Reed"), and the owner approves (the job runs) or declines in Approvals. The per-video cap stays a hard refusal for everyone. Editors may edit brands and Cast & places characters (Reed, 2026-10-30).
5. Editor allowances: Quick clips 150 credits a week; own scripts about 145 credits ($15) a week. *Changing (Reed, 2026-10-30), being built:* one allowance of $10 (about 97 credits) per editor a week, no rollover, for **Quick clips only**. Anything in a script or video belongs to a brand and is work, so it isn't counted: one check on the job's kind, with no "side video" category (Producer, on Reed's "least potentially error'd path"). Past it, the press is held for the owner's approval (rule 4), never "ask Reed". An editor's paid media presses at the script level with no video (song takes, narration, music; not writing) are held for the owner (rule 4); the owner is never held. No per-script budget for now; revisit with the director's budgets (Producer, 2026-10-30, after QA found these presses had no server limit). A Quick clip belongs to the editor who made it: History shows each editor their own, and the owner sees everyone's (Mine / Everyone's). Brandless Quick clips wait until after 1 Nov. *Changing (Reed, 2026-11-02), being built:* Freestyle replaces Quick clip, and every editor send in it is held for Reed before any spend (rule 33).
6. No paid call (Replicate, Claude) without Reed's approval. Test spend is capped at $15 a week; every paid call is logged in ops/SPEND.md.

**Faces and cast**
7. Every named or speaking role in the script (locked or not) is a role at Assets. Unlocked roles get a per-video look made automatically, which the editor approves; it's never saved to Cast & places. Background people are drawn as part of the scene.
8. Nothing reaches Storyboard or Clips without an approved sheet for every character in it, with a one-click way to Assets.
9. One person is never two roles: the cast read merges duplicates, "Merge into…" has Undo, and "These may be the same person" is flagged (with "They're different" for one video on Assets).
10. The character sheets are the authority on faces: attached in code (never by the AI), the main character's sheet first, the sheets set each face and, unless the video sets its own outfit for that character (locks and outfits, #1104), the outfit; they override the first frame where they differ, no generic face words; storyboard stills use the sheet as their main reference. Looks fit the role's age, gender and job.
11. No automatic face checks, on stills or on takes. If a face drifts, the editor uses "Change this" or "Make another take". *Changing (Reed, 2026-10-29):* once it ships from Next, automatic checks may flag face, outfit and product problems as Advice, each with a ready-written "Change this…" note; they never block and never redo anything on their own. Until then this line holds on live.
12. Only characters made in Cast & places go into the library. Looks made for a video or script stay with it and can't be saved to Cast & places (no "Save to Cast & places" button); Reed remakes a look there himself if he likes it.

**Screens and products**
13. App screens are on a phone in the character's hand, in the scene, never a flat full-frame screenshot. "Use the real image" cut-ins are off unless an editor turns them on, and cleared when their shot is replaced.
14. On an app screen only the fomo logo or the word "fomo" must be right. Numbers, coins and charts can be anything; nothing blocks or is redone for numbers. Montage and trading beats (any shot showing trading, gains or losses) attach the brand's logo, not the Token chart or Desktop trading screenshots, and ask for an invented trading screen in the brand's style: the logo from the attached picture sharp in the top corner, the chart and numbers the story beat needs, each screen beat bigger than the last and visibly climbing when they're winning (a climb that doesn't rise is Advice, never a block). Storyboard stills follow the same rule. The real screenshots are kept only for brand moments (the payoff or CTA Home / Home (rich) screen), logo and layout exact, numbers free.
15. A fomo-mention or CTA moment accepts any of the brand's home-type screens; that check is Advice.
16. A product's real bag appears exactly: brand name, main claim and flavour label right; small print may garble.

**Montages**
17. The hero is in every shot. A montage only when the story or reference has one, never a formula (Reed, 1 Nov: "montages are forced"); when there is one, its beats build and the money climbs. Payoffs are invented per video, never the reference's or a recent video's. Fewer, longer shots, each one clear action, told with the sound off; quick cuts only where the reference has them (Reed, 1 Nov: "you don't actually tell the story visually"). A remake copies its reel shot by shot, swapping only brand and cast (Reed: "the 'copy these' videos aren't copied at all"). Gasps and other sounds are fine when the story motivates them; the rule is to think, tell a story and chase engagement, not to ban things (Reed, 1 Nov: "We CAN have gasps, i just want the director to THINK. Be creative how we have. tell a story and chase engagement."). The hero keeps one outfit through a montage unless the script changes it. A montage is one climb with one dip: the offer of a way out leads straight to someone opening the app with the others leaning in, then grind with small wins that each look bigger, one tense moment, then the payoff; celebration only at the payoff (creative guidance, not a hard check). A reaction line names the feeling and its reason, not just the face.

**End line**
18. Live-action parts of a reference are replaced, by default with the brand CTA. When the end line is the CTA, the CTA shot shows the character holding the phone up to camera with clear fomo branding; no end card and no logo overlay. A video ends on a call to action that names the brand and says where to find it (e.g. "link in my bio"); the brand's saved end line is the default wording, not a required match, and may be adapted per video. The end-line check is Advice only and never blocks, approved or not (Reed, 2026-10-28).

**Script and prompts**
19. The script's words never change in Enhance, breakdown, clips or notes. An adapted reference keeps its dialogue word for word, except for the brand swap.
20. Prompting follows the providers' official docs (docs/prompting). For styles, the guidance studied from Starpop (docs/research/starpop-2026-10-28) may be used too; our current look is the House style and stays the default (Reed, 2026-10-28).

**Approvals and blockers**
21. Clip prompts are reviewable before takes are paid for. "Approve all" (ready items only, skipped ones listed with the reason, Undo) is on every per-item approval list. *Changing (Reed, 2026-10-30), not built yet:* a video has four editor steps, Plan → Cast → Make (Stills, Clips) → Final cut. Stills and takes the AI check finds clean approve themselves, and every earlier and AI-flagged take stays visible, pickable, playable and downloadable, with the reason shown. Clip prompts are shown, and can be changed, before their takes are made; *Check prompts before takes* pauses for a per-prompt OK (UX audit §7.2). The Approvals page is hidden until something waits. *Changing (Reed, 2026-11-02), being built:* the tabs become Director, Plan, Cast, Clips, Final cut, with one place per clip instead of Make's Stills and Clips, and Approvals becomes Needs you (rule 33).
22. No problem blocks without a one-click fix on the same screen (a free repair, Advice, or a button). Editing the raw prompt is never the only way out; Clips never dead-ends.
23. People never see raw ids; messages name the character.
24. A plain-English "Change this…" is the main way to edit stills and clips, with the price shown first.

**Team and process**
25. Stabilization first: no new features until the stabilization pass is done.
26. Code stays lean: no redundant or duplicate code, and delete what a change replaces. About 36,000 lines of app code (server/app plus web/src, without tests or generated files) is a guide, not a hard cap: the app can grow when a needed fix or feature needs it, as long as it isn't redundant. The Steward reports the count on every PR.
27. A QA account is kept for live testing; its sign-in lives in the environment, never in chat or code.
28. Only the Producer merges, and no agent touches the live server.
29. A clip holds one scene or one continuous action, as long as the scene needs; it never crosses a place or time change. Longer clips can drift, so past about 13 s consider splitting at a natural beat, but that's a judgement call, not a ceiling: the story comes before face or wardrobe drift (Reed, 1 Nov: "we said no hard rules, just suggestions"). No rigid timings in the creative rules. Dialogue cuts quickly to whoever is talking; a reaction or action moment can go wide. Continuity (Reed, 2026-10-28): a scene never ends abruptly on its last line; the reaction gets room to land, the scene ends on a beat the next one picks up (a look, a sound, a matching action), and the next scene opens continuing from it. Scripts are written the same way, for every brand.
30. Real brand names (cars, sneakers and the like) are fine in scripts and prompts; Seedance doesn't refuse them (Reed).
31. A published script's lines, cast and breakdown are final; only Reed (the owner) may still edit its title, about and notes (Reed, 2026-10-28), and its role words to match an approved look. An editor brings a video in line by updating that video's own look; the script stays as it is. A song ad's lyrics are the script's lines (final once published); the Preferred song take, Sung or B-roll per section, re-timing and new takes are each video's own choices (Producer, 2026-10-28).
32. Text in the footage (Reed, 1 Nov: "I want 3d numbers and numbers/text that is important to the videos in. When i say no text, i mean no captions."): "no text" means no captions, subtitles, titles or overlay text burned into stills or clips; words on screen are added in editing. In-world text that matters to the video (3D numbers, a sign, a label, a screen) is allowed when the script or story calls for it, and its words are stated exactly. The take check still flags garbled or invented text.

**Redesign v4 (Reed, 2026-11-02: "I approve the v4, lets get it built")**
33. The app follows the v5 wireframes (canvas in UX-Auditor's session; Reed, 2026-11-02: "yes they are good"), the build spec for every screen, built in small steps so the test site works between them. v5 keeps the v4 redesign below and makes video director-first (a new video starts from pasted words or a script version, and the director's chat is the video's main view). Every web PR carries each changed screen's wireframe beside a screenshot of the build, and QA fails any visible difference the PR doesn't list and explain. The v4 points:
- Desktop first; phones must still work at 390 px, designed second.
- The look is Linear-style: cool neutral dark and light, one indigo action colour (white text) only for each screen's main press, Inter only, a denser type scale, smaller corners, and statuses as a dot plus words. It replaces Screening room and its amber.
- One desktop rail (Nav A): brand switcher at the top, account at the bottom, no desktop top bar. Make: Scripts, Videos, Freestyle. Library: Cast & places, Media, Brand. Studio (owner): Needs you, In production, Usage, Team, Settings. Phones keep their top bar and tabs.
- Projects leave the rail: "Project ▾" on each list picks, makes, renames or deletes one.
- Scripts is a table: stage tabs with counts, grouped by project, each version's stage on its row, and bulk Move to project or Archive. A published script opens on Overview: its versions and their videos in one table, with the writing steps as tabs. Videos uses the same table, grouped Needs you, Making and Done, each row linked to its script.
- The video page has one row of tabs: Director, Plan, Cast, Clips, Final cut (Director first when there is one). Clips has one place per clip: its still, its takes side by side with each passed-over take's reason, and its prompt. The lock buttons (Cast, Look, Length, Song, End line, Screens) sit at the top of the Director tab.
- Director chat: the director's moves and the person's messages in one thread; it asks before spending.
- Needs you is the owner's one inbox (requests, stuck videos, changes waiting). It replaces Approvals and shows only while something waits.
- Settings holds Looks, Languages, Studio name and Credit limits. Team is people and invites only.
- Freestyle replaces Quick clip: pick a type (Dialogue, Song, Narrated or One clip), a style, a shape and an optional brand, paste a script, and the director builds it in a chat thread. An editor's sends, and each change asked in the thread, are held for Reed before anything is spent; Reed's own run straight away.

34. Prices the director runs to (Reed, 2026-11-02: "as long as short (under 60s) shorts aren't costing like $20 I think A is fine", choosing the true bound over a planned-cost quote): a Dialogue freestyle's "up to" is the most one run can charge under its own limits: the first takes and their checks, up to 2 remakes a clip with their checks, and the director's own limit. The owner's OK covers that whole price, and nothing past it runs without another OK. The director's own limit is 75 credits a run for its turns and its watches of the cut, on every video (the Producer's call). A step that would pass it waits for the owner, whoever started the run, and each OK grants another 75. The per-video cap (729 credits, $75) and the director's $5 real-cost guard stay. The same bound holds for a director run on a normal video: the run price shown before Make it or Make it again is the planned cost, plus 2 remakes a clip with their checks, plus the director's own limit (Producer, telling Reed A covers the video page too). A person's own Redo and take presses keep their own exact prices and need no bound. A version's price may stay its planned cost, labelled "planned", never "up to". Reed's condition: a short under 60 s should typically cost under about $20 (about 194 credits), to be checked in his first real test. *Being built:* the 75-credit limit and the normal video's run price (#1180 and its follow-up).

35. How the director works (Reed, 2026-11-02: "the point was the flow we have used in the chats where it understands WHY and what makes these shorts engaging and has creative leeway"; Song-Producer is the benchmark: "It is an incredible thinker/director/producer"): the director's first move on every run is a treatment in the chat (the hook in the first 2 s, the story in one line, why it holds a viewer, each beat with its purpose and picture, the ending), and the plan, stills, takes and its watch of the cut follow it. Story and engagement are the director's job, and it may restructure shots, order of pictures, pacing, framing and callbacks; the script's spoken lines and on-screen text are never altered, added to or reordered (Reed: "saying to not vary from the scripts"). It reads the script, the treatment and the plan on every turn, reviews stills before takes, never delivers a cut it hasn't watched, and says plainly what doesn't land. Its craft comes from two sources: producer craft (docs/next/skills/producer-craft.md, from the producer chats, mainly Song-Producer's) for story and engagement on every video, with no camera or look rules; and the style's own skill for how it looks and how the camera moves (Reed: "the skill should really just be the visual style, and how the cameras and stuff move"), which wins on anything visual. The format skills (skeleton-ad, song-ad, zack-d) also keep their own script structure and format rules (Reed: "script structure makes sense in certain formats, so lets keep that"). House's skill (docs/next/skills/house.md) is a baseline of camera tools the director may use when they serve the story, never by habit (Reed: "I don't want it to force slow motion or anything... I want it to be pretty creative with a specific baseline"). *Being built:* #1192 and #1193.
