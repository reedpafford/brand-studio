## The team's craft (what Reed liked in the videos made by hand)
<!-- From docs/next/harvest/fomo-producer.md §1 and song-producer.md §1 (#1075): the general craft only, no brand's own notes. Loaded with the look's skill into treatment, plan, director, review_stills and watch_cut. -->

**Why a short holds.** It opens on the conflict, with no setup: the hook is in the first 2 seconds. Every beat has something happening (a shove, a splash, things flying), never people standing and talking. Every line of the script gets its own vivid, exaggerated picture, so the story reads with the sound off, and every action the script mentions is shown. Reed asks for visual interest, "something happening", not more cuts, and for continuity that makes sense: the order of events, and a white flash for a time jump. He rewatches as a director and notices the boring or wrong parts. His notes are guidance, never hard rules.

**What works.** Problems and symptoms played big and funny; a hidden cause made visible (a glowing x-ray, a trip inside the body); payoffs invented for this story, never the same run of car, penthouse and jet; a quiet, warm payoff after a loud problem; one dip before a climb; a short yes/no question to the viewer before the end line, asked straight. A problem-and-fix story often runs: the hook, the daily routine, the cause made visible, the find, the product in use, "weeks later" with the reveal, the hug or apology, the proof, the end line.

**What Reed rejects.** Static, talky clips. A screen that doesn't match the script, or a full-screen UI insert. The same payoff over and over. A character not saying their line on camera. A story that doesn't make sense in order.

**Making it.** The camera is always moving: a locked-off frame looks stiff. Feelings are written as face and body, never as words (the model says them aloud). One picture per shot: two quick cuts in a second come out as a split screen. At most one payoff per montage clip. Check the stills before paying for takes: a wrong still is cheaper to redraw than a bad take. Rewatch the whole cut start to finish before it goes out.
