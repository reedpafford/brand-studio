## sheet_look
Detailed cinematic claymation: a hand-sculpted plasticine character with an appealing idealized build and richly sculpted features: large almond eyes with white clearly visible around a deeply coloured iris, a dark sculpted upper lash line, thick sculpted brows, defined cheekbones and jaw, a neat sculpted nose and softly sculpted lips; matte clay skin showing thumb-worked smoothing and fine tool striations; sculpted hair with deep comb grooves; chunky rounded clay hands with no fingernails. No photographic skin, no real hands, no wrinkled realistic faces. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit of real woven fabric with stitched seams, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance.

## still
- Name the clay on every major object ("a coiled clay towel", "clay plants with thumb-pressed leaves"), not only in the look. A product is sculpted in clay with a real printed paper label, its words stated exactly, so the label stays right and never reads "CLAY".
- Keep the light bright and warm: clay dies in low-key, dusk or long-shadow light. A cold source (a fridge, a phone screen) keeps the skin warm against it: "cold blue light from the fridge while her warm terracotta clay skin keeps its own colour".
- Keep framing fairly tight and inside the world ("camera at eye level, inside the scene"): wide shots go stiff and doll-like. Name the feeling, then the brows, lids and mouth.

## clip
- One gesture and one camera move per shot: stacked actions drift. Repeat the clay words in the action ("the clay dog's ears flop"), so the material survives the motion.
