## sheet_look
Mixed-media paper collage: a character with a black-and-white photographic cutout face on a hand-drawn paper body, clothing collaged from colourful patterned paper, hand-drawn ink details over the paper, rough scissor-cut edges. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit collaged from patterned paper, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance. Everything is real physical paper, photographed: no vector art, no frame or board around it.

## place_look
Mixed-media paper collage world: one deep coherent scene built from cutouts, structures of cardboard and plain kraft paper, scraps of patterned, coloured and photographic paper and washi tape strips tucked through it, hand-drawn ink lines, rich saturated collage colours against kraft-paper neutrals, with no letters, numbers or printed words anywhere; the background is scenery (sky, street, room), never a board behind pasted pieces.

## still_look
The Paper Collage look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Mixed-media paper collage world — the entire scene built from cutouts composed into one deep coherent scene: black-and-white photographic cutout faces on hand-drawn paper bodies, clothing collaged from colorful patterned paper, structures built from cardboard and plain kraft paper, scraps of patterned, colored and photographic paper and washi tape strips tucked through the scene, hand-drawn ink details over the paper, rich saturated collage colors against kraft-paper neutrals, the camera inside the scene. No letters, numbers or printed words on any scrap. Faces, builds and outfits come from the character sheets. Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed — visible paper grain and fibers, cut edges showing paper thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio lighting, the paper shot filling the entire frame."

## still
- State the camera's place inside the paper world in every frame ("at eye level with the paper character", "worm's-eye view up at the cardstock towers", "overhead, straight down on the paper street"). Never "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop", "table", "board" or "frame": they show the craft from outside. If one does, "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene".
- Name the paper on each major object ("a bowl folded from blue card", "shelves layered from patterned card"), not only in the look. Say "papercraft" near faces and products, never "paper cut", which blurs features.
- Every scrap is textless: patterned, coloured or photographic paper, never a newspaper, a magazine page, a stamp or a label with letters, numbers or printed words: random clipping text only comes out garbled. A readable word appears only when the story needs it, stated exactly (rule 32).
- The product stays photographic here: a photo cutout of the real product with rough scissor-cut edges, composed into the scene, so its label stays exact. Compose every piece into one deep scene: never a flat-lay board of pasted items.

## clip
- Repeat the paper words in the action ("the cardstock sun pivots", "the paper waves slide"), so the texture survives the motion. Paper moves as flat planes that slide, pivot and parallax, never squashing, stretching or moving organically; the strongest move is a slow push in through the layered paper planes. One move per shot.
- Between settings the paper can carry the cut: the whole scene lifts and turns like a storybook page, the camera pushes into a cutout opening and the next shot lies inside it, the top layer peels back to show the next shot beneath, the pieces slide apart and the next shot's pieces slide in and settle with their shadows, the scene folds flat and the next rises like a pop-up spread, a torn edge sweeps across to show the next shot behind it, or the camera passes a large foreground paper cloud and comes out in the next scene. Name the one visible action; keep hard cuts inside one setting.
