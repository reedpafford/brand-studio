## sheet_look
Flat construction-paper cutout: a jointed paper-puppet character built from simple flat card shapes with snipped edges and visible construction-paper grain, bold hard colour fills, soft drop shadows between the flat layers; a simple papercraft face. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit of flat card shapes, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance. Everything is real physical paper, photographed: no vector art, no frame or board around it.

## place_look
Flat construction-paper cutout: a scene of simple layered flat card shapes with snipped edges and visible paper grain, bold flat colours, soft drop shadows between the layers, the scene filling the entire frame.

## still_look
The Flat Paper look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Flat construction-paper cutout scene — simple layered flat card shapes, snipped edges, visible paper grain, jointed paper puppet characters, soft drop shadows between the layers, bold flat colors, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the paper world. Faces and builds come from the character sheets, and outfits too unless this video gives its own. Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed — visible paper grain and fibers, cut edges showing paper thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio lighting, the paper shot filling the entire frame."

## still
- State the camera's place inside the paper world in every frame ("at eye level with the paper character", "worm's-eye view up at the cardstock towers", "overhead, straight down on the paper street"). Never "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop", "table", "board" or "frame": they show the craft from outside. If one does, "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene".
- Name the paper on each major object ("a bowl folded from blue card", "shelves layered from patterned card"), not only in the look. Say "papercraft" near faces and products, never "paper cut", which blurs features.
- Bold, saturated flat colour, stated in every frame. A jointed paper puppet does one simple gesture a shot ("raises one arm, pivoting at the shoulder"); comedy lands on the pose, not on smooth movement.

## clip
- Repeat the paper words in the action ("the cardstock sun pivots", "the paper waves slide"), so the texture survives the motion. Paper moves as flat planes that slide, pivot and parallax, never squashing, stretching or moving organically; the strongest move is a slow push in through the layered paper planes. One move per shot.
- Between settings the paper can carry the cut: the whole scene lifts and turns like a storybook page, the camera pushes into a cutout opening and the next shot lies inside it, the top layer peels back to show the next shot beneath, the pieces slide apart and the next shot's pieces slide in and settle with their shadows, the scene folds flat and the next rises like a pop-up spread, a torn edge sweeps across to show the next shot behind it, or the camera passes a large foreground paper cloud and comes out in the next scene. Name the one visible action; keep hard cuts inside one setting.
