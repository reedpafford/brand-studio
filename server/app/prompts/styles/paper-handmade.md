## sheet_look
Handmade paper stop-motion: a character built from torn construction paper with visible glue seams, finger-crumpled textures and hand-placed imperfection, construction-paper grain on every piece; a simple papercraft face. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit of torn and glued paper, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance. Everything is real physical paper, photographed: no vector art, no frame or board around it.

## place_look
Handmade paper stop-motion: torn paper edges, visible glue seams, construction-paper grain, finger-crumpled textures and hand-placed imperfection, soft practical lighting with visible falloff, the scene filling the entire frame.

## still_look
The Handmade Paper look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Handmade paper stop-motion scene — torn paper edges, visible glue seams, construction-paper grain, hand-placed imperfection, soft practical lighting with visible falloff, the scene filling the entire frame. Faces and builds come from the character sheets, and outfits too unless this video gives its own. Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed — visible paper grain and fibers, cut edges showing paper thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio lighting, the paper shot filling the entire frame."

## still
- State the camera's place inside the paper world in every frame ("at eye level with the paper character", "worm's-eye view up at the cardstock towers", "overhead, straight down on the paper street"). Never "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop", "table", "board" or "frame": they show the craft from outside. If one does, "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene".
- Name the paper on each major object ("a bowl folded from blue card", "shelves layered from patterned card"), not only in the look. Say "papercraft" near faces and products, never "paper cut", which blurs features.
- State the palette in every frame, saturated and contrasting. Always say "paper stop-motion", never "stop-motion" alone, which drifts to clay.

## clip
- Repeat the paper words in the action ("the cardstock sun pivots", "the paper waves slide"), so the texture survives the motion. Paper moves as flat planes that slide, pivot and parallax, never squashing, stretching or moving organically; the strongest move is a slow push in through the layered paper planes. One move per shot.
- Between settings the paper can carry the cut: the whole scene lifts and turns like a storybook page, the camera pushes into a cutout opening and the next shot lies inside it, the top layer peels back to show the next shot beneath, the pieces slide apart and the next shot's pieces slide in and settle with their shadows, the scene folds flat and the next rises like a pop-up spread, a torn edge sweeps across to show the next shot behind it, or the camera passes a large foreground paper cloud and comes out in the next scene. Name the one visible action; keep hard cuts inside one setting.
