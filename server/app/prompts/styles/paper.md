## sheet_look
Layered papercraft: a character built from stacked cardstock, with visible paper thickness on every cut edge, layered cast shadows between the paper planes and real paper grain and fibres, in saturated, high-contrast colour; the face is papercraft with clear, simple features. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit of layered card in clear colours, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance. Everything is real physical paper, photographed: no vector art, no frame or board around it.

## place_look
Layered papercraft world: everything built from stacked cardstock with visible paper thickness, deep layered depth between foreground, midground and background paper planes, layered cast shadows, paper quilling details, saturated colour stepping clearly from layer to layer, soft studio light, the paper world extending past every edge of the frame.

## still_look

Faces, builds and outfits come from the character sheets. Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed: visible grain and fibres, cut edges showing their thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio light."

## still
- State the palette in every frame, saturated and contrasting ("greens stepping from lime to deep forest"), and name the paper on each major object ("a bowl folded from blue card", "a bag snipped from cream cardstock with its real label").
- Say "papercraft" near faces and products, never "paper cut" (it blurs features). State the camera's place inside the world ("worm's-eye view up at the cardstock towers"). Never "table", "board" or "frame": the paper shot fills the whole frame.

## clip
- Repeat the paper words in the action ("the cardstock sun pivots", "the paper waves slide"), so the texture survives the motion. One move per shot.
