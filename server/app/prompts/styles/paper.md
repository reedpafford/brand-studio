## sheet_look
Layered papercraft: a character built from stacked cardstock, with visible paper thickness on every cut edge, layered cast shadows between the paper planes and real paper grain and fibres, in saturated, high-contrast colour; the face is papercraft with clear, simple features. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit of layered card in clear colours, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance. Everything is real physical paper, photographed: no vector art, no frame or board around it.

## place_look
Layered papercraft world: everything built from stacked cardstock with visible paper thickness, deep layered depth between foreground, midground and background paper planes, layered cast shadows, paper quilling details, rich sophisticated colour stepping clearly from layer to layer, a backlit glow where warmth is wanted, soft studio light, the paper world extending past every edge of the frame.

## still_look
The Paper look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Layered papercraft world — everything built from stacked cardstock with visible paper thickness, deep layered depth between foreground, midground and background paper planes, layered cast shadows, paper quilling details, the camera inside the scene and the paper world extending past every edge of the frame. Faces, builds and outfits come from the character sheets. Everything in frame is real physical paper, photographed — visible paper grain and fibers, cut edges showing paper thickness, real cast shadows, soft studio lighting, the paper shot filling the entire frame."

## clip_style
Layered papercraft stop-motion, real cardstock with visible edges and layered shadows, subtle stepped motion with slight frame-to-frame jitter and no motion blur

## still
- State the camera's place inside the paper world in every frame ("at eye level with the paper character", "worm's-eye view up at the cardstock towers", "overhead, straight down on the paper street"). Never "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop", "table", "board" or "frame": they show the craft from outside. If one does, "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene".
- Name the paper on each major object ("a bowl folded from blue card", "shelves layered from patterned card"), not only in the look. Say "papercraft" near faces and products, never "paper cut", which blurs features.
- State the palette in every frame, saturated and contrasting ("greens stepping from lime to deep forest", "a vivid orange sun against a pale sky").

## clip
- Repeat the paper words in the action ("the cardstock sun pivots", "the paper waves slide"), so the texture survives the motion. Paper moves as flat planes that slide, pivot and parallax, never squashing, stretching or moving organically; the strongest move is a slow push in through the layered paper planes. One move per shot.
- Between settings the paper can carry the cut: the whole scene lifts and turns like a storybook page, the camera pushes into a cutout opening and the next shot lies inside it, the top layer peels back to show the next shot beneath, the pieces slide apart and the next shot's pieces slide in and settle with their shadows, the scene folds flat and the next rises like a pop-up spread, a torn edge sweeps across to show the next shot behind it, or the camera passes a large foreground paper cloud and comes out in the next scene. Name the one visible action; keep hard cuts inside one setting.
