# Step: Script draft
<!-- Inputs: <brief> (goal, audience, platform/aspect, planned seconds, format), <cast>, optional <reference> (a shot-by-shot breakdown of a reel to adapt: a catalogue reel, or the script's own imported reel), optional <new_lines>true</new_lines> (write fresh dialogue in the reference's style). Output: ScriptDrafts. -->

The editor has a brief and no script yet. Write 2 or 3 drafts in the house format, each with a clearly different hook (an accusation, a dare, a shocking number, a caught-in-the-act moment). The rest of the story may be shared.

Size each draft to the planned length. The house style speaks fast, about 4.8 words per second while someone talks, in short 1–5 word lines fired back and forth, and talking fills under half the runtime: the montage, reactions and the brand moment are silent. A 30 s video is roughly 50–75 spoken words; 60 s is 100–150. The first line is spoken at 0 s.

Pick one of the house story shapes (docs/house-style, from the most-viewed animated reels) and adapt it; don't force it:
- **They said I'd fail:** a doubter confronts the hero over money, with a number, in the first line → the hero's short dare by about 5 s → a wordless grind montage → a cold night low point → the brand's moment lands (the screen, the bag) → scale-up → a shared or mirrored win in under 8 words → a comic button (the doubter's scream) → about 2 s of the real brand element.
- **She chose money:** a public rejection at a big moment → the rival flaunts it → the hero alone → grind montage → time jump → the mirrored meeting, the ex wants back in → the hero's smug one-liner → the brand element.
- **Status gap:** someone rich mocks the hero → "Just watch." → a satisfying proof montage → growth → the hero returns on top → a quotable line → the brand element.

A grind or proof montage earns its payoff: its action line lists at least three effort or setback beats that build (a red day, a small first win, another late night, a bigger win) before the got-rich payoff, never one trade straight to the car. **Money climbs.** Every trading or win beat in the montage, the ★ screen lines included, says what the screen reads ("the position reads +$42", then "the balance reads $1,240", then "$18,900"), each bigger than the one before; a red day may show a loss. The payoff balance is clearly large, six figures or more (e.g. "$512,400"). This holds for an adapted reference too: its montage is rebuilt shot by shot with the figures, never summed up in one paragraph. When the story opens on an exchange (a proposal and the no, a dare), the script's first lines are that exchange, with nothing set up before it.

**Adapting a reference.** When there is a <reference>, the editor wants that reel remade for this brand. Keep what makes it work: the hook mechanic, the beat order and timing, the shots, the story shape, the pacing and feel, and how much is spoken (match the reference's word count, not the guide above).
- **Its dialogue stays, word for word.** Every spoken line the reference keeps is in each draft exactly as the reel says it, by the same character, in the same order. Rewrite only the lines the brand swap needs (a reselling line becomes a trading line, a product name becomes the brand's), keep each rewrite to the same length and rhythm, and list every rewritten line, as written in the draft, in `changed_lines`. Nothing else is reworded, trimmed or "improved"; the editor changes words before approving if they want to.
- **Nobody new speaks.** The speakers are the reference's (by the cast's name where the cast has that role, else by their part, e.g. GROOM), and no one else gets a line; the end line goes to the hero.
- **Echoes repeat what was said.** A voice heard in someone's head (`NAME (V.O.)` from a character who also speaks on screen: a memory, a taunt that echoes) repeats words that character said earlier, word for word or clearly shortened ("You're just a loser who doesn't get invited to anything." echoes as "…doesn't get invited to anything."), never a new line: the callback only lands if the viewer heard it first. When the reference's echo is close to an earlier line but not the same (usually a transcription slip, e.g. "New position doesn't get invited to anything." after "You're just a loser who doesn't get invited to anything."), write the earlier line and list it in `changed_lines`.
- **With <new_lines>true</new_lines>**, write new dialogue instead: the same characters, beats, shots and pacing, each line doing the same job at the same length in the brand's voice, and `changed_lines` stays empty.
- Rebuild anything the reference marks "Replace" from the brief, keeping its length and its visual device, and put the brand's screens there. Never copy its on-screen text or captions. Live-action segments are never kept: their slot becomes the brand's end line. Here the 2–3 drafts differ in how they play the hook (framing, timing, the reaction), never in its words.

Every draft ends on the brand's end line from the brand block, word for word (or the brief's closing line if the brand has none), said by the hero over the real brand element.

Use only the cast you are given, by name. Put every brand moment on its own ★ line with the screen that fits. Dialogue is short, spoken, and sounds like real people; no narrator explaining the story. Captions, text overlays and music are added in post, so don't write them.
