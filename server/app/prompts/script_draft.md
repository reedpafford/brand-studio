# Step: Script draft
<!-- Inputs: <brief> (goal, audience, platform/aspect, planned seconds, format), <cast>, optional <reference> (a shot-by-shot breakdown of a reel to adapt). Output: ScriptDrafts. -->

The editor has a brief and no script yet. Write 2 or 3 drafts in the house format, each with a clearly different hook (an accusation, a dare, a shocking number, a caught-in-the-act moment). The rest of the story may be shared.

Size each draft to the planned length: about 3.85 spoken words per second, and leave room for silent beats (reactions, inserts, a short grind montage). A 30 s video is roughly 70–90 spoken words; 60 s is 140–180.

A shape that works for these brands (adapt it, don't force it):
hook (0–2 s, the doubter acts on the hero, stakes in frame) → doubt grows → the low point (quiet, held) → the grind or the try (no dialogue, quick inserts) → the payoff on the brand's screen → the reversal (the doubter's own words come back) → a one-line call to action.

**Adapting a reference.** When there is a <reference>, the editor wants that reel remade for this brand. Keep what makes it work: the hook mechanic, the beat order and timing, the story shape, the pacing and how much is spoken (match the reference's word count, not the guide above). Write your own words wherever the brand or cast needs them (short lines that land like the reference's are fine). Rebuild anything the reference marks "Replace" from the brief, keeping its length and its visual device, and put the brand's screens there. Never copy its on-screen text or captions; leave out a live-action tag at the end unless the brief asks. Here the 2–3 drafts differ in how they play the hook, not in its mechanic.

Use only the cast you are given, by name. Put every brand moment on its own ★ line with the screen that fits. Dialogue is short, spoken, and sounds like real people; no narrator explaining the story. Captions, text overlays and music are added in post, so don't write them.
