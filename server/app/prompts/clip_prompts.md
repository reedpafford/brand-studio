# Step: Clip prompts
<!-- Inputs: <video_model> (name, min/max seconds, reference limits, recommended images), <assets> (id, kind, name, what it shows; storyboard frames as frame:<clip id>; voice samples as audio), <breakdown> (JSON). Output: ClipPrompts. -->

Write one video prompt per clip in the breakdown. The model generates the whole clip in one go, cuts included, with its own sound, so the prompt is a short production brief for the clip, shot by shot.

**References.** Use only the assets listed (our own approved character sheets, places, storyboard frames and brand screens; never a raw uploaded photo of a person), never a frame from a reference reel or the house-style board. Always use reference media, never the first-frame setting (the model can't combine the two); the storyboard frame goes in as [Image1] and the References line names it the first frame. In order: the clip's storyboard frame, then the characters in the order they first appear in the clip's shots, the place, then every brand screen the clip's shots list, with the payoff screen last. Keep a clip to 2 screens, or 3 for a got-rich arc (chart down → chart up → big balance): with more, the model mixes them up. Prefer 8 or fewer images; drop the least important prop or place before a character or a screen. Add a character's voice sample as audio when it has one, and bind it in the References line ("[Image2] is Aiden and uses the voice from [Audio1]."). If the assets include a reference video segment for this clip, attach it as video and write "Match the cutting rhythm and camera moves of [Video1]; not its characters, setting or text." Images are [Image1], [Image2], … in the order you list them; videos [Video1], …; audios [Audio1], …

**Prompt shape** (plain text, these lines, nothing else; the first and last lines are the same audio line, word for word):
```
Audio: only the spoken lines and <room sound>. No music, background music, BGM, score, instrumental, melody, synth effects, ambient pad, drone, swelling tone or stinger. No subtitles.
References: [Image1] is the first frame. [Image2] is Aiden. [Image3] is Dad. [Image4] is the fomo "Token chart up" screen.
Summary: <who does what, where, and the turn or payoff, in one sentence>.
Style: Pixar-style 3D animation, big glossy eyes, soft subsurface skin, warm amber key against cool blue shadows, shallow depth of field, locked-off cinematic framing, big readable expressions.
Setting: <the scene's look: palette, visible light, depth, background people>.
Shots (hard cuts):
Shot 1 | 0-2 s. Low-angle close-up, push in. Dad leans over the desk, brows crashed down. Dad (shouting): "Seven days. Ten grand."
Shot 2 | 2-3 s. Two quick cuts: an extreme close-up of Aiden, his eyes snap wide; then a close-up of Dad, one brow arching. No one speaks.
Shot 3 | 3-4 s. Close-up on the phone, static. Aiden's phone fills the frame and shows [Image4], the fomo Token chart up screen. No one speaks.
Keep: Aiden (red beanie, grey hoodie) as in [Image2] and Dad (grey beard, blue work shirt) as in [Image3], one of each; the screen's logo and layout as [Image4].
Audio: only the spoken lines and <room sound>. No music, background music, BGM, score, instrumental, melody, synth effects, ambient pad, drone, swelling tone or stinger. No subtitles.
```
Aspect, length and resolution are settings, not prompt words: never write them.

**Nobody speaks unless the script says so.** The model voices anything that reads like a line, so: a clip with no spoken line opens and closes with "Audio: no one speaks (no voices, narration or dialogue), only <room sound>. No music, background music, BGM, score, instrumental, melody, synth effects, ambient pad, drone, swelling tone or stinger. No subtitles." instead; every shot without a line ends with "No one speaks."; and action is what the body does, never a line in disguise (not "does not sell", "says no", "tells her"; write "his thumb hovers over Sell, then pulls away").

**Montages.** The main character is in every montage shot, in frame from its first moment and already mid-action: write him first ("Theo, already in the doorway, shoves the doors open"), never a reveal, an entrance into an empty frame, or the place before him. Name him in every quick cut ("Malik's hands fly over the keyboard", not "his hands"). The grind beats before the payoff each get a whole second (a late night, a red day, a bigger win), and only the got-rich payoff run shares seconds in quick cuts. Each beat is something he does or gets (see the breakdown's montage beats).

**Opening.** The first shot is the breakdown's first shot, the inciting exchange: the line and the reaction on screen from the first frame (Shot 1 is the close-up of Nate saying "I do." at the altar, and the bride's "HELL NO!" follows in the next second). Never open on a wide establishing shot or a crowd cutaway the breakdown doesn't have.

**Timing.** Shot times are whole seconds written with a plain hyphen ("Shot 2 | 2-3 s."), back to back from 0 to the clip length (the breakdown keeps its decimals; only this prompt rounds). Round each shot to the nearest second; shots shorter than a second share one segment written as "Two quick cuts: …; then …" (or three), and no segment is shorter than 1 s. A reaction that must be seen gets its own whole second instead of sharing one. Spread the dialogue shots across the clip so the talking doesn't finish early and leave dead air.

**Making it play.** Dialogue is `Name (how it's said): "words"`, word for word from the breakdown, and each spoken line appears once in the prompt. In a dialogue shot with others in frame, add that the others keep their mouths closed. Voiceover is `Voiceover (<who>, <how it sounds>): "words"`, and nobody on screen lip-syncs it. Acting is big and specific: eyes widen before the jaw drops, brows crash for anger, the desperate one is frantic, the winner moves slow and calm, and reactions hold about half a second. Show emotion through face and body, never as light in the eyes. On a payoff line, write the visible end state, never the old problem: if the line says the eyes healed or there's "nothing to wipe", the shot shows his eyes clean, clear and dry, and the prompt never mentions tears, wiping or the symptom (the model draws the words it reads). The Keep line holds each character's identity across every shot, even when their outfit or setting changes: the same face shape, skin tone, age and build as in their sheet, named in words ("Nate as in [Image2]: the same narrow face, light skin tone, age and slim build in every shot, whatever he wears"). When a shot's outfit hides a signature feature (a cap over the hair, a hood), keep a cue of it visible and say so ("dark curls showing under the cap"): the model redraws a face it can't recognise. Name each character's fixed look from their sheet's description in the Keep line (a dog's coat and markings, e.g. "brindle coat, white chest blaze"; a person's hair and outfit) so no shot changes their colours. Every action is physically plausible for that body: a dog walks, trots, sits, sniffs, wags; it never leaps straight up, floats or bends wrongly. Mention water, waves or echo only when the scene has them. The camera is always a third-person camera: never a POV shot with the viewer's own hands (a character's hand pushes the door). One camera move per shot, in standard terms (close-up, push in, pull out, pan, track, orbit, low angle, overhead, handheld); write anything rarer as "term: what you see".

**Brand screens.** Any shot with a screen describes it as: the phone (or monitor) fills the frame, held still and flat-on, and shows [ImageN], named by its screen every time, without quote marks (e.g. "shows [Image5], the fomo Home (rich) screen"; quotes mean speech), with its logo, colours and layout exactly, and its numbers read the figure the breakdown's shot states ("the balance reads $1,240"), word for word, never a smaller one than the shot before; only a shot that states no figure says the numbers may differ. Keep hands and glare off the screen. Beyond a number you name, never describe the screen's content in your own words; the reference is the content. A got-rich payoff shows a big portfolio balance (hundreds of thousands), never a coin's price: use the listed screen that already shows one (its name says rich, portfolio or balance), else the home screen, and name the shot's balance ("the portfolio balance reads $512,400"); any six-figure balance is fine, it needn't match the one printed on the screen. The model copies pictures far better than it writes numbers. The end line's shot (the call to action, "Want to trade like me?") follows the same rule: when a screen already shows a big balance, that's the one its phone shows, never an empty $0.00 home screen. A product (a bag) is shown as [ImageN]: front label to camera, steady, shape, logo and main text exactly, no face or limbs unless the script makes it a character. Frame a product no tighter than the whole bag, with its main text large: the brand name, the main claim line and the flavour ("PEANUT BUTTER FLAVOR") must read; small print may blur, so never a close-up of a badge, the net weight or the fine print.
