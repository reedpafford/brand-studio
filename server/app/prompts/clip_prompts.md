# Step: Clip prompts
<!-- Inputs: <video_model> (name, min/max seconds, reference limits, recommended images), <assets> (id, kind, name, what it shows; storyboard frames as frame:<clip id>; voice samples as audio), <breakdown> (JSON). Output: ClipPrompts. -->

Write one video prompt per clip in the breakdown. The model generates the whole clip in one go, cuts included, with its own sound, so the prompt is a short production brief for the clip, shot by shot.

**References.** Use only the assets listed (our own approved character sheets, places, storyboard frames and brand screens; never a raw uploaded photo of a person), never a frame from a reference reel or the house-style board. Always use reference media, never the first-frame setting (the model can't combine the two); the storyboard frame goes in as [Image1] and the References line names it the first frame, which sets the opening composition only: the character sheets set each face and outfit and override the first frame where they differ, and the References line says so. In order: the clip's storyboard frame, then the characters (the main character's sheet first, the rest in the order they first appear in the clip's shots), the place, then every brand screen the clip's shots list, with the payoff screen last. Keep a clip to 3 screens at most (e.g. a got-rich arc: chart down → chart up → big balance): with more, the model mixes them up. Prefer 8 or fewer images; drop the least important prop or place before a character or a screen. Add a character's voice sample as audio when it has one, and bind it in the References line ("[Image2] is Aiden and uses the voice from [Audio1]."). If the assets include a reference video segment for this clip, attach it as video and write "Match the cutting rhythm and camera moves of [Video1]; not its characters, setting or text." Images are [Image1], [Image2], … in the order you list them; videos [Video1], …; audios [Audio1], …

**Prompt shape** (plain text, these lines, nothing else; the first and last lines are the same audio line, word for word):
```
Audio: only the spoken lines and <room sound>. No music, background music, BGM, score, instrumental, melody, synth effects, ambient pad, drone, swelling tone or stinger. No subtitles.
References: [Image1] is the first frame. It sets the opening composition only; the character sheets set each face and outfit and override the first frame where they differ. [Image2] is Aiden. [Image3] is Dad. [Image4] is the fomo logo: the word fomo in lowercase.
Summary: <who does what, where, and the turn or payoff, in one sentence>.
Style: {{clip_style}}.
Setting: <the scene's look: palette, visible light, depth, background people>.
Shots (hard cuts):
Shot 1 | 0-2 s. Low-angle close-up, push in. Dad leans over the desk, brows crashed down. Dad (in English, shouting): "Seven days. Ten grand."
Shot 2 | 2-3 s. Extreme close-up of Aiden, snap zoom: his eyes snap wide. No one speaks.
Shot 3 | 3-4 s. Over-the-shoulder close-up, static. The phone in Aiden's hand shows an invented trading screen in the fomo style, the fomo logo from [Image4], the word fomo in lowercase, sharp and legible in the top corner; the green chart shoots up to +$900, large and readable. No one speaks.
Keep: Aiden's identity and clothing stable in every shot, as in [Image2] (red beanie, grey hoodie), and Dad's, as in [Image3] (grey beard, blue work shirt); one of each; Aiden never in Dad's work shirt; the fomo logo as [Image4].
Audio: only the spoken lines and <room sound>. No music, background music, BGM, score, instrumental, melody, synth effects, ambient pad, drone, swelling tone or stinger. No subtitles.
```
Aspect, length and resolution are settings, not prompt words: never write them.

**Nobody speaks unless the script says so.** The model voices anything that reads like a line, so: a clip with no spoken line opens and closes with "Audio: no one speaks (no voices, narration or dialogue), only <room sound>. No music, background music, BGM, score, instrumental, melody, synth effects, ambient pad, drone, swelling tone or stinger. No subtitles." instead; every shot without a line ends with "No one speaks."; and action is what the body does, never a line in disguise (not "does not sell", "says no", "tells her", "replies", "answers", "shouts"; write "types back", "nods", "shakes his head", "his thumb hovers over Sell, then pulls away").

**Montages.** The main character is in every montage shot, in frame from its first moment and already mid-action: write him first ("Theo, already in the doorway, shoves the doors open"), never a reveal, an entrance into an empty frame, or the place before him. Name him in every shot ("Malik's hands fly over the keyboard", not "his hands"). The grind beats before the payoff each get a segment of their own, long enough to read (a late night, a red day, a bigger win), and so does the payoff. One shot is one image: never "two quick cuts" inside a shot, which the model draws as a split screen, and never a split screen. Beats too short for a whole second in one place run on as one continuous action in one shot ("spins in the chair, slams the faders up and leans back, arms wide"); a beat in another place gets its own shot or is dropped. At most one payoff per clip. Each beat is something he does or gets (see the breakdown's montage beats), written as an action with energy, never a pose: he slams, spins, tosses, sprints or laughs, and the camera moves with it. For a trading app, the grind shows him trading, not only reacting: "Malik's thumb scrolls the coin list and taps Buy, his face lit green by the screen". The cuts stay quick; the face and outfit come from his sheet in every one.

**Opening.** The first shot is the breakdown's first shot, the inciting exchange: the line and the reaction on screen from the first frame (Shot 1 is the close-up of Nate saying "I do." at the altar, and the bride's "HELL NO!" follows in the next second). Never open on a wide establishing shot or a crowd cutaway the breakdown doesn't have.

**Timing.** Shot times are whole seconds written with a plain hyphen ("Shot 2 | 2-3 s."), back to back from 0 to the clip length (the breakdown keeps its decimals; only this prompt rounds). Round each shot to the nearest second; shots shorter than a second share one segment written as "Two quick cuts: …; then …" (or three), and no segment is shorter than 1 s. A reaction that must be seen gets its own segment, long enough to land, instead of sharing one. The clip's last line never ends in its last moments: leave the closing shot room for the reaction to land, and end on the hand-off the breakdown's `seam` names; the first shot answers the previous clip's hand-off. Spread the dialogue shots across the clip so the talking doesn't finish early and leave dead air.

**Making it play.** Dialogue is `Name (in English, how it's said): "words"`, word for word from the breakdown, and each spoken line appears once in the prompt; every line names its language, or the model may speak another one. The quoted words are the breakdown's character for character, capitals included (rule 19); how a name is said goes only in the how-it's-said note, without quote marks (quotes are speech), never into the quote: `Mom (in English, warm, saying fomo as FOH-moh, rhyming with promo): "Get fomo."`. In a dialogue shot with others in frame, add that the others keep their mouths closed. Voiceover is `Voiceover (<who>, in English, <how it sounds>): "words"`, and nobody on screen lip-syncs it. Acting is big and specific: eyes widen before the jaw drops, brows crash for anger, the desperate one is frantic, the winner moves slow and calm, and reactions hold long enough to land; a reaction names the feeling and its reason, then what the face and body do ("touched by her kindness: eyes well, a shaky smile"). The feeling is a named emotion and its cause, never words a character could say ("can't believe it worked", "here we go"): the video model speaks them aloud, even in a shot with no lines, so a gasp of gratitude never reads as shock. Show emotion through face and body, never as light in the eyes. In a crying or tearful close-up the face keeps its sheet's age and shape: tears on the same face, never a younger or rounder one. On a payoff line, write the visible end state, never the old problem: if the line says the eyes healed or there's "nothing to wipe", the shot shows his eyes clean, clear and dry, and the prompt never mentions tears, wiping or the symptom (the model draws the words it reads). The Keep line holds each character's identity and clothing across every shot, even when the setting changes: the same face shape, skin tone, age and build as in their sheet, in the official form ("Keep: Nate's identity and clothing stable in every shot, as in [Image2]: the same narrow face, light skin tone, age and slim build"). The outfit changes only where the script changes it. When a shot's outfit hides a signature feature (a cap over the hair, a hood), keep a cue of it visible and say so ("dark curls showing under the cap"): the model redraws a face it can't recognise. Name each character's fixed look from their sheet's description in the Keep line (a dog's coat and markings, e.g. "brindle coat, white chest blaze"; a person's hair and outfit) so no shot changes their colours. Every character the clip attaches is in its Keep line, by the name its References line gives, with the outfit from their own sheet, never one from the role's or the script's description (a cast line may still carry an old role's clothes), and that outfit stays the same in every clip of the video unless the script changes it. A character with a sheet looks exactly as it, whatever any words say: never write their face, breed, coat, skin, hair or colouring in a shot or the Keep line, not even from the breakdown or a role's name ("Biscuit, the golden dog" with a French bulldog sheet is "Biscuit, as in [Image3]", never "golden coat"). When two or more people share a clip, pin each outfit to its owner and add a "never" for the clothes most likely to swap ("Omar in the grey quarter-zip, never the rival's black leather jacket"): the model otherwise moves one person's clothes onto another. Every action is physically plausible for that body: a dog walks, trots, sits, sniffs, wags; it never leaps straight up, floats or bends wrongly. Mention water, waves or echo only when the scene has them. The camera is always a third-person camera: never a POV shot with the viewer's own hands (a character's hand pushes the door). A shot after a time jump opens on a quick white flash, written first ("White flash. Two years later, Nate…"). One camera move per shot, in standard terms (close-up, push in, pull out, pan, track, orbit, low angle, overhead, handheld); write anything rarer as "term: what you see".

**Never reads as sexual.** The video model refuses a clip that could read as sexual, even an innocent one, and a reworded one still failed: a clip whose first frame or shots show a person in bed, under the covers or undressed carries no bodily sound or action word at all (licking, slurping, sucking, wet, moaning, groaning, panting, gasping, sighing, tongue). The animal's behaviour goes in its own clip with no person in bed in frame ("Biscuit licks his own front paw on the rug, close on the paw"); the bed clip only refers to it as "a noise from the foot of the bed", and the person, in pyjamas, reacts with a look (eyes open, pulls the pillow over her head), never a sound.

**Brand screens.** Any shot with a screen shows it on the phone (or monitor) in the scene, in a character's hand or in front of him, over his shoulder or close on his hand holding it, with his face or hands in frame; never a screenshot filling the frame flat-on. The phone is held steady and angled to the camera, close enough that the brand's logo reads, and shows [ImageN], named by its screen every time, without quote marks (e.g. "shows [Image5], the fomo Home (rich) screen"; quotes mean speech), with its UI, logo, colours and layout exactly. Then name the coin, numbers or chart the shot needs, if any ("shows [Image5], the fomo Token chart up screen, the SNORE coin up to +$4,200"): the screen's look comes from the reference, its numbers from the story, so no two shots copy one screenshot's coin and price. Never describe the screen's look in your own words. Only a moment the brand has no screen for gets an invented one, in the brand's style with its logo: "the phone in his hand shows an invented trading screen in the fomo style, the fomo logo from [Image4], the word fomo in lowercase, sharp and legible in the top corner; the red chart dips to -$60". Either way, name the chart's shape and its one number, and make each winning beat bigger than the last and visibly rising (+$40, then +$900, then +$12,000). Always name the brand's word with the logo (the word fomo in lowercase): the picture alone came out as "JOMO" or as the icon with no word. Play a screen beat for drama: his face lit by the screen's glow, then a cut over his shoulder onto the phone in his hand, his thumb tapping; never a phone held up flat to us, except at the end line or a camera swap the script calls for. Keep glare off the display, and fingers off the logo. A got-rich payoff shows a big portfolio balance (hundreds of thousands), never a coin's price: use the listed screen that already shows one (its name says rich, portfolio or balance), else the home screen, and name the balance. The end line's shot (the call to action, "Want to trade like me?") is the one where the screen faces the camera: the character holds the phone up to the lens beside his face, in the scene, steady and large in frame, so the brand's logo and the app as it really looks read clearly ("Omar holds his phone up to the camera beside his grin; it shows [Image4], the fomo Home (rich) screen, the fomo logo sharp and legible"). Any of the brand's home-type screens will do; prefer the one that already shows a big balance. A camera swap or flip the breakdown calls for is written as one: "Whip pan into a front-facing selfie angle: Omar holds the phone at arm's length and talks straight to the lens; his other hand holds up [Image4], the fomo Home screen", never a plain cut to him holding the phone up. The brand's product appears only as its attached [ImageN], never an invented bag or label of your own. A product (a bag) is shown as [ImageN]: front label to camera, steady, shape, logo and main text exactly, no face or limbs unless the script makes it a character. Frame a product no tighter than the whole bag, with its main text large: the brand name, the main claim line and the flavour ("PEANUT BUTTER FLAVOR") must read; small print may blur, so never a close-up of a badge, the net weight or the fine print.
