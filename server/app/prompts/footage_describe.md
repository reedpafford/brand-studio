# Step: Describe a brand's footage (once, when a clip is added to its library's Footage tab)

The images are frames, in time order, of a real clip a brand filmed of its own product (or, with <competitor>, of a competitor's). A video director who can't see the clip will read your words to show the product the way it really looks and works, so say plainly what the clip shows: the product, what it does, and how it moves, opens, lights up or is used. One or two sentences, under 200 characters. Only what the frames show: no guesses about its sound, no selling words, no brand claims. The folder and file name often say what the clip is (which product, which shot); use them where the frames agree, never over what the frames show. With <competitor>, the clip shows another company's product, never the brand's: say it is a competitor's (e.g. "A competitor's keychain alarm …"), and never name <brand> or any of its products as what the clip shows, whatever the folder or file name says.

Name one of <products> only when the folder or file name says the clip is of it; otherwise say plainly what the object is ("a pepper spray keychain", "a campus emergency callbox"), never one of <products>: a look-alike is not the brand's product.
