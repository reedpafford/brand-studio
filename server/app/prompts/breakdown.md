# Step: Breakdown
<!-- Inputs: <brief>, <cast>, <places>, <video_model> (name, min/max seconds per clip, reference limits), <script> numbered, optional <reference> (a reel this video adapts). Output: Breakdown. -->

Turn the working script into scenes, shots and clips that the team will storyboard and generate. The editor can edit everything afterwards; give them a breakdown that already plays well.

**Shots.** Cover every script line, in order, with no gaps. Lines that start with a tag in brackets ("[Camera] …", "[Movement] …", "[Reaction] …", "[Brand screen] ★ …") are directions the editor accepted: use them to shape the shot they sit next to and list their line numbers in that shot's `script_lines`; they are never spoken or shown as text. The words in `line` are the script's, unchanged. Break dialogue so each speaker's line and each turn in an argument gets its own shot (shot and counter-shot), and give the big moments their own reaction shot. The house style (the most-viewed animated reels) cuts about once a second overall, with a median shot of about 0.55 s: arguments and reactions 0.3–1 s, wordless montage shots about 0.4 s, nothing over 3 s except a dialogue two-shot or one continuous reveal move; the low point is the one place it slows (a 3–5 s held close-up with a slow push in). Every big line cuts to the listener's face. Inserts (the phone screen, the number, hands on the keyboard, the clock) move the story without dialogue. Use standard camera terms (close-up, push in, pull out, pan, track, orbit, low angle, overhead, handheld; anything rarer as "term: what you see"), and write camera so it carries emotion: push in on the doubter's accusation, a high or isolated wide for the low point, a low angle and a tracking move for the payoff.

**Voiceover.** For a voiceover script, `speaker` is the narrator (e.g. 'Dog (V.O.)') and `line` holds the exact words heard during that shot; split long sentences across shots so the picture changes every 1–3 s and always shows what the words say. A dog narrator's mouth doesn't move unless the script makes the dog talk on screen.

**Scenes.** One per place or time. The `look` tells the story state in light and colour: cold blue and dark for struggle, the warm glow of a laptop or phone lighting the face for the grind, warm gold or neon for the win, background people who react when it's public.

**Brand.** Tag each shot's `moment` from the brand block. Any shot whose moment has a screen gets that screen in `screen_ids`, usually as its own insert, and the reaction to it in the next shot. If a trading or brand moment has no ★ line in the script, still show the screen: it's required. The video ends on about 2 s of the real brand element (the app screen, the bag) when the script gives it a closing line.

**Montages.** A montage is a run of quick wordless shots. The main character is in every one of them, in frame from the first moment: no empty rooms, no establishing shots without him, no entrance into an empty frame. When growing wealth matters to the story, every beat reads as trading → getting rich: concrete, escalating payoffs he's part of, invented for this story (a dealer drops car keys into his palm; he's behind the wheel of the new car; he pushes open the penthouse door; he walks out onto the balcony over the city), cut against the brand where it fits (the trade on his phone, the desk screen). The got-rich payoff shows a big portfolio balance (hundreds of thousands), never a coin's price, on the screen that already shows one when the brand has it (its name says rich, portfolio or balance), else the home screen; the screen's numbers are ours to set, its logo and layout are not. A montage clip uses only the screens its beats need, 3 at most (for a got-rich arc: chart down → chart up → the screen with the big balance): no home or app-open insert before the trade, and no desktop screen unless the script asks for it. The video model mixes up screens when a clip carries more.

**Clips.** Group consecutive shots into clips the video model generates in one go, each within its min and max seconds. End a clip only on a hard cut that changes place or time, or when the next shot would pass the max. With voiceover, end clips between sentences. A clip that starts after a time jump opens on an insert (phone, screen, hands) before the hero's face. Fewer, longer clips keep characters consistent; don't split a conversation across clips unless you must.

**Reference.** When there is a <reference>, match it beat by beat: its shot lengths and cut rhythm, its framing and camera moves, how big the acting is, and its light and palette, unless the script or brand needs something else. A section marked "Replace" keeps its length and its device (e.g. match cuts: same pose, bigger world) with the brand's content and screens in it. Live-action segments are gone; their slot is the brand's end line over the real brand element.

Use only ids from the user message and the brand block.
