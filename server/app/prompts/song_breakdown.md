# Song ads

This script is a song: its lines are lyrics, sung by the music model, and the <song> is the real take, measured. The song leads and the pictures follow it; everything above still holds unless this section says otherwise.

**The song is the clock.** Never guess a length: every shot sits on the song's real time, and the measured line times in <song> are that clock. A shot that holds a sung line starts where that line is sung and ends where it ends (or where the next line starts), from those times, never from the words' count. `song_start` and `song_end` are seconds in the song, on beats from <song>; the shots run back to back from 0 to the song's end with no gaps, instrumental stretches included (they get pictures too: the hero reacting, the product, the world moving on the beat). A shot holds one lyric line or less, and never runs across a section's edge; `seconds` is `song_end` minus `song_start`. A sung line's shot covers it from its first word to its last: never cut mid-word.

**B-roll by default.** Every shot is B-roll: silent picture under the song that shows what the line at that moment says, literally, so the story reads with the sound off. `line` and `speaker` are null for B-roll (nobody on screen speaks or sings; the song plays over it). `sung` is true only on lines or sections the script marks [Lip-sync]: the lead sings them on camera, `speaker` is the lead and `line` holds the sung words exactly. Vary the B-roll framing (wide, close on the product, the hero's face, an insert), cut on the beat, and give the chorus the biggest pictures.

**The shape that sells.** For a product that fixes a problem, Reed's Iron Paws songs ran this way, and their pictures should too: exaggerated, comic close-ups of each symptom on the hook; the daily routine it spoils; a cute trip inside the body that shows the cause (friendly cartoon microbes, a wave of greens); the find on the phone; the product into the bowl and the pet's mid-air leap; "weeks later", the change plain to see, then the apology and the hug; the proof, shown as something you can see (the vet's nod, the glossy coat, the clean bowl); and on the last line the pet nudges the product to the lens. Exaggerate like a cartoon, never gross: a fur blizzard, a vacuum's fur mountain, a shampoo-foam hat, a desert-dry nose with a tumbleweed, the wise old dog in slow motion. Proof words (a figure, a quote) are added in editing, never drawn.

**Clips follow the song.** A clip's shots are consecutive and its slice of the song runs from its first shot's `song_start` to its last shot's `song_end`; `seconds` is that span rounded up, within the video model's limits. End clips on a section's edge or a strong beat, never mid-line, and still one place and one action a clip. A clip with people in it runs 16 s at most: split a longer piece of the song in two at a beat, because faces drift in long clips.

**The brand.** The required screens and products still show at their moments, on the line that names or needs them. The product shows exactly, as its attached picture, at four moments: the find (on her phone), the feeding, the proof and the end. The last sung line's shot shows the real brand element (the pet nudging the bag to the lens, the phone with the app), large and clear.
