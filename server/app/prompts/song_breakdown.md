# Song ads

This script is a song: its lines are lyrics, sung by the music model, and the <song> is the real take, measured. The song leads and the pictures follow it; everything above still holds unless this section says otherwise.

**The song is the clock.** Never guess a length: every shot sits on the song's real time, and the measured line times in <song> are that clock. A shot that holds a sung line starts where that line is sung and ends where it ends (or where the next line starts), from those times, never from the words' count. `song_start` and `song_end` are seconds in the song, on beats from <song>; the shots run back to back from 0 to the song's end with no gaps, instrumental stretches included (they get pictures too: the hero reacting, the product, the world moving on the beat); the last shot holds the payoff through the song's tail after its last line, so it lands before the video ends. A shot holds one lyric line or less, and never runs across a section's edge; `seconds` is `song_end` minus `song_start`. A sung line's shot covers it from its first word to its last: never cut mid-word. A picture of something a line names (a number, the product, the scorecard) lands on the word that names it: start its shot at that word's time in <song>, splitting the line there.

**B-roll by default.** Every shot is B-roll: silent picture under the song that shows what the line at that moment says, literally, so the story reads with the sound off. `line` and `speaker` are null for B-roll (nobody on screen speaks or sings; the song plays over it). `sung` is true only on lines or sections the script marks [Lip-sync]: the lead sings them on camera, `speaker` is the lead and `line` holds the sung words exactly. Vary the B-roll framing (wide, close on the product, the hero's face, an insert), cut on the beat, and give the chorus the biggest pictures.

**A shape that has worked.** For a product that fixes a problem, Reed's pet-supplement songs ran this way; use it only when it fits this song's story, which is its own: exaggerated, comic close-ups of each symptom on the hook; the daily routine it spoils; a cute trip inside the body that shows the cause (friendly cartoon microbes, a wave of greens); the find on the phone; the product into the bowl and the pet's mid-air leap; "weeks later", the change plain to see, then the apology and the hug; the proof, shown as something you can see (the vet's nod, the glossy coat, the clean bowl); and on the last line the pet nudges the product to the lens. Exaggerate like a cartoon, never gross: a fur blizzard, a vacuum's fur mountain, a shampoo-foam hat, a desert-dry nose with a tumbleweed, the wise old dog in slow motion. Proof words (a figure, a quote) are added in editing, never drawn.

**Clips follow the song.** A clip's shots are consecutive and its slice of the song runs from its first shot's `song_start` to its last shot's `song_end`; `seconds` is that span rounded up, within the video model's limits. End clips on a section's edge or a strong beat, never mid-line, and still one place and one action a clip. Faces drift in long clips, so a clip with people in it past about 16 s is worth splitting in two at a beat, when the song allows it.

**The brand.** The required screens and products still show at their moments, on the line that names or needs them. The product shows exactly, as its attached picture, at four moments: the find (on her phone), the feeding, the proof and the end. A line that names the brand shows the real brand element (the pet nudging the bag to the lens, the phone with the app), large and clear; the song follows its script, so that isn't forced onto the last line.
