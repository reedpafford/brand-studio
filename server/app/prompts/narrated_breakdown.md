# Narrated videos

This script is narrated: every spoken line is the narrator's voiceover, read once as one track and laid under the finished video. The <song> block is that narration, measured: its sentences, each with when it's heard. Everything above still holds unless this section says otherwise.

**The narration is the clock.** Never guess a length: every shot sits on the narration's real time. One shot per sentence, or per phrase when a sentence runs over about 2.5 s: a shot starts where its sentence (or phrase) is heard and ends where it ends (or where the next one starts), from the measured times (a phrase from its words' times), never from the words' count; `song_start` and `song_end` are those seconds, back to back from 0 to the narration's end with no gaps (a pause gets a picture too: a reaction, the product, the world moving on). Never cut inside a sentence except between its phrases, and never inside a word. `seconds` is `song_end` minus `song_start`.

**All B-roll.** Every shot shows what its sentence says, literally, so the story reads with the sound off. Nobody on screen speaks or moves their lips: `line` and `speaker` are null and `sung` is false on every shot; the narrator is never on camera saying the words. A character may look into the lens at the turns and the offer. The one exception is a style with talker shots (UGC): a talker shot is the presenter saying that stretch of the narration straight to the lens, `sung` true (their one lip-synced take is cut in there), `line` and `speaker` still null; give talker shots clips of their own.

**Clips follow the narration.** A clip's shots are consecutive pictures in one place: neighbouring shots share one multi-shot clip, up to the video model's longest clip, and a shot shorter than its shortest clip shares a clip with its neighbours rather than standing alone (it would be made at the shortest and mostly thrown away). A talker's shots get clips of their own. Its slice runs from its first shot's `song_start` to its last shot's `song_end`; `seconds` is that span rounded up, within the video model's limits. End clips between sentences. Faces drift in long clips, so a clip with people in it past about 16 s is worth splitting at a sentence's end, when the narration allows it.
