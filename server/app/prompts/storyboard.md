# Step: Storyboard frames
<!-- Inputs: <image_model> (name, max reference images, max character images, aspect), <assets> (id, kind, name, what it shows), <breakdown> (JSON), optional <per_shot>true</per_shot>. Output: StoryboardFrames. -->

Write one image prompt per clip, for the clip's first shot (or one per shot when <per_shot> is true). Each still becomes the visual anchor for its clip, so it must look exactly like the shot will: the same characters, place, light and framing.

For each frame:
- Pick references only from the assets listed (our own approved character sheets, places, props and brand screens; never a raw uploaded photo of a person); never a frame from a reference reel or the house-style board, which show someone else's characters. Pick every character in the shot (their sheets, the main character's first: the sheet is the main reference for the face; a character without its sheet comes out a different colour), the place, and the brand screen if the shot shows one, in that order. Attach at most <max character images> character sheets; if more people are in the shot, attach the ones nearest the camera and describe the rest as background people. Stay within the image model's total limit.
- Open with a verb and say how each reference is used: "Generate a still of the boy from image 1 in the bedroom from image 2: <shot size and angle>, <who does what, with the expression at its peak>, <the scene's look: palette, visible light, depth, background people>." Name each character's fixed look from their sheet's description ("the brindle pitbull with the white chest blaze from image 1"), and describe the main character's face in detail from it (face shape, eyes, skin, hair), so the still keeps his likeness. Each character wears their sheet's outfit, never one from the role's or the script's description, unless the script changes it. A character with a sheet looks exactly as that sheet, whatever any words say: never write their face, breed, coat, skin, hair or colouring, not even from the breakdown or a role's name ("Biscuit, the golden dog" with a French bulldog sheet is a French bulldog: write "Biscuit from image 2", never "golden"). This is a still, so catch the face mid-reaction, not neutral.
- An app screen is on a phone in the scene, in the character's hand or one he's looking at, with his face or hands in frame (over his shoulder, or close on his hand holding it), never a screenshot filling the frame flat-on or a phone held up flat to us (only the end line's shot, or a camera swap the script calls for, faces the camera); his face lit by the screen's glow sells the beat: "…, seen over his shoulder as he holds up his phone. Place the app screen from image 3 onto the phone's display, filling it; keep its logo, colours and layout exactly." Frame it close enough that the brand's logo reads; only the logo must be right, and the numbers may be anything. When the image given is the brand's logo (a logo-only trading screen), invent the screen instead: "Put the fomo logo from image 3 in the top corner of an invented trading screen on the phone's display, with the word “fomo” in lowercase letters exactly as in image 3, sharp and legible: a dark trading app with a big green chart shooting up to +$900." Always write the brand's word in quotes that way, so it never comes out misspelled or as the icon alone. Name the chart and its number, each winning beat bigger than the last; never "keep its logo, colours and layout exactly" for it. The end line's shot (the call to action) is the one where the screen faces the camera: "…holds his phone up to the camera beside his face, screen toward the lens. Place the app screen from image 3 onto the phone's display, filling it; keep its logo, colours and layout exactly, the logo sharp and legible." For a got-rich payoff use the screen that already shows a big balance when one is listed, else the home screen; never write a figure for the screen to show. The brand's product appears only as its attached picture: never describe, invent or redraw a bag, label or packaging of your own; if a shot shows the product, its image is attached. A product is "Place the bag from image 3 <where>, label facing the camera; keep its shape, label, logo and colours completely unchanged." Show the whole bag, large and flat-on, so its brand name, main claim and flavour read; never a close-up of a badge or small print.
- In a montage, the main character is in every frame: never an empty room or a bare establishing shot.
- The image model refuses the whole still if anything reads as sensitive, so frame around it: no close-ups of body parts (legs, knees, thighs, chests; put the pet beside the person, with a sleeve or hand at the frame edge), no tight leggings or bare skin, a still with a person in bed or undressed shows no animal licking, no tongue and nothing bodily, and its words name none (keep them in pyjamas; the animal's action is its own still, with no one in bed), and describe symptoms gently and dry ("faint tear stains", "a pink, sore paw", never pus, blood, wounds or anything wet or oozing).
- {{still_look}} (Aspect is a setting: never write it.) The only text in frame is on the real brand screen or product.
