# Step: Storyboard frames
<!-- Inputs: <image_model> (name, max reference images, max character images, aspect), <assets> (id, kind, name, what it shows), <breakdown> (JSON), optional <per_shot>true</per_shot>. Output: StoryboardFrames. -->

Write one image prompt per clip, for the clip's first shot (or one per shot when <per_shot> is true). Each still becomes the visual anchor for its clip, so it must look exactly like the shot will: the same characters, place, light and framing.

For each frame:
- Pick references only from the assets listed (our own approved character sheets, places, props and brand screens; never a raw uploaded photo of a person); never a frame from a reference reel or the house-style board, which show someone else's characters. Pick every character in the shot (their sheets; a character without its sheet comes out a different colour), the place, and the brand screen if the shot shows one, in that order. Attach at most <max character images> character sheets; if more people are in the shot, attach the ones nearest the camera and describe the rest as background people. Stay within the image model's total limit.
- Open with a verb and say how each reference is used: "Generate a still of the boy from image 1 in the bedroom from image 2: <shot size and angle>, <who does what, with the expression at its peak>, <the scene's look: palette, visible light, depth, background people>." Name each character's fixed look from their sheet's description ("the brindle pitbull with the white chest blaze from image 1"). This is a still, so catch the face mid-reaction, not neutral.
- An app screen is "Place the app screen from image 3 onto the phone's display, filling it; keep its logo, colours and layout exactly." Its numbers read the figure the shot states ("…; the balance reads $1,240."), else they may differ; for a got-rich payoff use the screen that already shows a big balance when one is listed, else the home screen, with the shot's six-figure balance: "…; the portfolio balance reads $512,400." A product is "Place the bag from image 3 <where>, label facing the camera; keep its shape, label, logo and colours completely unchanged." Show the whole bag, large and flat-on, so its brand name, main claim and flavour read; never a close-up of a badge or small print.
- In a montage, the main character is in every frame: never an empty room or a bare establishing shot.
- The image model refuses the whole still if anything reads as sensitive, so frame around it: no close-ups of body parts (legs, laps, knees, thighs, chests; put the pet beside the person, with a sleeve or hand at the frame edge), no tight leggings or bare skin, and describe symptoms gently and dry ("faint tear stains", "a pink, sore paw", never crust, pus, blood, wounds or anything wet or oozing).
- The house look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Stylised 3D animated feature-film look (Pixar/Disney-like CG). Characters have slightly enlarged heads (about 6 heads tall), big glossy round eyes with one sharp catchlight, thick expressive brows, small noses and soft subsurface skin. Wardrobe is simple, clean and real-world, with fine cloth and prop detail. Lighting is motivated and cinematic: warm amber practicals or golden-hour sun against cool blue shadows, deep but not crushed blacks, no blown highlights. The frame is mostly locked off at eye level with a shallow depth of field and creamy background blur. Expressions are big and readable with the sound off." (Aspect is a setting: never write it.) The only text in frame is on the real brand screen or product.
