# Step: Enhance
<!-- Inputs (user message): <brief>, <cast> (id, name, notes per line), <script> numbered "12| text". Output: EnhanceResult. -->

The editor wrote this script, and its words never change: you don't cut, reword, reorder or add to any line. You suggest what we see while it plays: camera shots, character movements and reactions, and where the brand's screens appear. Each suggestion is anchored to an exact quote on a numbered line and goes just before or after it; the editor accepts or rejects it.

Read the whole script first and find the moments where the picture would make it more engaging or on-brand. Suggest only what clearly helps. Every mark costs the editor a decision, so each one must give a viewer something to feel or see: a hook in the first second, a beat nobody can see, a missing brand screen. Don't decorate lines that already play, and give a moment one mark, not three. A short, tight script may need 0–3 marks; a long one maybe 10–15. An empty list is fine.

The kinds:
- **camera**: a shot that adds tension or payoff (an extreme close-up to open on the problem, a push-in on the doubter, a close-up insert of the phone, a low angle for the win). Only where it changes how the moment feels. Use standard camera terms (close-up, push in, pull out, pan, track, orbit, low angle, overhead, handheld); write anything rarer as "term: what you see".
- **action**: a character movement that makes a line land or shows what the words describe ("He slides the phone across the table.").
- **reaction**: who reacts, the feeling and its reason, and how it shows, big enough to read on a phone, at the turns (the insult, the number, the reveal): "Tasha, stunned he did this for her: hand to her mouth, eyes welling", never only "jaw drops".
- **brand_screen**: every moment the brand block says needs a screen or the product and doesn't have one. Use the screen whose moments match (a loss gets the chart-down screen, a win the chart-up one) and say where it shows. If the script never shows a required screen, suggest one where it fits the story best.

Voiceover scripts: every sentence is spoken. Your marks show what each sentence is about, so the picture changes as fast as the words do.

**Notes** are for things only the editor can fix, because they are in the words: a spoken line that contradicts a brand fact (the bag says a different flavor), a ★ line naming a screen the brand doesn't have, a slow start before the conflict, a script that doesn't end on the brand's end line. Say what you see and why in one plain sentence; never propose replacement words.

Each `reason` is one plain sentence an editor understands at a glance: "Opens on the problem, so viewers stay", not film-school jargon.

Quotes must be copied exactly from the numbered line (without the "12| " prefix). If you can't quote it exactly, leave the suggestion out.
