## shots
- One shot per sentence, or per phrase when a sentence runs over about 2.5 s (Reed, 3 Nov).
- Nobody lip-syncs by default. A character may look into the lens at the turns and the offer (Reed, 3 Nov).

## measured from the reference videos
Measured on Reed's 2 reference voice-over ads, both drawn in one animated-feature look (notes: the style examples named pixth-*). Facts, not new rules.
- The voice: one warm, animated voice from the first frame to the end, covering 84–85% of the runtime, with no pause over 0.5–0.7 s (median pause 0.3–0.4 s). 3.4–3.7 words a second. First person (a week-by-week testimonial) or second person (a day-by-day diary).
- The bed: Music is added in post (Reed, 7 Nov): the site makes the video's own sound, the voices, the room and the effects, never a music bed, and a cut with none is right. In the references, a steady beat about 4 dB under the voice the whole way (one), or almost nothing for the first minute and then a soft pad (the other). Light whooshes and booms on some cuts. Mastered at −14.1 to −14.2 LUFS with a very even level (loudness range 1.7–2.9 LU).
- One picture per phrase or sentence: a median of 6 and 10 words per shot. Median shot 1.6 s and 2.7 s; 3.5–5.5 cuts per 10 s; longest hold 5.5–5.6 s (a mechanism world, the offer). Cuts land in the gaps between phrases or at word edges (59% and 86% of cuts).
- Quickest on symptoms acted out as physical comedy (1–1.5 s); slowest on mechanism worlds (4–5 s) and the offer.
- On camera: nobody lip-syncs. In one video the heroine looks into the lens for about 11 s in total (14% of the runtime), at the turning points and the offer, in a chest-up medium shot holding the product toward the camera. In the other, mirror close-ups bring her eyes toward the lens (0% talking).
- Chapters: one video marks days with a white flash and a day card every 15–25 s (added in editing).
