# Altar rejection: "Sorry, I'm fully booked"

A dialogue short. A groom is dumped at the altar for a richer man, grinds for two years, and
when his ex finds him in a penthouse surrounded by women he tells her he's fully booked.

## The format pattern (how the script reads)

A revenge glow-up in five beats, told almost entirely with pictures. Only 11 short lines.

1. **The wound, at its peak** (0 to 4.9 s): a public humiliation in two words. The first line
   is the setup ("I do"), the second destroys it ("HELL NO!"). Then the reason, in one line
   ("I'm marrying him!"), and the hero's one-word reaction ("What?!").
2. **Left alone** (4.9 to 9.3 s): no words. She leaves with the rival; he is alone in a wide
   shot; he picks up the ring.
3. **The vow turned into fuel** (9.3 to 17.4 s): one tender flashback line, then its last
   words echoed three times over his face. The echo is the turn from grief to resolve.
4. **The rise** (17.4 to 29.2 s): no words. Same pose, bigger world, faster and faster; ends
   on him alone, looking down on a city. Then a black time-jump card.
5. **The reversal and the payoff** (31.1 to 42.7 s): the ex sees him (his name, twice, then a
   shocked sound). He is now the one being chosen. His single comeback line closes the story.
6. **Tag** (42.7 to 45 s): the creator's real life (in this reel; a brand's end line in ours).

What carries it: the hero barely speaks. The other side does the talking until the very end,
so his one line at the end lands with all the weight.

## The style pattern (how it looks)

- **Look**: glossy 3D animation in a big-studio family-film style: big eyes, soft skin, clean
  shapes, expressive faces pushed to the edge (the scream, the jaw drop).
- **Light and colour**: bright warm daylight by the sea for the wedding; a hazy, overexposed
  warm glow for the flashback; dim desk lamps and cool blue warehouse light for the grind; deep
  blue night and purple party light for the penthouse. Colour tracks the mood.
- **Camera**: mostly locked off. Movement is saved for meaning: a quick push into the scream,
  a whip pan following her pointing arm, a handheld follow as she leaves, a tracking shot for
  his arrival, a snap push onto her shock, and one slow push in across the whole payoff.
- **Framing**: big faces (close-ups and extreme close-ups) for every emotion; low inserts for
  objects (ring on the floor, ring on the finger); wide, centred and symmetrical when he is
  alone.
- The last 2.4 s break the style on purpose: real, handheld phone footage.

## Why it works for a viewer

- **0 to 1 s**: the most painful thing that can happen at a wedding, at full volume, in the
  first second. No context needed to feel it.
- **1 to 5 s**: everyone reacts (groom, priest, guests), so the viewer reacts too; the ring
  bouncing on the floor makes the rejection physical.
- **5 to 17 s**: the pace drops and lets the hurt sit. The viewer now wants payback; the echo
  promises it's coming.
- **17 to 29 s**: wordless, fast and musical; the same pose in ever-bigger rooms shows two
  years of success without a word of explanation. Satisfying to watch even on mute.
- **29 to 34 s**: the time card and the ex's double take set up the reversal the viewer has
  been waiting for.
- **34 to 43 s**: the longest hold in the video goes to the payoff. One smug, quotable line.
- **43 to 45 s**: a quick, fast flex that ends abruptly, so the loop back to "I do" feels
  natural and many people watch twice.

## How the director recreates this from a new script

This is guidance for the house dialogue shorts. The script's words are said exactly; the
pictures below are how to stage them.

**The recipe, line to beat to picture**

| Script beat | What the line does | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| Opening exchange | A setup line answered by a brutal reversal | Open wide for under half a second, then smash to an extreme close-up of whoever delivers the blow, with a quick push in. Cut on every word or two for the first 2 to 3 s. |
| The insult lands | Silence or one-word reaction | Reaction close-ups of the hero and of bystanders; one object insert that makes the moment physical (thrown, dropped, torn), low and close. |
| The reason / the rival | One plain line naming why | Move the camera with the gesture (whip pan along the pointing arm). The rival gets a smug look, no line needed. |
| Left alone | No words | Hold wide and centred on the hero, alone. Let shots run 1.5 s or more. |
| Memory or vow | One tender line, then a few words of it echoed | Warm, hazy flashback with a white flash in and a dissolve out; echo the last words over a slow push on the hero's still face. |
| The rise | No words (or the brand's screens) | A montage at 0.2 to 0.5 s per shot: same pose, same framing, bigger world each cut (match cuts). End on one longer move that shows he has arrived. |
| Time jump | A short card | Plain black card, small text, under 2 s. |
| Reversal | The other side reacts | The ex or the doubter spots him: two-shot, then a snap push onto their shock. |
| Payoff | The hero's one comeback line | The longest hold in the video: start wide, slow push in all the way to his face as he says it. |
| End | The brand's end line | Short and fast, ends abruptly so it loops. |

**Rules of thumb it shows**

- Spend the first 2 seconds on the conflict, not the setup.
- Cut fast where no one speaks; hold where a line has to land.
- Give the hero few words; let the payoff line be his.
- Save camera moves for meaning; lock off the rest.
- Every emotion is a big face in close-up.

**What NOT to copy**

- The creator's real-life tag at the end (the CG-to-real morph and "BANDS > BRIDES"): it is
  this creator's own brand. In ours, the end is the brand's end line.
- The reselling business in the montage (laptop, camera, parcels, warehouses): it is the
  creator's story. In a fomo video the rise is told on fomo's screens.
- The exact characters, names and wedding: the structure carries over, the story does not.
- The look itself belongs to the chosen style, not to this format; the camera grammar above
  carries over to any style.
- The yellow caption look is this creator's; captions in ours follow the brand and style.
- The flash shot of the priest at 0.10 s works in an edit; a generated clip that short is
  rarely usable *(inferred)*: aim for 0.25 s and up.
