## measured in the reference videos
<!-- 3 Nov, study-house: format-level facts from the 10 House reference reels (the @nateassets dialogue skits; per-reel notes in styles/house/examples/*/notes.md), deep-watch kit. Facts about how the skits are built and sound, not their words. Reed's answers of 3 Nov applied. -->
- **The opening:** the first line is spoken at 0–0.3 s in all 10, in the middle of the conflict, with no establishing shot.
- **The shape, in the references:** a confrontation, a dare or a deadline, a wordless grind, a low point (about 7 of 10, mostly at night), the turn (a notification or a first sale), the result growing, a payoff that mirrors the opening's place and people, then a comic button on the doubter's face (8 of 10). The rejection version (2 of 10) opens on a public rejection and jumps time with a card. Every reference has at least one montage; a montage is still only for a story that needs one (craft.md).
- **How much is spoken:** speech covers about a quarter to a half of the running time (25–50%); the rest runs on music and effects. Lines are short, 1–5 words, fired back and forth; inside a line about 3.5–4.8 words a second; a reply often starts 0.05–0.2 s after the last line ends; pauses between lines have a median of 0.4–1.4 s.
- **Delivery:** shouted and very animated: the voice's pitch moves 6.9–9.3 semitones (10 of 10). The doubters overplay and the hero underplays the payoff line.
- **Cutting against the sound:** dialogue cuts land on lines, often in the gap just before the next one; montage cuts land on sound hits; no reel cuts to the musical beat (10 of 10 at chance).
- **The sound bed:** music under every second, no silence (10 of 10), about −14 LUFS overall. Under lines it is a soft pad or low drone 10–27 dB below the voice (8 of 10; in the other two it sits close to the voice), and it rises, with drums, whenever nobody speaks. Effects on every physical action and whip; a ding for each notification; low booms at time jumps.
- **The ending:** all 10 end on a 1.5–3.7 s live-action handheld tag (clips of 0.1–0.8 s, on its own louder, drum-heavy track). In the references it is the creator's own life, never to be copied.

## closing slot
<!-- 3 Nov, Reed's H8 answer (approved rec). -->
An optional closing slot for the brand's real thing: after the animation, about 1.5–3.7 s of the brand's own real footage, screens or product, cut fast. Use it when the brand has something real to show; leave it out when it doesn't.
