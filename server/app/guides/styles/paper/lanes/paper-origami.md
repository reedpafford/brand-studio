## sheet_look
Origami: a character folded from paper with clean geometric creases and crisp angular folds, pleated paper details, soft pastel-coloured paper; a simple folded papercraft face. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's; a teenager looks like a teenager, a child like a child. One fixed outfit of folded paper, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance. Everything is real physical paper, photographed: no vector art, no frame or board around it.

## place_look
Origami world: everything folded from paper with clean geometric creases, crisp angular folds on every object, pleated paper details, soft pastel papers (blush, sage, cream) with gentle contrast, soft diffused light casting gentle shadows that reveal the folds, the scene filling the entire frame.

## Stills
- State the camera's place inside the paper world in every frame ("at eye level with the paper character", "worm's-eye view up at the cardstock towers", "overhead, straight down on the paper street"). Never "diorama", "shadow box", "tabletop", "table", "board" or "frame": they show the craft from outside. If one does, "the paper shot fills the entire frame, the camera is inside the scene".
- Name the paper on each major object ("a bowl folded from blue card", "shelves layered from patterned card"), not only in the look. Say "papercraft" near faces and products, never "paper cut", which blurs features.
- Soft pastels (blush, sage, cream) with gentle contrast: the one paper look that is never saturated, so the fold shadows carry the frame. The pop-up unfold suits it best for a change between scenes.
