## Look
- **The man:** a long, lanky, clearly clay figure with a narrow stretched face, a long nose, big round bulging eyes with dark lids and bags, deep cheek hollows, a long neck, thin arms, oversized hands with long fingers; hair as sculpted chunky strands. His face is caricatured and goofy-sad, not idealized. Fingerprint and tool texture is visible on skin in close-ups. Clothes read as clay (a sculpted black tee, a grey suit) rather than real fabric. Cartoon expression devices are added in the scene: a circling ring of yellow clay stars when dazed, orange lightning bolts for stress, a single sweat drop.
- **Two other faces:** a woman in a pink top (same caricature style); and one near-photoreal bearded man (shot 25) who doesn't match.
- **Clay vs real:** people, bedding, numbers and the end-card finger are clay. Rooms, offices, city windows, phones, screens, espresso and some props are realistic, film-like CG. In two places a fully plain "everything is clay" set appears (shots 15 tan room, 37 plinth).
- **Light and colour:** mostly low key. Warm amber practicals (bedside lamps, desk lamps) against cold blue night windows; phone screens cast green or red on the face. Average colour #544737 across the video (sampled 1 frame a second; ffmpeg brightness 77/255, the darkest of the five; saturation 15). The "better morning" at the end is the only bright, sunny, golden passage (shots 31, 35–36, brightness 37–57, warmth 21–25).
- Depth of field: shallow in close-ups, deeper in rooms.

## On-screen text
- Burned-in captions (an editor's job; rule 32): small white sans in sentence case with a soft shadow, mid-frame (about 55–60% down), 1–6 words on one or two lines, building word by word as they're spoken (each new word fades in from grey to white). **The key word in each line is set in a white italic serif** (a mixed sans plus italic serif pairing). This is the most designed caption style of the five.
- In-world text: phone screens with large numbers, a digital clock, a label on a bottle, the product box.

## The style pattern
- A caricatured clay everyman in realistic, moody, film-lit spaces; cartoon devices (stars, bolts, sweat drop) sculpted into the scene; clay chapter cards; whip-pan montages; a sunlit dream-morning world for the payoff.
- The clay is strongest in faces, bedding, the countdown numbers and the end-card finger; the world around is mixed CG realism.

## Why it works
- The snoring close-up and the alarm sound land the problem in the first second.
- The countdown card is a recurring promise that something is about to change, and it breaks a nearly 3-minute video into chapters.
- The cold night and phone-glow versus the warm morning make the before and after physical.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- For a long second-person story: one caricatured clay character, low-key practical lighting, long holds for feelings, whip-pan montages for routines.
- Use a sculpted clay chapter card (numbers or a word) between sections, with a tick or hit for each beat.
- Save the bright, golden light for the better future.
- Don't copy: the alarm, the band, the countdown's exact numbers, the realistic stranger.
