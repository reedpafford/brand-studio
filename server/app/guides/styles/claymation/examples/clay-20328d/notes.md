## Look
- **The man (Cinematic, older-character variant):** a hand-sculpted plasticine man with a long face, deep forehead lines, crow's feet, eye bags, stubble dots, thick dark brows with tool marks, white sclera around brown irises; clumped sculpted hair with grey at the temples; a real grey knitted jumper with a collar showing. He is clearly lined and middle-aged: the guide's "unlined clay skin" does not describe him. From about 87 s on he drifts to a smoother, rounder, more 3D-animated face (shot 49) *(a consistency slip, not a choice)*.
- **The body world (Goofy creatures):** filters are balls of wound clay strands like yarn, with big cream googly eyes and small sculpted mouths, shown in crowds; waste blobs are glossy brown lumps with angry brows, yellow eyes and fangs; ingredient creatures have small cross or happy faces. Tissue walls are pink-to-maroon clay arches with red clay veins and **dense fingerprint whorls**; tubes are cream clay with a glossy liquid inside.
- **Liquids are glossy:** goo, fluid and drips are wet and shiny next to matte clay. The contrast is part of the look (matte clay, wet liquid).
- **Product:** the bottle reads as a real printed bottle (photographic label, real plastic), not sculpted, even in object-only shots, set on clay or stone plinths among clay plants and fruit.
- Real-looking hand: one insert shows a fairly realistic hand and sleeve flushing a clay toilet (shot 41).
- Sets: an ordinary home (hallway, cluttered kitchen, bedroom, living room) and a clinic, all densely dressed with tiny props (tins, jugs, papers, scales), against the inside-the-body caves.
- Light and colour, as story:
  - home at the start: cool blue-grey (#484f5a, warmth −7);

## The style pattern
- Two clay registers in one ad: a cinematic, slightly melancholy older man in real rooms, and a goofy creature world inside his body that explains what he can't see.
- Explanation by sculpted metaphor: organs as clay caves, filters as yarn-ball creatures, waste as grumpy blobs, ingredients as little workers.
- Colour carries the story: cold to warm, grey for dead, black-and-white for the old way, gold for fixed.
- Macro lens, shallow focus and crowds of identical little creatures make the inner world feel huge.

## Why it works
- The man's worried face gives the viewer someone to feel for; the creature world makes an invisible process watchable and funny.
- A new picture about every 2 s keeps a nearly 2-minute narration moving.
- The colour shifts tell you where you are in the story before the words do.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- For any explainer: one relatable Cinematic-lane person in ordinary rooms, cut against a goofy clay world showing the hidden mechanism as creatures and sculpted places.
- Cut on phrases, about every 1.5–3 s; keep macro and shallow focus inside the hidden world.
- Plan the colour arc: cold or grey for the problem, warm gold for the fix; one black-and-white beat is available for "the old way".
- Don't copy: the kidney anatomy, the yarn-ball filters, the specific ingredients, the toilet.
