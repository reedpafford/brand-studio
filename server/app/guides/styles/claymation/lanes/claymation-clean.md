## sheet_look
Clean, simple claymation: a character sculpted from smooth matte modeling clay in clean rounded forms with soft even surfaces and subtle tool marks, simple dot eyes, gently exaggerated cartoon proportions, chunky rounded clay hands with no fingernails. The body and face follow the character's age: an adult reads as an adult, a teenager as a teenager, a child as a child. One fixed outfit sculpted in clay, identical in every view, and one silhouette-defining item that reads at a glance.

## place_look
Clean, simple claymation: a tidy set sculpted from smooth matte modeling clay, clean rounded forms with soft even surfaces and subtle tool marks, a few well-chosen props, soft bright light with real cast shadows.

## still_look
The Clean Claymation look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Polished claymation scene — everything sculpted from smooth matte modeling clay, clean rounded forms with soft even surfaces and subtle tool marks, gently exaggerated cartoon proportions, refined handcrafted stop-motion feel, the scene filling the entire frame with the camera inside the clay world. Faces and builds come from the character sheets, and outfits too unless this video gives its own. Tidy compositions in soft bright light with real cast shadows."

## clip_style
Handmade claymation throughout: matte plasticine, subtle tool marks, gentle smooth motion with one action per shot, a calm camera. Every cartoon touch is sculpted in clay. Non CGI. Animation must start at the first frame

## fit_look
a clean, simple claymation look with rounded matte forms and dot eyes

## Stills
- Keep it tidy: one clear subject, few props, so the product out-details the world. The product is its real packaging, set on a clay plinth or held in clay hands, its label exactly as in its image.
- Name the clay on every object; glass and metal are "sculpted as a professional clay imitation of …"; liquids are glossy and wet against matte clay. Keep the subtle tool marks in every shot: a clay world with no trace of hands reads as computer animation.
- Keep the camera inside the clay world at a named angle; never show the set's edges or the workbench. Name the feeling, then the brows and mouth.

## Clips
- One gesture and one camera move per shot: stacked actions drift. Repeat the clay words in the action, so the material survives the motion.
- Calm transitions suit it: a push in from the scene to the product's label, or the scene kneads and reshapes into the next. Name the one visible action that carries one shot into the next.
