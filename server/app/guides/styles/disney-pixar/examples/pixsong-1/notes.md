## The format pattern (shape only)
A long musical drama in acts: betrayal at a party → loneliness → a rival's flashback → the low point → the discovery (mechanism and ingredients) → the turn (the band drops out) → the glow-up → the payoff at the party/bar → the product end card. The product enters late (about 160 s of 363 s).

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look on an adult human ensemble with exaggerated bodies and soap-opera acting; night-party colour (blue, magenta, festoon gold); extreme close-ups for emotion; quick cuts in the drama, slower in the turn.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- A kiss on the wrong man in the first second, and the heroine's face falling: instant drama.
- It plays like a short animated series: characters with clear silhouettes, a villain, a flashback, a glow-up.
- The song carries the story so the pictures can stay on faces.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Cast a small ensemble, each with one signature outfit and silhouette; lock them as sheets.
- Open on the most dramatic moment as an extreme close-up, cut to the reaction within a second.
- Cut fast in the drama (about 1.1–1.4 s), slow down to 3 s shots when the music drops for the turn.
- Show the problem on the body and the change as the same face transformed.
- Let a character mouth the odd sung line on camera (0.5–2 s); otherwise silent acting under the song.
