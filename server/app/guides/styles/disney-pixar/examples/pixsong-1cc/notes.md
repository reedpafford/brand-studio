## The format pattern (shape only)
Same as pixsong-1bb: the pet sings its symptoms → the owner ignores or misreads them (laptop, TV, phone) → a vet and a mentor → the inside view → what doesn't work (wipes and sprays) → the discovery on the phone → order, delivery, first chew → week markers → the bond → proof by other owners → end card with the pet holding the product.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look in soft peachy light; the animal is the lead and does big cartoon acting (tear sprays, howls); symptoms as coloured glows; the inside of the body shown through a window or fly-in, populated with cute germ and bacteria characters.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- A crying, howling puppy and an owner on a laptop: guilt and comedy in one frame.
- The song lets the pet "say" what owners feel bad about not noticing.
- Long holds give the discovery and the reunion time; quick runs keep the logistics brisk.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Lead with the character who suffers, doing one big readable cartoon action (a howl, a tear spray), and the person who doesn't notice in the same frame.
- Alternate quick montages (1–2 s) for logistics and 5–8 s holds for scenes with a feeling.
- Draw the symptom as a coloured glow under the skin or fur; heal it to a cool mint glow.
- Give the mechanism its own toy world of cute characters, entered through a window in the body.
- Don't force lip-sync: the pet's face acts the lyric; one wailing or mouth-open pose is enough.

## What NOT to copy
- The product, delivery box, phone shop page, back label, other owners as testimonials, the offer and end-card CTA: brand material.
- Captions, emojis, the banner and disclaimer are editing layers (rule 32).
- The lyrics: not studied here.
