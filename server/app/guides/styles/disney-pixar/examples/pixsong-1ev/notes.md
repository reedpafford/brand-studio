## The format pattern (shape only)
A sung saga in chapters, narrated by a witness: the humiliation (the punch) → the backstory (the rancher in his prime, the marriage) → the betrayal → the rancher at his lowest (alone, driving, packing) → a friend brings the fix → mechanism and ingredients → the change (fitter, admired) → the rival's comeuppance → the rancher strong again → the ingredients and the lesson.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look on a rugged western cast; honky-tonk amber, neon blue, prairie gold; low-key, dark grade; western genre shots; slow-motion physical comedy.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- A punch in the first two seconds, in slow motion on a squashed cartoon face: violent and funny at once.
- A genre (country song, western town) that matches its audience, so the ad feels like entertainment.
- One picture per line for nine minutes: the eye always has something new.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Pick a genre setting that suits the song's style and stay in it (props, light, wardrobe).
- Open on the most physical moment (a punch, a fall) as a slow-motion close-up.
- Hold one picture per sung line at 1.4–1.9 s and keep that rhythm all the way, even in long songs.
- Keep the hero in one outfit throughout so he reads instantly in every shot.

## What NOT to copy
- The product, bottle, herb names and the offer: brand material.
- Crude moments (the vomiting) and swearing in captions are this ad's tone, not the style's.
- The lyrics: not studied here.
