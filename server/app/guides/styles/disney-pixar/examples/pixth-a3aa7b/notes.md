## The format pattern (shape only)
A first-person before-and-after story told week by week. Hook with the product in hand and a question → each step names a time marker and a body change, shown as a literal picture (the body change on her, a symptom acted out, or a mascot world) → a big emotional payoff (mirror, party) → proof pictures (factory, sold-out shelves) → the offer, to camera, with the product → a final to-camera close in fresh daylight.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look throughout: one heroine with sculpted braids and glossy eyes in warm, softly lit, lived-in homes; mechanism shown as toy-like mascot worlds with glowing characters in dark voids; symptoms acted out as big physical comedy; changes shown as in-shot transformations with a shimmer.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- The first frame already shows the product, the heroine and her problem (the belly) in one low-angle picture: nothing to decode.
- Every line gets its own picture within about 1.6 s, so the eye always has something new.
- The symptoms are funny, not clinical (racing to the bathroom, flattening against the door), which makes an embarrassing topic watchable.
- The mascot worlds turn an invisible mechanism into a little film with heroes and villains.
- The change is shown on her, in one continuous shot (belly shrinking, face de-puffing), which feels like proof.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- One heroine, locked as a sheet, in a "before" outfit and an "after" outfit; the change shown on her body in a continuous transformation shot with a soft shimmer.
- A picture for every phrase: 1–2.5 s shots, median about 1.6 s; give the mechanism its own toy world (mascots, glowing particles, a dark teal or violet void) and let those shots run longer (3–5 s).
- Act out every symptom as a big readable moment of physical comedy.
- Warm amber light by default; go cool and desaturated only for a "dull, stuck" beat; go hot and saturated for the mechanism.
- Voice-over carries the words; let her look into the lens at turning points and for the offer, without needing true lip-sync.
- Mostly push-ins; whip pans or a shimmer for big turns; one dissolve at most.

## What NOT to copy
- The product, the pouch, the brand's name on the scale and phone, the flag-and-factory proof beat, the delivery man and the sold-out shelves: they belong to this brand's claims.
- The captions are an editing layer: never burned into generated clips (rule 32).
- The script's words: not studied here (look and feel only).
