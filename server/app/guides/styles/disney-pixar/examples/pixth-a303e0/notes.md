## The format pattern (shape only)
A diary in days. Hook on the symptom at the mirror → Day 1 doubt → each day a small improvement, seen at the same mirror and the same bed → the moment others notice → "it wasn't what you thought" turns, each a picture with an icon → the gentle mechanism → social proof as a picture → the offer over calm product inserts.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look with a soft, cosy grade: one blonde heroine with huge blue eyes in three repeating home sets, night blue vs morning gold, symptoms drawn as glowing lines on her skin, slow push-ins on nearly every shot.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- The first frame is the viewer's own morning: a worried face at the mirror with the problem lit up on her skin.
- Repeating the same mirror and bed makes each day's change easy to see: the lines glow less each time.
- The day counter promises a payoff and shows progress, which keeps people watching a 2:46 video.
- Second-person voice-over plus near-to-lens mirror shots: it feels personal without anyone talking on camera.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- One heroine, one outfit, two or three home sets that recur; vary the light by time of day (cool blue night, warm gold morning).
- Draw the symptom as light on the body (a glow on the skin, fading as it heals) rather than clinically.
- One picture per sentence, 2–3.5 s each, a slow push in on most; let a split-frame before/after hold 2.5–4 s.
- Chapter the story (days or weeks) and hand the editor the markers; the white flash and the "DAY N" title are editing, not footage.
- Keep music very low or absent while the story sets up; bring a soft bed in for the second half.
