## The format pattern (shape only)
A first-person song from the pet: hook with the grossest symptom → each symptom gets its own picture and sound-word → owner misreads it (night, movie up) → a mentor explains → mechanism fly-through → villains (what doesn't work) → the fix arrives → the change and the bond (hug, sleeping together) → proof → end card with the pet and product.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look at full warmth and saturation; animals as the leads with human acting; symptoms as green clouds and glows; fast rush-ins and zoom-blur whips; a glossy cartoon world inside the body.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- A paw in your face and a green explosion in the first second: gross, funny, impossible to scroll past.
- The pet sings it, so the viewer hears the problem from the one who can't talk.
- Every sung line has a matching picture; the sound-words make the symptom land with the sound off.
- The emotional turn (owner sorry, hug, sleeping together) is held longer, on a breakdown in the music.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Pick a lead who can't normally speak (the pet, the body part, the product) and let the song be theirs.
- Hook: the worst symptom pushed into the lens with a visible effect (cloud, glow), then a whip to the human's reaction.
- One shot per one or two sung lines, 2–4 s; hold 5–7 s on the explanation and the emotional turn.
- Show the mechanism as a fly-through into a glossy, cute inside-the-body world, entered through an X-ray glow or a zoom-blur whip.
- Make competing fixes into small villain characters.
- Warm amber everything; blue only for night.
