## The format pattern (shape only)
A storytime song in the first person: headline hook at the party → years of being taken for granted (hosting, shifts) → overhearing the betrayal → the low point → the sister brings the fix → mechanism → weeks pass (title cards) → the glow-up and the comeback dinner → the son's look → she sings to the camera: the lesson and the offer.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look on an adult human cast; night turquoise and lamp gold; slow push-ins, backlit silhouettes and macro inserts; ends in a centred, lip-synced to-camera close.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- The headline plus a hurt face at a party: the viewer knows the genre at once.
- Every lyric line has its own literal picture, so the story reads with the sound off (captions help).
- Ending on her singing to us turns the story into advice from a friend.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Hand the editor a headline for the opening caption box; open on a busy wide scene with the heroine apart from it, then her face.
- One picture per lyric line, 1–2.5 s, slow pushes.
- Bring the fix through a mentor character; mark time with a title card.
- Save the on-camera, lip-synced singing for the last 30–45 s: a seated medium shot, centred, soft window light, cut with B-roll in the same outfit.

## What NOT to copy
- The product, pouch, sister's handover, clinic and the offer: brand material.
- Captions, the headline box and title cards are editing layers (rule 32).
- The lyrics and the story's specifics: not studied here.
