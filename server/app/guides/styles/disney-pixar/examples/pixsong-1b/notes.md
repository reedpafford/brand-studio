## The format pattern (shape only)
A storytime confession sung in the first person: a headline hook → the betrayal shown on phones → the discovery at night → the low point → the mother as mentor → the hidden cause (mechanism) → the ingredients → the turn (the band almost silent, a swirl into a memory) → weeks of change on her face → walking away → the product and proof → the new her.

## The style pattern
Disney-Pixar look on an adult human cast; phone-lit faces in deep blue; phone screens drawn in the style; sunset and cream warmth for the good stretches; slow push-ins.

## Why it works (for a viewer)
- A red headline and a stolen photo in the first two seconds: a drama people already know how to watch.
- Phone screens make the betrayal concrete and readable with the sound off.
- The product arrives as the mother's advice, inside the story, at about the halfway mark.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Lead with the headline moment as action (a phone raised, a photo taken), and hand the editor a headline for a title bar.
- Use phone screens and phone-lit faces as the visual language of discovery.
- Cast a mentor figure (here the mother) to bring the fix.
- Slow down (3–4 s shots) for the turn and the healing; quick (1.5 s) for the setup.

## What NOT to copy
- The product, pouches, warehouse, social posts and links: brand material.
- The red title bar, captions and highlight are editing layers (rule 32), and any text on phone screens should be written into the scene, not burned on top.
- The lyrics and the story's specifics: not studied here.
