## The style pattern
- Three picture types, cut together: talker full frame in her real workplace; talker cut out over literal or metaphor B-roll; other real-looking people talking to their phones.
- B-roll is a mix of everyday phone-shot scenes, close-up "metaphor" demos (objects that stand in for the problem), top-down ingredient flat-lays, and glossy 3D anatomy renders. No common grade: each picture keeps its own colour (brightness 24–69, warmth −16 to +25).
- Landscape B-roll is used in a band layout with a blurred copy above and below *(seen at 0–3 s and 40 s)*.

## Why it works
- The uniform (scrubs) and the real clinic say "expert" in the first second; the cut-out keeps that face on screen while the pictures explain.
- Metaphor B-roll makes a sensitive subject showable and memorable.
- The other-talkers run feels like social proof because it looks like different phones, rooms and caption styles.

## How the director recreates this look from a new script
- One main talker with a role-signalling outfit in a real, specific workplace, filmed waist-up on a still phone, eyes to lens.
- Open with a composite: an arresting literal or metaphor picture with the talker cut out over it.
- Middle: composites, one B-roll picture per phrase (1–3 s each).
- End: talker full frame, holding the product, jump-cutting on phrases.
- Optional: 3–5 short "other people" selfie clips, each in its own room and light.
- Don't copy: the subject's metaphors, the product, this talker's look.
