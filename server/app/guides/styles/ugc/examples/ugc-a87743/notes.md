## The style pattern
- Talker full frame, very close, in his own real room; cutaways to full-frame B-roll.
- B-roll is mostly one family: glossy, translucent 3D medical renders of the organ the script is about, on a soft grey studio background, getting closer as the explanation goes deeper. Around it, phone-shot people (before and after moods) and product-in-hand shots.
- Opening with split screens of body close-ups is a hook device, not repeated.

## Why it works
- The split-screen hook shows the viewer's own symptoms twice over before anyone speaks to them.
- The persistent red headline keeps the promise on screen for 3.5 minutes, so anyone who joins late knows what it's about.
- The renders make an invisible process concrete and almost beautiful; the long talker holds carry the trust.

## How the director recreates this look from a new script
- One talker take, selfie-close, in a real room that says who they are; eyes to lens; urgent, unbroken delivery.
- Hook: 2–3 split-screen pairs of phone-shot close-ups of the problem, about 1.4 s each.
- Middle: alternate long talker holds (5–15 s) with runs of 3D renders of the mechanism (2–7 s each, slow push-ins, getting closer).
- End: product in hand (a selfie testimonial and a hand-held shot), then a long talker close.
- Editor: one persistent headline banner plus phrase captions above it; picture-in-picture pops for named objects.
- Don't copy: the organ, the product, the headline's words, this man's look.
