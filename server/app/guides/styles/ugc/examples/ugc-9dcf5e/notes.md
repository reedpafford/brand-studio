## The style pattern
- Two picture types cut back and forth: one talker in a real, topic-matched room, and full-frame B-roll.
- B-roll mixes phone-shot everyday people (before and after moods, often handheld selfies), glossy 3D anatomy renders (clear bodies, glowing organs), scientific-looking footage (endoscope, microscope), and ingredient still lifes on wood and stone. No shared grade: B-roll saturation runs 7–74, warmth −12 to +34.
- Cuts are all hard; no flashes or whips.

## Why it works
- The talker's room is the credibility (shelves of jars say "he makes this stuff"), and the lean-in framing feels like a private tip.
- The face comes back every 3–5 s in the first half, so the long explainer runs later still feel anchored.
- Calm delivery plus graphic B-roll (worms, guts) gives contrast: the pictures shock, the voice reassures.

## How the director recreates this look from a new script
- One talker take in a real room that matches the subject, chest-up, phone on a stand, eyes to lens, leaning in.
- Cut to B-roll on every showable phrase (1–3 s), return to the talker for opinions and turns (2–8 s).
- Allow longer B-roll-only runs (up to about 20 s) when the script explains a process.
- Hook option: a second person cut out over the first for the opening line.
- Don't copy: the subject's pictures (worms, guts), the shop page, this man's look.
