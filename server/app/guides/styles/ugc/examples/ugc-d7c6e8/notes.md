## The style pattern
- Two layers, always: B-roll background (full frame) + talker cut-out (bottom-left) + caption box between them.
- B-roll mixes three looks with no common grade: phone-shot everyday people and hands (handheld, natural window light), glossy 3D medical renders (warm pinks, golden goo, glowing green), and clean product heroes in warm natural settings (garden, kitchen, wooden tables, soft bokeh). Per-shot brightness runs 20–81 and warmth −8 to +26: each picture keeps its own colour.
- The B-roll shows exactly what the line says, literally, one idea per picture.
- Transitions are editor effects, used about every third cut: white or yellow flash, whip or zoom blur, white line wipe, a red tint flash. The rest are hard cuts.

## Why it works
- The face never leaves, so trust and voice stay constant while the background changes every 1–2 s, which keeps the eye busy for 86 s.
- Literal pictures make every claim concrete in under a second.
- The caption sits in the eye line between face and picture, so it's read without looking away.

## How the director recreates this look from a new script
- Make **one** talker take for the whole script (chest-up, eye-level, looking into the lens, plain clothes, soft frontal light, plain backdrop that is easy to remove).
- Write one B-roll picture per phrase: a literal picture of what's said. Mix phone-shot people and hands, 3D inside-the-body renders when the script explains a mechanism, and product heroes.
- B-roll clips can be short and cheap: 1–3 s each, gentle push or handheld drift.
- In the edit: cut the talker out, place him bottom-left at 40–55% width, add the white-box phrase captions above his head, add a flash or whip on roughly every third cut, a quiet music bed that changes at the turn.
- Don't copy: the product, the offer cards, the guarantee seal, this man's look.
