## sheet_look
Stylised 3D render, one fixed look identical in every view. A host or doctor character (the description says so) is a cartoon doctor with a matte clay, vinyl-toy finish and no skin texture: a long head, a big rounded nose, prominent ears, thick dark slanted brows, small round white eyes with dark pupils, a wide mouth, chunky fingers; a white knee-length lab coat with notched lapels and pockets over a light blue collared shirt and a red tie, brown trousers and brown lace-up shoes; full body, standing, on a plain backdrop. A skeleton character (the description says so) is our Skeleton figure: a full anatomical skeleton with natural adult human proportions, tall and lanky, smooth ivory-cream bones with realistic bone detail (not toy-smooth, not chibi, not scary), and large expressive cartoon eyes with white sclera and dark pupils set in the eye sockets: an emotive, lovable face; its bones are bare, with no outfit, unless the description dresses it, and then it wears that one outfit with the skull and hands still showing. When the description says x-ray, it is instead a translucent glowing anatomical human body revealing the full white skeleton plus visible internal organs (heart, lungs, intestines) glowing red and orange through a blue-tinted translucent skin outline, with large expressive cartoon eyes. Anyone else is a stylised 3D human whose body and face follow their age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's.

## place_look
Glossy 3D medical-illustration render of either a seamless studio backdrop in a saturated blue gradient (deep navy at the top to mid blue, a soft top light), or a real, lived-in room in warm golden-hour light with photoreal detail, or an inside-the-body scene (glossy tissue, vessels, translucent x-ray skin, glowing particles on a dark navy field). Vertical, with the bottom corners kept clear.

## still_look
The Dr. Pickle look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Glossy 3D medical-illustration render, vertical. The picture shows the line literally, as it happens: the skeleton from its sheet acting it out in a warm, lived-in real room or on a saturated blue studio backdrop, or an inside-the-body cutaway (an x-ray body, organs, vessels, glowing particles) where the body visibly changes. Green or gold glow means good; orange-red glow, grey smoke or black-and-white means bad. The subject sits in the upper two-thirds; the bottom corners stay clear for the doctor, who is his own layer over the picture and is never drawn into it."

## clip_style
Glossy 3D medical-illustration animation, the background acting out the line as it is said (the body changes on screen), slow push-in or pull-out, the same cartoon-eyed skeleton in every shot, saturated blue or warm golden light, bottom corners clear

## fit_look
a stylised 3D look (the doctor is a matte clay-toy cartoon in a white lab coat, light blue shirt and red tie; a skeleton character is an ivory cartoon skeleton with big expressive eyes, in its one outfit when it has one)

## The host
- Reed: "It is always a doctor. The name is just a name."
- The doctor is one continuous layer over full-frame B-roll for the whole video: the B-roll cuts under him every couple of seconds and he is never cut. In the references his pose and lip-sync run straight across every background cut.
- He is framed from the waist up, standing at the bottom edge in a bottom corner, at a fixed size of about 38 % of the frame's height (the references: 35–45 %). He keeps to one side, or steps to the side that's empty so he never covers the action.
- He performs with his face and hands: lip-sync on every word, blinks, small head bobs and sways; eyebrows up and mouth wide on the punchlines, deadpan looks, an open palm toward the background ("look at this"), a finger up, a hand on his chin or belly, palms out. He never walks or leaves his spot.
- He looks lit for himself (soft, even light on a matte finish); he isn't matched to the background's light and casts no shadow on it.
- In this style only, the doctor is a second cartoon element beside the skeleton.

## Stills
- Every still is B-roll: the doctor is never in it. Keep the subject in the upper two-thirds and the bottom corners free of anything that matters.
- The picture is the proof of the line, at the moment it's said: if the line says fat melts, skin tightens, a gut is clogged or fog lifts, show exactly that happening to the body.
- One recurring skeleton per video, from its sheet, the same face and the same outfit or accessory in every shot. Give it two kinds of place: the saturated blue studio backdrop for explanations and transformations, and warm, lived-in real rooms (couch, bed, kitchen, bathroom, gym, office, store) for life scenes; messy and slumped for "before", tidy and bright for "after".
- Inside-the-body cutaways for every "inside you" line: x-ray bodies on navy, organs, vessels, nerves, cells, glowing particles.
- Gross is allowed in this style (worms in a gut, goo, toilets, rotting food in a belly), never blood or wounds.
- Glow carries the meaning: green or gold glow and light for good; orange-red glow, angry blobs, green gas or grey smoke for bad; black-and-white with grain for the bad outcome; a burst of light for the fix.
- Useful devices from the references: a split before/after panel, a 2x2 grid of the figure, a turntable shot of the figure on the blue backdrop with its problem zones glowing.

## Clips
- One background clip per line, silent: the doctor's voice carries the video.
- The change happens inside the shot, while the line is said: the body transforms on screen, not between shots.

## camera
- The B-roll camera moves slowly on almost every shot: a slow push-in by default (2–10 % of the frame a second); a pull-out to reveal a change; a slow tilt to travel a body from head to feet. One camera move per shot.
- Enter the body with a fast fly-through push (20–63 % of the frame a second).
- Life scenes can be locked off with only the figure moving.
- The doctor's framing never moves: the same waist-up size and spot for the whole video, no camera move on him.

## Script
- Reed: "Dr pickle infamously is very blunt and cusses a lot - this is very important."
- How much he swears is the script's call; no bleep by default.
- In the references one voice, the doctor's, says everything, from the first frame to the last half-second, at 3.1–4.3 words a second with only short pauses. Three of five swear 4–6 times a minute, often in the very first line.
- A script that already exists keeps every line word for word.

## Sound
- One fixed voice for the doctor: male and deep (median pitch 91–99 Hz, 4 of 5 references), with a wide swing (9–10 semitones between its low and high) and either a non-stop rant (3–12 pauses over a quarter-second in two minutes) or punchlines with short hard stops (30–42 such pauses, median 0.3–0.4 s).
- No music by default (none in 3 of 5; a quiet bed 17–20 LU under the voice in 2). One voice, mixed loud (about −14 LUFS in 3 of 5), no risers, whooshes or hits.

## pacing
Measured on the five reference videos (examples/*/notes.md):
- Shot length (the B-roll): median 2.0–2.4 s (5 of 5); shortest 0.2–1.1 s; longest hold 4.8–6.1 s (4 of 5), up to 9.9 s for a slow tilt or push on an explainer (1 of 5).
- Cuts per 10 s: 3.4–4.2 (5 of 5).
- Hook: the voice starts at 0.0 s (5 of 5). The first cut lands at 0.8–2.4 s (3 of 5), or the opener is one continuous moving shot of 4.2–5.6 s (a slow tilt or a turntable, 2 of 5). The figure is big in frame showing the problem, and the doctor is there from the first frame.
- Cuts follow the line's meaning, landing on or within a tenth of a second of a word edge (median 0.03–0.12 s, 5 of 5), not on a beat.
- By segment: life scene 1.5–3.5 s (up to 6 s locked off); inside-the-body cutaway 1–2 s with fast fly-throughs, or a slow 4–10 s explainer; transformation on the blue backdrop 2.8–5.4 s; a quick "result" run of 3–5 shots at 0.7–1.2 s; product beats 2–4 s.
- The doctor's layer never cuts (5 of 5).

## must keep
- The doctor in a bottom corner of every shot, one continuous layer, never cut (5 of 5).
- His design: matte clay-toy 3D doctor, long head, big nose, heavy brows, small round eyes; white lab coat, light blue shirt, red tie (5 of 5).
- He performs with face and hands only, and reacts big on the punchlines (5 of 5).
- The background shows the line literally, as it's said, and the body changes on screen (5 of 5).
- One recurring skeleton per video with its one outfit or accessory (a recurring skull-faced figure in 5 of 5).
- Glossy 3D medical-illustration cutaways inside the body (5 of 5), with glow as meaning (5 of 5).
- Two kinds of place: a saturated blue studio backdrop (4 of 5) and warm, lived-in real rooms (4 of 5).
- Blunt, non-stop delivery from the first frame, in one fixed deep voice (5 of 5).
- About 2 s background shots with a slow camera (5 of 5).

## safe to vary
- The doctor's skin tone and hair (light with slicked brown hair in 4; dark brown with short twists in 1) and his side of the frame.
- Which Skeleton figure plays the background: the bare cartoon skeleton, dressed in one outfit, or the x-ray body. Outfit and accessory follow the story (a sweatband in 3 of 5).
- How much of the video is on the blue backdrop versus in real rooms (one is almost all navy; one mostly warm rooms).
- How much he swears (the script decides).
- Split panels, grids and a three-panel end card.
