## sheet_look
Stylised 3D render, one fixed look identical in every view. A host or doctor character (the description says so) is a cartoon doctor with a matte clay, vinyl-toy finish and no skin texture: a long head, a big rounded nose, prominent ears, thick dark slanted brows, small round white eyes with dark pupils, a wide mouth, chunky fingers; a white knee-length lab coat with notched lapels and pockets over a light blue collared shirt and a red tie, brown trousers and brown lace-up shoes; full body, standing, on a plain backdrop. An anatomy character (the description says so) is a glossy 3D medical-illustration human body whose face is a bare ivory skull with large cartoon eyeballs in the sockets, its body showing smooth red-brown anatomy-model muscle, or a clear glass skin over an ivory skeleton, as the description says, with one signature outfit or accessory kept in every view. Anyone else is a stylised 3D human whose body and face follow their age: an adult has an adult's height, build and face, never a child's.

## place_look
Glossy 3D medical-illustration render of either a seamless studio backdrop in a saturated blue gradient (deep navy at the top to mid blue, a soft top light), or a real, lived-in room in warm golden-hour light with photoreal detail, or an inside-the-body scene (glossy tissue, vessels, translucent x-ray skin, glowing particles on a dark navy field). Vertical, with the bottom corners kept clear.

## still_look
The Dr. Pickle look, unless the brand block says otherwise: "Glossy 3D medical-illustration render, vertical. The picture shows the line literally, as it happens: the anatomy character from its sheet acting it out in a warm, lived-in real room or on a saturated blue studio backdrop, or an inside-the-body cutaway (an x-ray body, organs, vessels, glowing particles) where the body visibly changes. Green or gold glow means good; orange-red glow, grey smoke or black-and-white means bad. The subject sits in the upper two-thirds; the bottom corners stay clear for the doctor, who is his own layer over the picture and is never drawn into it."

## clip_style
Glossy 3D medical-illustration animation, the background acting out the line as it is said (the body changes on screen), slow push-in or pull-out, the same skull-faced anatomy character in every shot, saturated blue or warm golden light, bottom corners clear

## fit_look
a stylised 3D look (the doctor is a matte clay-toy cartoon in a white lab coat, light blue shirt and red tie; an anatomy character is a skull-faced medical-illustration body with its one outfit)

## The host
- The doctor is one continuous layer over full-frame B-roll for the whole video: the B-roll cuts under him every couple of seconds and he is never cut. In the references his pose and lip-sync run straight across every background cut.
- He stands at the bottom edge in a bottom corner, at a fixed size (about 35–45 % of the frame's height), cropped by the frame edge anywhere from his shoes to his waist. He keeps to one side, or steps to the side that's empty so he never covers the action.
- He performs with his face and hands, never his feet: lip-sync on every word, blinks, small head bobs and sways; eyebrows up and mouth wide on the punchlines, deadpan looks, an open palm toward the background ("look at this"), a finger up, a hand on his chin or belly, palms out. He never walks or leaves his spot.
- He looks lit for himself (soft, even light on a matte finish); he isn't matched to the background's light and casts no shadow on it.

## Stills
- Every still is B-roll: the doctor is never in it. Keep the subject in the upper two-thirds and the bottom corners free of anything that matters.
- The picture is the proof of the line, at the moment it's said: if the line says fat melts, skin tightens, a gut is clogged or fog lifts, show exactly that happening to the body.
- One recurring anatomy character per video, the same skull face, cartoon eyes and signature accessory in every shot. Give it two kinds of place: the saturated blue studio backdrop for explanations and transformations, and warm, lived-in real rooms (couch, bed, kitchen, bathroom, gym, office, store) for life scenes; messy and slumped for "before", tidy and bright for "after".
- Inside-the-body cutaways for every "inside you" line: x-ray bodies on navy, organs, vessels, nerves, cells, glowing particles.
- Glow carries the meaning: green or gold glow and light for good; orange-red glow, angry blobs, green gas or grey smoke for bad; black-and-white with grain for the bad outcome; a burst of light for the fix.
- Useful devices from the references: a split before/after panel, a 2x2 grid of the figure, a turntable shot of the figure on the blue backdrop with its problem zones glowing.

## Clips
- One background clip per line, silent: the doctor's voice carries the video.
- The camera moves slowly on almost every shot (a push-in by default; a pull-out to reveal a change; a slow tilt to travel a body from head to feet). Enter the body with a fast fly-through push. Life scenes can be locked off with only the figure moving.
- The change happens inside the shot, while the line is said: the body transforms on screen, not between shots.

## Script
- Reed: "Dr pickle infamously is very blunt and cusses a lot - this is very important."
- In the references one voice, the doctor's, says everything, from the first frame to the last half-second, at 3.1–4.3 words a second with only short pauses. Four of six swear 4–7 times a minute, often in the very first line.
- A script that already exists keeps every line word for word.

## On-screen text
- Captions are added in editing (never in the clips): one or two words at a time (up to four), popping in with a quick scale-up, centred a little below the middle (55–68 % of the frame's height), above the doctor's head. In the references the default face is a white heavy rounded sans with a soft black shadow, and swears are always printed in full, even when the sound bleeps them.
- For a ladder script, a small top-left tag (white heavy capitals on a black rounded box) can show the rung (day, week, month).

## Sound
- In the references: one voice, mixed loud (about −14 LUFS in 4 of 6), no risers, whooshes or hits. No music in 3 of 6; a quiet bed 17–24 LU under the voice in the other 3. When a swear is bleeped (1 of 6), it's a pure 1 kHz tone of 0.2 s with the voice cut out under it.
- The voice is male, deep in 4 of 6 (median pitch 91–99 Hz), with a wide swing (9–10 semitones between its low and high) and either a non-stop rant (3–12 pauses over a quarter-second in two minutes) or punchlines with short hard stops (30–42 such pauses, median 0.3–0.4 s).

## pacing
Measured on the six reference videos (examples/*/notes.md):
- Shot length (the B-roll): median 1.6–2.4 s (6 of 6); shortest 0.2–1.1 s; longest hold 4.8–6.1 s (5 of 6), up to 9.9 s for a slow tilt or push on an explainer (1 of 6).
- Cuts per 10 s: 3.4–4.2 (5 of 6); 5.6 in the one found-footage collage.
- Hook: the voice starts at 0.0 s (6 of 6). The first cut lands at 0.8–2.4 s (4 of 6), or the opener is one continuous moving shot of 4.2–5.6 s (a slow tilt or a turntable, 2 of 6). The anatomy figure is big in frame showing the problem, and the doctor is there from the first frame.
- Cuts follow the line's meaning, landing on or within a tenth of a second of a word edge (median 0.03–0.12 s, 6 of 6), not on a beat.
- By segment: life scene 1.5–3.5 s (up to 6 s locked off); inside-the-body cutaway 1–2 s with fast fly-throughs, or a slow 4–10 s explainer; transformation on the blue backdrop 2.8–5.4 s; a quick "result" run of 3–5 shots at 0.7–1.2 s; product beats 2–4 s; a list-number card 0.6–0.8 s (1 of 6).
- The doctor's layer never cuts (6 of 6).
- Camera speed: slow pushes and pulls of 2–10 % of the frame a second on most shots; fly-through pushes into the body 20–63 % a second.

## must keep
- The doctor in a bottom corner of every shot, one continuous layer, never cut (6 of 6; in the found-footage one he's in about a quarter of the shots).
- His design family: matte clay-toy 3D doctor, long head, big nose, heavy brows, small round eyes; white lab coat, light blue shirt, red tie (5 of 6; the sixth has a glossier doctor in the same coat and shirt).
- He performs with face and hands only, and reacts big on the punchlines (6 of 6).
- The background shows the line literally, as it's said, and the body changes on screen (6 of 6).
- One recurring skull-faced anatomy figure per video with its one accessory or outfit (5 of 6).
- Glossy 3D medical-illustration cutaways inside the body (6 of 6), with glow as meaning (6 of 6).
- Two kinds of place: a saturated blue studio backdrop (5 of 6) and warm, lived-in real rooms (5 of 6).
- Blunt, non-stop delivery from the first frame (6 of 6), and swearing (4 of 6), printed in full in the captions.
- About 2 s background shots with a slow camera (6 of 6).

## safe to vary
- The doctor's skin tone and hair (light with slicked brown hair in 4; dark brown with short twists in 1), his side of the frame and his crop (shoes to waist).
- The anatomy figure: a muscle-anatomy man, a goo-covered man, a sweaty man whose skin shows the muscle under it, a glass-body skeleton, a skull-faced woman. Gender, build, outfit and accessory follow the story (a sweatband in 3 of 6).
- How much of the video is on the blue backdrop versus in real rooms (one is almost all navy; one mostly warm rooms).
- Music: none, or a quiet bed far under the voice (3 and 3).
- Bleeping the strongest swear (1 of 6) or leaving all swears clean (5 of 6).
- The caption face: white rounded heavy sans with shadow (3 of 6), condensed capitals with an outline, white bold italic, or black on a yellow box (1 each).
- Labels for a ladder (day, week, month) or numbers for a list; split panels, grids and a three-panel end card.
