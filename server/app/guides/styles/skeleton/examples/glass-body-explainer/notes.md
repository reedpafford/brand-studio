## Zoom-in behaviour (what Reed asked about)
- **How often:** 32 of 50 shots (64%) push in, covering 62% of the runtime. The per-second timeline shows 74 of 127 seconds (58%) with zoom above 1.5 %/s, against 16 seconds pulling out. A new push starts every 2.8 s (median), or **2.5 push-ins per 10 s**. Pushes come in runs: consecutive pushing shots run 2, 4, 1, 1, 1, **7**, 3, 3, 4, 2, 4 long.
- **Two speeds:**
  - **Slow drift** (21 shots, 44% of the time): median **3.3 %/s**, range 1.7–10.6 %/s. That is roughly 1.07–1.15x over a 2–4 s shot. It is steady from the first frame to the last, with no ease (my meter shows even steps of about 0.5–1.5% per 0.2 s). It runs under almost every explanation shot: the torso diagrams, the liver, the pack shot, the face at the end.
  - **Fast fly-in** (11 shots, 18% of the time): median **25.9 %/s**, range about 20–67 %/s (the fastest is shot 21 at 67 %/s). These shots end 1.5–1.9x tighter than they start, inside 1.1–2.5 s. They often speed up (shot 3: 1.5 → 8 % per 0.2 s). Shot 1's "push-fast" tag is subject motion, not zoom.
- **On what:** always the thing being named, inside the body: the liver as it is named (3, 15, 23), the ducts and sac (15–17), stones (18–19), the fat-cell tissue (21), the skin layers (22), the belly fat (2, 13). It is never the face, except the final cartoon man (47). The product only gets a slow 2 %/s drift (49).
- **The deep-dive chain:** at 31–45 s the edit strings 9 shots together (15–23), each pushing or flying one level deeper: body → organ → duct → sac → stones → tissue → skin layers → back out to the body. Every link is a hard cut on a word. Fly-ins end in a macro, inside-the-body shot, and the next shot starts on that macro. The chain is the video's "zoom in on what's being said" moment.
- **Camera-made or edit-made:** almost all the zooms are in the render. Parallax changes during them (in 17 and 21 the foreground fat balls and cell walls slide past the background, and 22 descends through layers), so these are generated camera moves *(inferred from parallax; the low resolution hides crop softening)*. One zoom is clearly made in the edit: shot 38's split-screen panel scales up to fill the frame. The captions keep the same size and position through every zoom, so they are an overlay.
- **Timing against words and beats:** 78% of cuts land within 0.1 s of a word starting, against 58% for random times. 47% land within 0.1 s of a beat, against 33% for random times. 17 of 49 cuts fall mid-word. So cuts follow the voice more than the music, but they don't wait for sentence ends. Pushes begin at the cut, so a push "lands" when its shot starts on the word that names the organ *(inferred from the strips: the organ word appears 0–0.4 s after the cut in shots 3, 15, 23)*.

## Style pattern
- **Look:** clean, glossy 3D medical-explainer renders. A translucent "glass" human body (a clear shell outline over ivory-cream bones) with oversized, toy-glossy organs. The liver and fat are gold, yellow or amber, sometimes molten, beaded or metallic. The macro inserts are hyper-glossy and wet-looking (spheres, crystals, tunnels). There is no gore: everything reads as gold beads and syrup.
- **Character:** there is no single recurring character. At least six bodies appear: a blue-grey x-ray man in a T-shirt (hook), a white-sweep full body, glass torsos on sky blue, a skeleton with realistic human eyes, a skeleton couple on a bed, and a stylised 3D cartoon man in the close. Skulls are mostly anatomical with no cartoon eyes.
- **Setting:** mostly a flat sky-blue to steel-blue gradient backdrop (like #5a95a8 / #8196a9) or a white-grey studio sweep. A few real-world plates: a dark navy bedroom (hook, 52 s), a warm blurred kitchen (57 s).
- **Light:** soft, even studio light with a gentle rim. The organs glow from inside (orange-gold emissive). Macro shots have bright specular highlights.
- **Colour, measured per shot:** brightness median 62 (10th–90th percentile 42–70); contrast median 17 (low, flat); saturation median 36 (20–63; the macro gold shots peak at 63–72); warmth median +8.8 (+0.8 to +36.9). Five shots are cool (the blue backdrops, warmth −17 to −10); 15 are strongly warm (the gold macros, +20 to +45). The palette is a deliberate complement: **cool blue ground, warm gold subject**. Example averages: hook #5d5257; blue-backdrop torso #9a9382; gold macro #b68148; blue motion graphic #5a95a8.
- **Camera:** an almost constant slow push, broken by bursts of fast fly-ins at macro scale, plus two 160–180° orbits. No handheld feel, except the last UGC clip. Shot sizes stay medium or closer; the only full-body shots are shot 4 and the bed wide.

## Lane match
- **Nearest: skeleton-xray**, but only part of the way. It matches on: a translucent body showing the skeleton plus organs, a "clean blue gradient backdrop, soft rim light", and a cool body with warm organ glow. It does not match on:
  - no large cartoon eyes (the skulls are anatomical, with closed or empty sockets, or realistic eyes);
  - no single same character in every shot;
  - the organs are gold/yellow/amber, not "red and orange";
  - the body is a clear glass shell more often than "blue-tinted skin" (blue tint only in the hook).
- Not Bare-Bones (no photoreal warm sets, no lovable cartoon-eyed hero). Not the Cute mascot.
- The real lane here is a **"glass-body medical explainer"**: the body is a window, and the camera keeps pushing into whatever organ the voice names.

## Why it works (look-wise)
- Every 2–3 s the picture moves closer to the thing being named, so the eye never rests. The slow drift keeps even still diagrams alive.
- The deep-dive burst (31–45 s) is the visual peak. Each cut lands one level deeper inside the body, which feels like discovery. The fly-ins end on glossy, tactile macro textures (beads, crystals, syrup) that are satisfying to look at and never gross.
- The cool blue ground and warm gold organ put the subject at the hottest point of every frame, under a caption that sits right on it.
- One-word captions over the belly keep the reader's eye exactly where the push is going.

## How the director recreates this look from any script
- Keep:
  - one shot per spoken idea;
  - a slow push of about 2–5 %/s under every explanation shot;
  - for the key "inside" moment, a chain of 4–9 hard cuts, each a fast fly-in (20–60 %/s, speeding up) one level deeper, ending in a glossy macro;
  - a translucent glass-body render on a flat sky-blue gradient, with warm gold emissive organs;
  - hard cuts on word onsets;
  - one-word captions added in the edit at about 70% height;
  - a narrator over a bed kept about 30 dB down.
- Vary freely: which organ or body part, the props that float in (foods, pills, fruit), the bed or kitchen plates, an orbit for a "spreading" beat, a split-screen panel scale-up as one edit-made punch-in.
- Don't copy:
  - the unrelated cartoon-man ending and the bodies changing between shots (it reads as stock-mixing, not design);
  - the product pack shots and UGC hand clip (they belong to that brand);
  - the 360x640 softness;
  - any medical claim imagery tied to that product's ingredients.
