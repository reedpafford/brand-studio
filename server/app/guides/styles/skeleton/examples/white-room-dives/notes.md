## Zoom-in behaviour (the thing Reed asked about)
In this video the zoom is **not** a constant push on every shot. It is a short, fast "dive into the body" used a few times, in the stretch where the voice explains what happens inside.
- **How often:** 3 dive moments in 105 s (one every ~35 s on average), all between 59 and 77 s. Counting every measured push (including slow drifts on macro shots), the kit marks 6 push shots (15% of shots, 16% of runtime), 0.6 a 10 s. 70% of shots (66% of runtime) are holds where only the character moves.
- **The three devices:**
  1. **Zoom-blur punch** (59.34–59.47, 0.13 s): a radial blur scale-up on the character's belly, cut on the blur into a macro organ shot. An edit transition, not a camera move.
  2. **Continuous dive-in** (63.53–64.14, ~0.6 s): from a medium on the seated character, the camera pushes through the ribcage into the gut, and the real organs give way to a 3D medical render of the same organ. The kit reads 36 %/s and 33 %/s for those two seconds (averaged over the whole second); frame by frame the scale grows about 2.5x in ~0.4 s *(estimated from the strips)*. A slow push (8.8 %/s) keeps going on the macro shot after it lands.
  3. **Pull-out** (76.0–76.4, ~0.4 s): the reverse of 2: from the macro organ back out through the ribs to the full character on the couch (kit: −27.5 %/s for that second).
- **On what:** always the body part the voice is naming at that moment (belly/gut, the organ that is suffering or healing). Never the face, never the product.
- **Slow pushes:** the macro inside-the-body shots drift in slowly, 4–9 %/s. The cartoon-character shots almost never zoom.
- **Is it camera or edit?** The dive and pull-out look generated (one continuous move whose picture changes from cartoon body to medical render; parallax on the ribs). The zoom-blur punch is an editor effect (radial blur on a still frame, then a cut). *(inferred from the strips)*
- **Timing against the voice:** each zoom starts on a word onset and lands on the next word onset (blur: starts 59.34, the next word starts 59.52, the macro is sharp by 59.54; dive: starts with the word at 63.40–63.86, lands at 64.07–64.14, the next word is at 64.30; pull-out starts exactly on the word at 76.00). Cuts in general: 82% land within 0.1 s of a word start (random times would: 61%); 38% land within 0.1 s of a music beat (random: 47%), so cuts follow the voice, not the music. 13 of 39 cuts fall mid-word.

## Style pattern
- **Character:** an adult-proportioned, lanky cartoon skeleton (ivory-white bones, big round white cartoon eyes with black pupils, expressive jaw and tongue) inside a **clear glass-like body shell** that shows the real-looking organs (pink intestines, dark liver, stomach). One identity accessory: a black wave cap/durag. Pain or effect is shown as a red or gold glow on the organ region. Not chibi, not scary.
- **World:** one all-white set language: a padded, quilted white room used for every place (hospital bed, kitchen, office, couch, corridor). Near-monochrome: walls #d4d3d6, whole frames #a0–#b8 grey, saturation 4–12, warmth about 0. The only colours are the organs and the glows. Mood beats change the room with grey fog (#545353, 35 brightness) rather than a new place.
- **Inserts:** 3D medical renders of the organs (saturated warm reds and browns, e.g. #6c312a, saturation 57–69, warmth 20–29, dark background, glowing edges); one photoreal calendar macro; and at the end real hand-held phone footage in a sunny garden and a factory (saturation 41–50, warmth 15–21).
- **Camera:** mostly locked and eye level; the energy comes from the character's broad, fast acting (subject motion 4–31 in the kit's units). Angles vary: frontal medium, top-down on the bed, profile at the stove, high angle for the bottle reveal. The only big camera moves are the dives into and out of the body.
- **Light:** soft, even, shadowless studio white, like a light box; no hard sun except in the real-footage end.

## Lane match
- Closest current lane: **skeleton-xray** in purpose (organs on show, a story about what happens inside the body). But the look does not match that lane's words: the shell here is **clear and neutral, not blue-tinted**; organs are **natural pink and brown**, glowing red or gold only to show pain or effect, not a constant red-orange glow; the world is a **white padded room**, not "a real setting with an x-ray glow or a clean blue gradient backdrop", and it is not "sci-fi medical" lit.
- Not Bare-Bones: there is a body shell and organs, and the world is a white set, not a "photoreal, richly dressed environment, warm colour grade, volumetric light". Not Mascot: adult proportions.
- So this is a fourth look the lanes don't name: a **clear-shell anatomical skeleton in a white room**.

## Why it works (look only)
- The red glow on the body in the first frame shows "something hurts here" with the sound off.
- One white set keeps the eye on the character and its organs; any colour on screen means something (pain red, remedy gold).
- Fast, broad acting keeps the picture alive without camera moves, so the rare dive into the body feels like an event: the viewer is taken inside exactly when the voice explains the inside.
- Macro renders give "proof-like" visuals; the real hand-held garden footage at the end switches to a believable, real-world register for the product section.

## How the director recreates this look from any script
- Lock the character first: clear-shell skeleton, visible organs, big cartoon eyes, one identity accessory, used in every character shot.
- Pick one set language (here the white padded room) and re-dress it per line (bed, desk, couch, kitchen) rather than travelling to new places.
- One phrase, one shot of 2–3 s with a big readable action or pose; show pain or effect as a coloured glow on the organ the line names.
- When the line moves inside the body, use a dive: a 0.4–0.6 s push through the ribs that lands on a 3D medical macro of that organ, timed to start on the word and land on the next one; hold the macro 2.5–5 s with a slow drift in (4–9 %/s); come back out with the reverse pull-out. Use 2–4 of these per ~100 s, not on every shot.
- An editor zoom-blur punch is the cheap version of the dive.
- Do NOT copy: the product, its bottle, the herbs, the garden and factory footage (they belong to this brand), the durag (any accessory works), the calendar flip as a fixed device.
