## The format pattern (no words)
1. First frame: someone already doing the thing.
2. A chain of problem → fix → new problem, each told by following the physical stuff (powder, foam, litter) to where it goes.
3. Time passing shown with an in-world flip calendar.
4. The bad result made visible and smelly (green bubble, green stink).
5. A closing gag (someone emerges from the mess). No sign-off.

## The style pattern (look and camera)
- Semi-realistic 3D CG, a little rougher than the other two: realistic-proportioned game-style people (slightly stiff faces), real materials, candy-coloured set. Average colour #645c55; saturation moderate (15 on ffmpeg's scale, 35 on the kit's); warm (+5 on the kit's warmth). Brightness median 94/255.
- One big set (a film set) the camera roams freely: crane, track, orbit, whip, all inside one space.
- Meaning colours in the world: lime-green highlight on a label, green glow for the rot and the smell.

## Why it works
- Following the stuff (powder → water → foam → litter → rot) means the viewer is always looking at the cause of the next line.
- A face reaction at each problem (director, passengers, people holding noses) gives the human beat.
- The final gag lands the story as a joke rather than a lesson.

## How the director recreates this from a new script
- Find the physical thing that travels through the story and make the camera follow it.
- Keep one set and move the camera around it (cranes, tracks, whips) instead of cutting.
- Insert a quick reaction face at each turn; link them with face-matched dissolves.
- Don't copy: the chocolate river, the film set, the specific gag.
