## Pacing
- Real cuts about 6.9 per 10 s overall, but it is lumpy, not even:
  - **Dialogue scenes: long holds.** 6.0 s locked two-shot, 9.2 s push-in, 7.4 s and 5.8 s two-shots. Dialogue shots run 1-9 s; reaction CUs 0.5-3.3 s.
  - **Montage of inserts: 0.2-0.7 s per shot**, about 2 cuts a second (12.4-17.5 s, 13 shots in 5.1 s).
  - **Time-lapse: one locked frame for 10-13 s** with the light or the subject doing the cutting (flips every 0.1-0.5 s).
  - **Tag: 0.2-0.5 s per clip** (8 clips in 2.7 s).
- Speeds up: the wordless montage (12-20 s) and the tag. Slows down: every dialogue exchange and the reveal of the parent joining in.
- Cuts are not locked to beats: 21 of 59 cuts fall within 50 ms of a beat, about what chance gives at this tempo. The montage is cut to the action, not the grid.

## Camera
- Mostly **locked off or a slow push**. The two biggest moves are the 9.2 s push from mid-wide to ECU (zoom about 3%/s) and a slow right pan reframing a reveal (-5.7%/s). Measured "handheld shake" in the dialogue CUs is mild drift (3.7-4.6), checked on the strips: framing stays steady.
- **Eye level** almost everywhere. Exceptions: low-angle frontal "webcam" frames (17.5-20.2) where he slumps back and the camera looks up his nose, and the night time-lapse looking slightly down at the desk.
- **Whip-blur cuts** in and out of ECUs (7.4, 56.6) and smear transitions; a 0.33 s black frame starts the montage.
- No slow motion. Fast motion with ghosting carries the time-lapse.

## Framing and shot sizes
- Two-shots and wides for dialogue (both characters full or 3/4 figure, central), ECUs for reactions (face fills the frame edge to edge, chin to brow), insert CUs on hands and objects.
- Centered, symmetrical, frontal compositions dominate. Depth: background stock towers and lit doorways sit soft behind the subject (shallow depth of field in CUs; deeper in the wides).

## Setting
- A real-world suburban home: a teen's bedroom turned stockroom (towers of black poly bags floor to ceiling, later rainbow-coloured stacks), a desk with laptop, tape gun and label printer, a white panel door to a warmly lit hallway, a closet of cardboard boxes. Ordinary, specific, cluttered with photoreal props.

## Talent look
- Stylised 3D feature-animation characters (Pixar-like CG render): oversized expressive eyes, thick sculpted brows, smooth skin with soft subsurface glow, slightly elongated faces. Adult parent has adult proportions and a defined jaw; the teen reads as a teenager.
- Expressions are pushed to cartoon extremes: saucer eyes, jaw drops, gritted teeth, a full-body star jump, glowing price tags reflected in the eyes as a visual gag.
- One fixed outfit each, identical in every shot: teen in a backwards blue cap, white tee with a small dark chest logo, silver chain, stud earrings, dark trousers; parent in a beige button shirt with rolled sleeves, dark trousers, small gold hoops, bob haircut. The cap is the silhouette item.
- Consistency is strong across 57 s (face, cap, chain, logo hold). Minor drift: the face is softer and blurrier in fast-motion frames.

## Screens, phones and app UI
- Shown **both ways**: phone held in hand (frontal two-hand hold, thumb tapping; face lit by the screen at night) **and full-frame screen inserts** (laptop/listing pages filling the frame with a finger tapping, a "sold"-style pop-up card, 13-17 s).
- At night, notification cards float in 3D space around the character (26-27.5 s): screens leaving the device as a visual device.

## Sound
- **A music bed runs under everything** (no silence; -14.1 LUFS). It changes per section:
  - 0-7.4 s: soft tonal bed only (sustained harmonic lines in "other"; drums and bass silent, -61 / -67 dB).
  - 7.4 s: one low impact (bass and drum burst) on the whip-cut into the ECU.
  - 12-20 s: full beat drops in on the black frame: drums -23 dB, bass -23 dB, loudest stretch with voice gone. Small high-frequency blips in the vocal stem during the screen inserts *(inferred: UI click/ding effects)*.
  - 20-29 s: beat drops out under the night scene; at 26-26.7 s a cluster of hits (notification dings) as the cards pop; 27.5-29.3 s a rising pitch sweep (riser) into the eye gag.
  - 29.4 s: the beat returns on the jump and drives the time-lapse; bass drops out 35-40 s while lines are spoken, returns 40-43 s.
  - 43.4 s: new warmer section, bass pulses, harmonic pads.
  - 50.8-56.7 s: a melodic, pitch-moving line takes over (the "other" stem jumps to -21 dB) with drums near silent: a lighter, feel-good cue under the payoff.

- Voice: shouted and comic, very wide pitch range (7.8 st variability), short exchanges with gaps; voice sits clearly above the bed in dialogue (about -25 vs -40 dB).

## Delivery energy
- High and comic: yelled openers, deadpan short answers, a whoop on the breakthrough, a sheepish last line. Lines are short with pauses between (median pause 0.9 s); long wordless stretches (12-20 s, 30-36 s, 39-48 s) let music and action carry.

## Style pattern (what makes it House)
- Stylised 3D feature-animation people in an ordinary, specific real-world home with photoreal props.
- Dialogue in long locked or slowly pushing two-shots and big-face ECUs; process and time passing in fast wordless inserts and locked time-lapses.
- Warm interior day light; night is blue with screen light on the face and warm light spilling from a doorway.
- Cartoon-extreme reactions and one visual gag at the turn.
- Phones and laptop screens are a constant device, in hand and full frame.

## Why it works (look and feel)
- 0-1.4 s: a face bursting toward the lens plus a title line; the viewer sees conflict before any word lands.
- 1.4-7.4 s: one held wide shows the whole situation (walls of stock) so the exchange can play without cuts.
- 12-20 s: the beat drops on black and the process plays in 0.2-0.7 s inserts: momentum after the talk.
- 20-29 s: the slow push and the rising sweep build to the eye gag; the payoff is visual.
- 30-43 s: a locked time-lapse with the parent at normal speed in the warm doorway shows weeks and her change of mind in one frame.
- 43-57 s: warm golden light and a melodic cue mark the turn to warmth; the ECU button ends on a laugh.
- 57-60 s: a hard switch to real phone footage and a louder track resets attention for the loop.

## How the director recreates this look from a new script
- Dialogue beats: one held two-shot or wide per exchange (4-9 s), cut to ECUs only for the sharpest reactions (0.5-3 s). Keep the camera locked or on one slow push.
- Process or "time passes" beats: wordless inserts at 0.2-0.7 s, or one locked frame with light flicker and fast-motion ghosting for 10-13 s.
- Night beats: blue room, screen light on the face, warm practical behind.
- One visual gag at the turn (something reflected or floating), sold by a push-in and a riser.
- Music: a bed that changes per section (soft under talk, beat under montage, warm melodic under the resolution); impacts on whip-cuts.
- Captions one word at a time, added in the edit.
- **Do not copy:** the reselling setting, the stock item, the shipping process, the live-action lifestyle tag (that is the creator's own channel device, not part of the look), the title text, the gibberish UI.
