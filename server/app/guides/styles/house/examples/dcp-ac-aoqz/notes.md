  she approaches (measured push-in 5.1 %/s plus her own walk). Big furrowed brows, wide eyes, bared teeth.

## Camera
- Mostly static or gently pushing inside a shot (kit: 26 of 48 shots "static camera, moving subject"; pushes on 8).
  Energy comes from the characters' motion (subject motion 10-30 on dialogue shots) and from the cutting, not from
  constant camera moves.
- Snap/crash zooms for emphasis: shot 24 (into one eye), shot 43 (into a screaming mouth), shot 11 (parent's face).
- Whip-pans with heavy motion blur as transitions (shots 5->6->7, 12->13, 26->27): shake values 13-55 on those shots are
  whips, not handheld (checked on strips).
- Low camera at desk/table height with the dark table top filling the lower third of frame (shots 4, 8, 10, 11, 26-41):
  a recurring "behind the counter" two-shot.
- Locked-off frames for time-lapse (shots 20-22) and for the repeated montage framing (28-41).
- Frontal, centred compositions are common (2, 23, 27, 28-42). Over-the-shoulder framing with a blurred shoulder/head in
  the foreground (3, 4, 43).
- The camera sits at or slightly below eye level; no overhead or extreme low angles in the CG part.
- Shallow depth of field on close two-shots (shot 12: background stock is a soft blur).

## Transitions
- Hard cuts on dialogue; whip-pans with motion smear into inserts; AI-style smear/morph frames joining scenes (the door
  burst at 0.1 s, 15.1 s, 23-26 s, all of shot 25, every montage cut). These smears read as speed, not as errors, because
  they last 1-2 frames.
- Time passing is shown in-shot: a clock face spinning, the sun crossing the window, stacks rising, an empty room filling.
- No white flash and no fade to black in this reel. The CG-to-live-action change at 58.0 s is a hard cut.

## Setting
- One real-world family home: a cramped teenager's bedroom/office with a desk, monitor, pin board, wire shelving and
  floor-to-ceiling stacks of bagged clothing and cardboard boxes; white panel doors, grey-green walls, beige carpet/tile.
- Dense, specific set dressing: dozens of poly-bagged garments in many colours, boxes with shipping labels, a wall clock,
  a laptop and phone. Props look close to photoreal (the bagged stock in shots 6-7 is nearly photographic).
- Montage backgrounds swap to impossible places behind the same table (palm trees, starry sky, brick wall) to show time
  passing.

- Night: cool blue moonlight plus the cold glow of laptop/phone screens lighting faces from below/front (shots 13-23).

## Talent look
- Stylised 3D feature-animation characters: big expressive eyes with catchlights, heavy sculpted brows, large mouths
  that stretch to cartoon extremes (bared teeth, jaw-drop screams, saucer eyes in shots 23-24, 43). Skin is smooth with
  soft subsurface warmth; hair is sculpted in clumps.
- Proportions: adults are adult-height with long faces and defined jaws; the teen is lanky with a long neck. Heads are
  slightly large for the body but not childlike.
- Real-world clothes, one fixed outfit each, identical in every shot: the teen in a navy cap worn backwards, white tee
  with a small dark logo, silver chain, black trousers, white sneakers; the parent in a beige button-up blouse, dark
  trousers, bob haircut. The cap is the silhouette item.
- Identity holds well across 58 s. Generation slips are visible in fast motion (smeared hands, a prop that changes
  mid-shot in shot 8, a garbled moment in shot 35 where the two faces merge for one frame). *(Treat these as errors to
  avoid, not style.)*
- Acting is broad and physical: pointing, slamming, chair spins, head in hands, arms thrown up.

    sustained tonal pad/keys with a soft pulsing bass and sparse light percussion.
  - Stare-down (13.4-15.1): the bed dips; near-silence under the stare (mix -21 dB).
  - Night grind (15.1-28.5): the bed swells and fills out (bass -32, other -30, drums -35): full, sustained, emotional,
    with dense drum ticks from ~20 s as the montage starts.
  - Discovery (28.5-43.6): the bed drops away for the realisation (bass gone 30-32 s), then rebuilds under the packing.
  - Packing montage (43.6-49.2): loudest and busiest (drums -26, bass -28 dB).
  - Payoff (49.2-58): bass and drums drop out (bass -63 dB); a bright stepped melodic line rises in the other stem

## Live-action tag
- 58.0-60.0 s: 5 handheld phone clips of real people (a pose that mirrors a CG moment, a yacht dinner, a car interior with

## The format pattern (structure only)
Cold open mid-conflict -> stakes stated -> ultimatum -> low point at night -> wordless grind montage with time passing ->
the turn (a screen shows success) -> the doubter joins in a faster montage -> quiet payoff two-shot -> topper reveal and
scream -> live-action tag.

## Why it works (look and feel)
- 0-2 s: a door bursting open and a face charging the lens is pure motion and threat with the sound off.
- Fast cuts and whips through the argument keep every line punchy; the big faces read at a glance.
- The night-blue grind against the warm day scenes makes the low point and the turn visible by colour alone.
- Locked frames with time-lapse and swapping backgrounds show effort and time without words.
- The slowest hold (5.5 s) is saved for the payoff, so it lands by contrast; the crash zoom on the scream is the button.

## How the director recreates this look from a new script
- Keep: stylised 3D characters with big readable faces in one real-world location dressed densely with photoreal props;
  warm, soft, muted day light and cool screen-lit night; mostly static or slow-pushing camera with snap zooms for
  emphasis and whip-pans for inserts; low desk-height two-shots; time-lapse in locked frames; montage cuts at 0.2-0.7 s;
  the longest hold on the payoff; a music bed that swells and drops with the story; captions added in the edit.
- Don't copy: the brand's logo tee and stock, the live-action creator tag, the pseudo-text screens, the generation slips
  (merged faces, mid-shot prop swaps).
