## 3. The hook (0 to 2 s)
- **Seen:** a medium-wide two-shot of an adult woman and a teen boy in a bedroom, framed from the far side of a bed. The bed's grey duvet fills the bottom third of the frame as a foreground. She upends a black bin bag. A cascade of small white objects (hundreds of the same item) pours onto the bed. He lunges in from frame right to grab the bag. Both faces are already at full anger and panic in the first frame.
- **Framing:** eye level to slightly low, from behind the bed. The camera drifts and pans right about 5% of the frame width per second to keep both people in frame (kit: "handheld shake" 11.8, pan -1.9%/s; the strips show a smooth follow, not real shake). At about 2.9 s a motion-blur whip, which the kit didn't count as a cut, swings to a wider angle that shows wire shelving stacked with stock.
- **What moves:** the pour (big particle motion in the centre), both actors' arms, the camera's follow.
- **Text:** a white title line in plain sans-serif sits mid-frame (about 48% height) with a skull emoji, from 0 to about 1.5 s. Word-by-word yellow captions sit under it (about 65% height).
- **Sound:** the shouted line starts at 0.00 s. The drums stem shows foley ticks (the objects landing) and no music beat. The bass stem holds one low sustained tone (a sub drone) under everything.

## 5. Pacing
- Overall 7.85 cuts per 10 s, but the rhythm is **bimodal**: long held two-shots for dialogue (shots 1, 5, 22, 24, 29, 35: 2.6 to 8.7 s) against bursts of sub-second inserts (0.17 to 0.6 s).
- By segment (kit shot lengths):
  - Opening confrontation (0 to 10.9 s): 7 shots, median 1.03 s, 0.33 to 3.5 s.
  - Grind montage (10.9 to 19.9 s): 14 shots, median 0.37 s, 0.2 to 2.13 s. About 15 cuts per 10 s.
  - Eviction and kerb scene (19.9 to 37.9 s): 4 shots, median 4.1 s, up to 8.7 s. Under 2 cuts per 10 s.
  - Kerb plus pivot (39.5 to 56.7 s): 9 shots, median 1.27 s, the 7.07 s two-shot then 0.5 to 1.7 s phone inserts.
  - Car flex (56.7 to 61.4 s): 7 shots, median 0.67 s, no dialogue, cut to sound effects.
  - Car dialogue (61.4 to 69.5 s): 8 shots, median 1.14 s, steady shot / reverse shot.
  - Tag (69.5 to 71.4 s): 4 shots, median 0.42 s.
- **Cuts aren't on the beat:** 23 of 55 cuts fall within 80 ms of a detected beat. With a beat every 0.42 s, chance alone predicts about 21. Cuts follow lines and actions, not the music. The one exception: the montage starts on the bass drop at about 10.9 s.

## 6. Sound (heard through stems, spectrograms and levels)
- Integrated level: the mix runs about -14 to -18 dB per segment (relative mean). It never falls below -29 dB in any 100 ms window, so **there's no silence anywhere**: something always sits under the voice.
- **Bed:** a continuous low sustained tone on the bass stem runs the whole video, a sub-bass drone or pad *(inferred: a cinematic tension bed)*. Under dialogue it sits about 15 to 20 dB under the voice (vocals -20 to -22 dB, bass -36 to -40, drums -43 to -46).
- **Music drop for the montage:** at about 10.9 s the bass stem turns from a drone into rhythmic pulses and the drums stem fills in (drums -31, bass -26 in the montage, against -43 and -40 in the confrontation). A beat-driven cue plays under the grind montage *(the kit's tempo is 143.6 BPM; the steady beat markers support a real pulse in that stretch)*.
- **Foley and sound effects:** short broadband transients in the drums stem line up with the visible actions (items pouring 0 to 3 s, slaps, the bag throw, cardboard). The car-flex stretch (56.7 to 61.2 s) has no speech (vocals -31 dB) and a loud broadband roar in the "other" stem (-22 dB): engine and exhaust-pop sound design carries the scene. A sustained horizontal tone at about 63 s in "other" *(inferred: a car horn or a held synth note)*.
- **Live-action insert and tag** change texture at once (drums -19 dB at 37.9 s, "other" and bass up in the tag): their own phone audio or a music sting.
- **Delivery:** median voice pitch 213 Hz, a 23-semitone p10 to p90 range, variability 8.4 semitones, which is very animated and shouty in the confrontations. 3.46 words/s while speaking, median pause 0.38 s, longest 4.5 s (the car flex). The first word lands at 0.00 s. The spectrogram shows tight back-and-forth with almost no gap between speakers in the dialogue scenes.

Each word appears when it's spoken and stays until the next word (no pop or scale animation visible at 10 fps). They run through every spoken line, including over full-screen phone inserts.

## 8. The style pattern (look)
- **Render:** stylised 3D CG feature-animation look. Big heads and eyes, oversized expressive brows, smooth skin with soft subsurface, stylised noses (the friend's long pointed nose, the stepmom's glamour proportions). Bodies are adult or teen proportioned (the teen about 6.5 heads tall *(inferred from strips)*), not chibi. Bystanders and crowds share the same render, slightly simpler.
- **Faces carry the comedy:** saucer eyes, jaw drops, teeth-gritted grimaces, smug half-lidded smirks. The expressions change within a shot (shot 6, 41, 47 to 51). Reactions are pushed to cartoon extremes but the timing stays naturalistic.
- **Real-world settings, dense set dressing:** a lived-in teen bedroom (cork board, posters, wire shelving stacked with product boxes), a front porch with siding and a mailbox, a suburban street, a home photo studio with softboxes, a city street of brick and stone shopfronts, a car interior. The props are near-photoreal (bin bags, cardboard boxes, packaging, a supercar), the people stylised.
- **Lighting:** motivated and naturalistic. Indoors: soft window light with practicals; golden-hour sun raking through curtains (shots 19 to 21: warmth 27.7, saturation 61, the warmest moment). Outdoors: bright late-afternoon sun with long shadows, clear blue sky. Car interior: dark cabin, face lit from the window side.
- **Colour grade (sampled):** warm-neutral, moderate saturation (25 to 35 on most dialogue shots), mid brightness. By segment (length-weighted means): confrontation #705854 (bright 35, sat 32, warm 10); montage #725d57 (49 / 30 / 7); eviction and kerb #52413b (37 / 23 / 7); kerb plus pivot #6d6664 (50 / 28 / 9); car flex #5f4e53 (37 / 30 / 3.5); car dialogue #503d3c (30 / 35 / 6); live tag #3c3230 (23 / 42 / 0). Shadow / highlight split: shadows are **neutral to warm brown-black** (#392b28, #1d1618, #2e180b, #080406), highlights warm cream (#bca196, #c59777, #dcb185). Only the outdoor sky frames have cool highlights (#c2c8cf). **No blue cast in the shadows.** Blue comes from set colours (blue-grey bedroom walls, a blue room, blue sky), not from the grade. Blacks go deep in the car interior (#080406) and come close to crushed.
- **Camera:**
  - Mostly static or locked-off frames (34 of 56 shots read "static camera, moving subject" or "locked off").
  - Movement comes from subject action and **object-to-lens transitions**.
  - Where the camera does move, it's a slow push or pull (2 to 13% zoom per second), a follow pan, a top-down push in the montage, a tracking shot on the car, and one **crash zoom into the mouth** for the exit.
  - Eye level dominates. Low ground-level angles come in for the pavement and car scenes (shots 24, 38, 40).
  - **Centred, symmetrical frontal compositions** for "time passing" (shots 13, 16 to 22).
  - Shallow depth of field on inserts and the over-the-shoulder car shots (soft foreground profile, sharp background face).
- **Transitions are the signature motion:**
  - Objects thrown or flying at the lens (bag, earbud, cardboard).
  - Motion-blur whips.
  - Morph or smear blends between generated clips.
  - A white flash-dissolve into a screen.
  - An app's photo area turning into a live shot.
  - One black frame for a hard time jump.
  - A glitch "rewind" out of live action.
  - The crash zoom into an open mouth to exit into live action.
  - Plain hard cuts in dialogue.
- **Continuity drift is tolerated:** the lead loses his cap and gains curlier hair in shots 17 to 22; his room changes wall colour between shots; in shot 37 the friend is rendered as a near-copy of the lead. The reel still reads because the outfit (white tee with a small dark chest logo, black trousers, white sneakers, navy cap, silver chain) stays fixed.
- **Screens:**
  - Two kinds. One is the **phone in hand** (shots 30 to 33: CU of the hand, screen about 60% of the frame, scrolling thumb, background blurred).
  - The other is the **full-frame app screen** (shots 11, 12, 15). Its photo area plays live footage of the character.
  - Both are AI-generated UIs with garbled text.

## 9. Format pattern (structure only, for the Dialogue format; no words)
- Cold open mid-conflict, dialogue from frame 1. A doubter figure versus the lead. A punchy one-line title over the first 1.5 s.
- Confrontation in 2 to 4 s held two-shots and OTS, then a beat-drop grind montage of sub-second inserts (top-down hands, macro product, app screens, a "days passing" same-pose run).
- A reversal scene in long locked-off two-shots (7 to 9 s holds), with gags played inside the wide frame.
- A short live-action "real clips" insert marked with a text label, then a glitch back to CG.
- A no-dialogue flex sequence cut to sound effects (car pass-bys, bystander reactions, a crowd turning).
- A shot / reverse-shot payoff exchange, then a crash zoom out to a 2 s live-action handheld tag of the creator's real life.

## 10. Why it works (look)
- Frame 1 has big motion (the pour), two extreme faces and a shout, so there's no ramp to wait through.
- Long held two-shots let the cartoon acting and physical comedy play out. The inserts then make the grind feel fast without new lines.
- Object-to-lens transitions keep energy across cuts and hide changes of place.
- The silent car-flex run swaps words for sound and spectacle right before the payoff, so the last exchange lands.
- The live-action tag makes the CG fable feel tied to a real person *(inferred: the creator's own branding)*.

## 11. How the director recreates this look from a new script
- Keep:
  - The stylised 3D feature-animation render with exaggerated readable faces.
  - Real-world, densely dressed places.
  - Warm-neutral, naturally motivated light, with golden hour for emotional or turning beats.
  - Mostly static frames, with moves saved for pushes, pulls, follows and one crash zoom.
  - Long held two-shots for dialogue, then sub-second insert bursts for montage.
  - Object-to-lens and blur-whip transitions.
  - Phones and app screens as story devices.
  - A constant low bed under the dialogue, a beat that drops in for montage, and foley on every physical action.
- Don't copy:
  - The specific products, brand names on props or the supercars.
  - The title's wording.
  - The yellow caption colour as a rule *(other house reels use white, see the other studies)*.
  - The live-action tag and the "actual vids" insert: they belong to this creator's own life and channel.
  - The garbled app text, which is a model limitation, not a choice.
