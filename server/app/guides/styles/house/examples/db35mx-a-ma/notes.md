## 10. Style pattern (the look)
- **Render:** stylised 3D CG *(Pixar-like CG as a description of the render)*. Soft, rounded forms, subsurface skin, fine hair strands, cloth with real weave. Sets and props are realistic and dense (living room, bedroom stacked with stock, a warehouse, a porch street at dusk).
- **Faces and proportions:** caricatured but readable. The antagonist adult has an enormous square jaw and chin, a long nose, a tall head and broad shoulders (adult proportions, ~7 heads); the teen hero has big brown eyes, thick dark brows, a curly mop under a backwards cap; the second parent has a bob and big lashes. Expressions swing from subtle (a flat stare, a slow blink) to cartoon-big (saucer eyes, jaw-drop, gritted teeth) on reaction beats (shots 2, 6, 45, 47, 71).
- **Wardrobe:** one outfit per character, identical in every shot across 86 s: the hero's white tee with a small navy chest logo, slate-blue backwards cap, thin silver chain, black trousers, white sneakers; the antagonist's navy suit, pale grey shirt, navy tie; the second parent's cream blouse and navy jeans. Silhouette items read instantly (the backwards cap, the suit).
- **Camera:** mostly eye level and close: medium close-ups and close-ups dominate, frontal singles for lines. Dirty over-the-shoulder framings with a big soft dark shoulder in the foreground (shots 1, 4, 16-17, 44). Moves are few and simple: slow pushes on lines, snap pushes to extreme close-up on shocks (6, 27, 71), whip-pans as cuts (5, 48, 58-59), one long arcing take for the main argument (19). Many shots are effectively static; the life is in the faces.
- **Depth of field:** shallow on close-ups (backgrounds melt: 18, 24, 64, 66); deeper on wides.
- **Lighting by place:** day interiors: soft window daylight from behind/right, warm-neutral beige walls, low contrast (avg #7a6457, brightness 43, warmth +12; shadows warm brown #2b1c16, highlights #c8bdb6). Dark-room grind: a single warm practical plus phone glow under-lighting the face from below, near-black surroundings (highlights #d6a175, shadows #130701). Night rain street: cool blue throughout, wet reflections, streetlights (avg #24344c, saturation 61, warmth -14; highlights #6586af). Dusk exterior: blue-hour sky with warm lit windows, the only place with a clear warm-against-cool split (avg #262c3b; shot 44's lit window inside a blue frame). Warehouse: flat overhead fluorescent, neutral.
- **Colour grade:** natural, mid-saturation (CG average saturation 37.5 on the kit's scale), gently warm overall (CG average #665650, warmth +7.9), lifted midtones indoors, deep blacks at night. Warm shadows indoors, not blue ones.
- **Screens:** shown four ways: POV over hands on a laptop with the screen filling ~60% of the frame (29-32, 40); a phone in hand filling the frame with the thumb visible (33, 38, 46); a phone held up to the face as a light source (34, 39, 45, 47, 57-58, 70); and once a bare full-screen app scroll with no phone frame (53). The UI is generic and its text is garbled AI lettering.
- **Inserts and B-roll:** a lot of it. Quick object inserts (bag, printer, tape gun, boxes), product walls and stock piles. Several inserts are photoreal or real phone footage (3.90, 4.00, 4.77, 25.37, 41-42, 49 product walls, 50 printer, 55 cart, 62 warehouse), cut in among CG shots without any change of grade.
- **Transitions:** hard cuts mostly; whip-pan blur; foreground wipes (a wall passes the lens, 23 and 25); a dissolve-in memory overlay (25); AI morph frames between flash inserts (9, 10); one cut to black before the tag.

## 11. Why it works (for a viewer, by time)
- 0-2 s: a face mid-shout and a smug counter-face, captions popping word by word: conflict is clear with the sound off.
- 2-6.6 s: the whip to the empty wall and a snap push to a jaw-drop, then a burst of sub-0.4 s inserts: the loss is shown, not explained, at a frantic pace.
- 6.6-24.8 s: the cutting slows to long held takes, so the argument feels real and the long take contrasts with the burst.
- 24.8-35 s: quick packing inserts, a lens-filling vow, then a cool blue rain walk: the colour change itself says "low point".
- 35-66 s: the warm dark room and phone-lit face make each small win intimate; the whip-blur montage speeds up as things grow; the dusk shot of the antagonist watching through a lit window sets up the payoff.
- 66-86 s: frontal singles, line by line, let each face land; the snap zoom and cut to black end on a laugh.
- 86-89 s: a 0.1-0.2 s flash of real footage at a louder level jolts the viewer and loops back to the start.

## 12. How the director recreates this look from a new script
- Carry over: the CG render with caricatured-but-readable faces and one fixed outfit per character; eye-level close singles for lines and dirty over-the-shoulder for confrontation; long held two-shots for the main argument; snap pushes to extreme close-up only on shock beats; whip-pan transitions in fast stretches; a colour shift per place (warm-neutral day interior, single-practical warm dark room with phone under-light, cool blue night, blue-hour exterior with warm windows); fast object inserts between lines; one-word uppercase captions added in edit; a soft music bed far under the voice that comes up only when nobody speaks.
- Don't copy: the specific characters, the resale story and its props, the garbled UI, the AI morph frames between inserts, the creator's live-action tag (that belongs to this creator's brand), the title text and the top line.

## Format notes (dialogue skit; structure and pacing only)
- Cold open mid-conflict: no establishing shot; the first frame is two characters already arguing, with a title line naming the doubter-and-payback premise.
- Beat pacing alternates: a sub-0.4 s insert burst, then long held dialogue takes (4-10.6 s), then medium-pace grind, a whip-blur montage (0.3-0.6 s) with the music up and almost no lines, then line-by-line singles for the payoff (~0.9 s median), a snap-zoom button, cut to black.
