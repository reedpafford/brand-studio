## 5. Pacing
- Overall 6.6 cuts/10 s (kit) to 7.3 (eye-checked); median shot 0.64–0.87 s; longest holds 6.2–6.5 s (the two final day takes and the night time-lapse).
- Dialogue scenes cut only on line changes: every dialogue cut lands in the gap between words, 0.05–0.15 s before the next line starts (word timings). Dialogue shots run 0.9–4.6 s; the opener is one 4.55 s take.
- Wordless stretches run 3–8 s each and carry the fastest cutting (0.1–0.8 s shots) — montage 1 (4.1 s) and the light-flip run (3.9 s). Speech covers ~47 % of runtime.
- Speeds up: 13–17 s and 37–41 s (montages). Slows down: 17–21 s (night low point, locked 4.1 s), 41–53 s (two ~6 s takes, warm resolution).
- Cuts are not beat-locked: 15 of 36 cuts fall within ±80 ms of a beat, about what chance gives at this tempo (beat every 0.42 s).

## 6. Camera and framing
- Mostly eye level. Shot sizes cycle MW / two-shot → CU / ECU reactions → MW again. ECUs are used for punchlines and the turn (shots 4, 6, 14, 31's last frames).
- The camera is mostly still: ~70 % of seconds read "static camera, moving subject" or "locked off". Energy comes from the characters' big physical acting, motion smears and speed ramps, not from camera moves.
- Moves that do occur are single and purposeful: a follow drift in the opener, a pull-out reveal (shot 2), a slow push-in to the realisation (shot 13), a quick pan right to bring a second character in (shot 30, peak −35 %/s), a fast whip-pan to a reveal and a crash zoom to a face (shot 31).
- Montages use one dead-centre, symmetric, locked frontal frame of the same desk, repeated with jump cuts — the time passing is shown by the room changing around a fixed camera.
- Depth of field: shallow on CU/ECU (backgrounds melt), deeper on wides where the set dressing must read.

## 7. Transitions

- Speed-ramped time-lapse with ghosting (several translucent copies of the lead at once) inside a locked frame; visible blocky AI smear artefacts are left in.

## 8. Setting
- One real-world teen bedroom used as the whole story world: grey-blue walls, poster-and-polaroid wall, shelves with boxes, a desk with laptop, wallet, tape gun, mailers, and towering stacks of folded, bagged colourful garments. Dense, specific, lived-in set dressing.
- A doorway onto a lit hallway is a repeating lighting and staging device (the parent appears there).
- Continuity drifts: the night wide (15–16) and part of the time-lapse use a plainer room (white panel door, one dark poster, empty walls). Faces and outfits stay consistent; the room does not.
- Last 0.55 s: the CG lead in a photoreal luxury room that matches the live-action tag room.

## 9. Lighting
- Day scenes: soft, fairly even interior light, gently warm (warmth +10), key from camera-left / window; no hard shadows. A ceiling dome light is visible in the wide.
- Night: dark room lit from below by a cool blue laptop/phone glow on the face, with a warm tungsten hallway spill through the doorway behind — a real warm/cool split.
- Ending: warm golden backlight from a window behind the characters, the warmest section (warmth +14).
- Phone glow lights the face in montage 1 too (the face goes pale and bright as he looks at the phone).

## 11. Talent look
- Stylised 3D feature-animation characters (Pixar-like CG render): big glossy eyes with large irises, heavy sculpted brows, smooth skin with soft subsurface warmth, slightly elongated faces and long noses on the adults, realistic hair grooms (the lead's dark curls, the parent's chestnut bob).
- Proportions read as real ages: a teen with a lanky teen build, an adult parent with adult height and figure.
- Outfits are simple, real-world and fixed: lead in a white tee with one small dark logo, silver chain, backwards blue-grey cap, black trousers; parent in a beige button-up blouse and navy trousers, small gold earrings. Same outfit in every shot of a day; at night the lead's hair goes messy and the cap comes off (a deliberate state change).
- Acting is cartoon-big: bulging eyes, bared teeth, star jumps, faceplants, hands on head. Expressions read with the sound off.
- One stylised gag effect: glowing price-tag graphics inside the pupils (shot 14).

## 15. Delivery energy
- Voice median pitch 223 Hz; pitch range −11 to +8 semitones (p10–p90); variability 7.4 semitones — animated, shouty in the opener, quiet and low at night, playful at the end.
- About 4.1 words per second inside phrases (kit's voiced-time figure: 2.2 w/s); 15 pauses over 250 ms; median pause 1.1 s; longest gap 6.8 s (a montage).
- Lines are short and answered fast: back-and-forth exchanges with 0.05–0.2 s between speakers.

## 16. Style pattern (what makes it look like House)
- Stylised 3D feature-animation characters with huge readable faces, in a dense, real-world, specific set.
- Soft natural day light, a blue night with screen glow and a warm doorway, and a warm golden ending.
- A mostly still camera at eye level; energy from acting, smears, speed ramps and a few single purposeful moves (pull-out reveal, push-in on the turn, whip-pan reveal, crash zoom).
- Locked-frame jump-cut montages for passing time, including light flips.
- Burned yellow one-word captions and a tiny title in the first second (editing, not the model).
- A loud, compressed mix: voice on top, a music bed that ducks under lines and rises in wordless stretches, with whooshes and pings.

## 17. Why it works (for a viewer)
- 0–2 s: an open door, a charging figure and a slammed object; the room of stacked stock says what's at stake before the second line.
- 4.5 s: the high wide reveal turns the room itself into the joke.
- 6.8 s: a reaction CU with the other character's line over it gets the laugh on the face.
- 13–17 s: a locked frame with smear jump cuts makes effort feel fast and cheap to read.
- 17–27 s: darkness and screen glow make the low point quiet; the glowing pupils make the turn a visual gag.
- 37–41 s: light flips say "days pass" with no words.
- 50–54 s: a whip-pan reveal and a crash-zoom horror face end on a laugh.

## 18. How the director recreates this look from a new script
- Keep: one specific, densely dressed real-world location per scene; stylised 3D characters with fixed simple outfits and big faces; eye-level coverage built from wide → two-shot → CU reaction; cut on every line change; use locked frontal frames for wordless montage beats with motion-smear jump cuts and light-flip time passing; day soft-warm, night blue with screen glow and a warm doorway; one purposeful move per shot where it matters (reveal pull-out, push-in on a turn, whip-pan to a reveal, crash zoom on the button reaction).
- Vary freely: which location, how many montages, whether there is a night section, the gag effect.
- Don't copy: the brand-specific stock and logos, the creator's live-action tag, the title text, the CG-in-real-room morph (it belongs to this creator's sign-off), the visible AI smear artefacts, the room continuity drift.
