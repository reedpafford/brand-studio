## 5. Pacing
- Overall 7.7 cuts per 10 s, median 0.98 s. It breathes by segment:
  - **Dialogue scenes** (shots 1-3, 11, 13-14, 29): 0.5-7.4 s, median about 1.5 s. Group dialogue is held in one static wide (3.5 s, 2.4 s) rather than cut line by line; the payoff is the single longest hold (7.4 s).
  - **Process montages** (4-10, 15-22): 0.63-1.77 s, median about 0.95 s; about 1 cut per second, a new micro-reveal per cut. No dialogue.
  - **Growth beats** (23-25): 0.6-0.9 s.
  - **Live-action tag**: 0.2-0.77 s, about 21 cuts per 10 s, the fastest part.
- Longest hold 7.37 s; shortest CG shot 0.63 s; shortest overall 0.20 s (tag).
- Cuts are not beat-locked: 16 of 33 cuts fall within 0.1 s of a detected beat, which is what chance gives at 0.42 s beat spacing (48 %).

## 6. Sound (from stems, spectrograms and loudness; not heard)
- **A music bed runs the whole video**, no silence. Mix loudness is flat at about -17 dB (100 ms RMS) in every CG segment, rising to -14 dB in the live tag.
- Bed character *(inferred from stems)*: steady kick/snare transients on almost every beat (drums stem), a continuous sub-bass line (bass stem bright at the bottom edge the whole way), and a mid-range pad/synth layer (other stem). Reads as a mid-tempo hip-hop/trap-style beat, not orchestral.
- **The bed comes up when nobody talks**: in dialogue the voice stem sits about 15 dB above drums and "other" (hook: voice -20, drums -36, other -36); in the montages voice drops to -27/-28 and drums/other rise to -24/-26 *(inferred ducking or a mix ride)*.
- **Sound effects on the process shots** *(inferred)*: broadband noise bursts on the spray and pressure-jet shots (7-9.3 s, 21-22.5 s, 24.4-25.8 s) show up in the mix and leak into the vocals stem; short transients on wipes and the vacuum.
- At 40.2-41.6 s a dense harmonic block in the vocals stem: the laugh turning into the scream, the loudest vocal moment.
- The live tag switches to a different track *(inferred)*: the bass jumps 5 dB, harmonic lines in the vocals stem and whisper picks up words there that are not dialogue, so likely a sung track.
- Delivery: median voice pitch 240 Hz (several voices, including a woman and shouting men), pitch range -12 to +7 semitones around it, variability 7.0 semitones (very animated, shouted); 3.2 words/s inside spoken runs, long silences between dialogue blocks (longest 11.6 s, the second montage).

## 7. On-screen text (an editing layer; our rule 32 keeps captions out of the generated clips)
- **Word-by-word dialogue captions**: one word at a time, bold italic condensed sans, all caps, **bright yellow fill with a thin black outline and soft shadow**, centred horizontally at about 31 % from the top in the dialogue wides (over the car bonnet) and up to the frame centre on close-ups. Each word replaces the last on its spoken onset; a word can linger across a cut (it carries over the 0.1 s black frame at 16.0 s and into the first frame of a montage shot).
- **Time-jump title**: white bold sans caps, centred, on a hero-object close-up, held 0.7 s right after a white flash.
- Captions only during speech; the montages are text-free.
- In-world text (van livery, clothing logos, phone UI) is AI-garbled or uses a real brand mark; that is the source's look, not something to copy.

## 8. The style pattern (look, light, colour, camera)
- **Render**: stylised 3D feature-animation characters (big eyes, sculpted brows, smooth skin, caricatured builds: the man is a near-square jaw and huge torso, the woman an exaggerated hourglass, the teen slim with a lean adult-ish face) inside **near-photoreal environments and props**. The process macro shots (wheels, foam, paint, vacuum, garage) are almost photoreal; only the hands keep the soft CG skin. Setting realism > character realism.
- **Talent look**: the hero teen is consistent across 20+ shots (backwards slate-blue cap, curly dark hair, white tee with a navy chest logo, thin silver chain, black trousers, white sneakers). He changes outfit only for the payoff (designer cap, tinted glasses, gold chain, cream logo tee): the "transformed hero" read. Side characters drift: the man is redesigned between the opening and the payoff (navy suit and dark hair, then cream shirt, blond and bigger), and the house changes from a modern white villa (shots 1-11) to a terracotta-roof Spanish house (13-14). Expressions are big and readable with the sound off: saucer eyes, wide-open jaw, smug half-smile.
- **Framing**: vertical, people placed centre or centre-right; dialogue wides use a **foreground object layer** (the car bonnet, the car door and mirror) with the group behind; eye level or slightly low on the characters; close-ups are chest-up MCUs. Macro inserts fill the frame with the object.
- **Camera**: mostly still. 25 of 34 CG shots read as static or locked off. Movement is saved for reveals: the pull-out from the clean bonnet to the stunned group (shot 11, -12.5 %/s zoom with a tilt up), the push on the arriving car (28), the rise over the car into the payoff two-shot (29), and a whip-blur crash zoom into the final scream ECU (29, 40.9-41.2 s). Process shots move the subject, not the camera.
- **Transitions**: hard cuts throughout; one **white flash** for the time jump (25 into 26: the frame blows out to near white at 30.0 s); a 1-frame dip to black at 16.0 s and 32.7 s; a jump cut inside shot 2; the whip-blur into the scream.
- **Light**: hard high daylight from back/right with blue-sky fill and warm bounce off pale paving in the exteriors (warm, sunny, mid-afternoon, long-ish shadows in 28-29); cool neutral overhead strip and hex-LED light in the garage montage; a dim single top light in footwells.
- **Colour** (my samples, frames averaged): hook #7f6760 (R-B +31, 44 % light, 14 % sat); first montage #85574e (warmest, R-B +56, 26 % sat, the red car); reaction #8e6963 (R-B +43); garage montage #5e5f59 (neutral, R-B +5, 3 % sat); payoff #766063 (R-B +19). Kit per-shot warmth: villa exteriors +7 to +13, red-paint inserts +29 to +32, garage -4 to +6. So: **warm sunlit exteriors, neutral-cool interiors, saturated hero-object colour** (red, purple). No teal-orange split in the sense of blue shadows on faces: shadows are neutral to slightly warm. Blacks are deep but open; highlights clip only on specular paint and the white flash.
- **Depth of field**: moderate. Backgrounds soften behind close-ups but stay readable (villa, palms); the wides are fairly deep.

## 9. Format notes this video shows (dialogue skit)
- Cold open mid-conflict, a status gap shown by setting and props, a dare, then **wordless proof montages** carried by the music bed and SFX, a short reaction/reward dialogue, a growth montage, a time-jump card, a mirrored return to the opening location for the payoff line, a comic scream button, and a 2.3 s live-action creator tag.
- Dialogue is clustered in three blocks (0-5.3 s, 14.8-19.5 s, 35-41.6 s); about 60 % of the runtime has no dialogue.

## 10. Why it works (for a viewer)
- 0-2 s: motion and shouting in frame 1, then a cut to an outsider who clearly has a plan.
- 5-13 s: "satisfying" macro cleaning, one reveal per second, no words needed; every cut is a tiny before/after.
- 13.6 s: the pull-out reveal pays off the montage on the faces; the saucer-eyed reaction lands the reward with the sound off.
- 20-30 s: same action, escalating cars and scale, then the white flash and title card signal the jump.
- 34-41 s: the longest hold lets the payoff exchange play in one take, then the whip-blur into the scream ECU is the button.
- 41.7-44 s: real-life handheld flex clips tie the cartoon to a real person.

## 11. What carries over to a new script (for the director)
- Keep: stylised 3D characters with big readable faces in near-photoreal places; one consistent hero outfit (changed once, on purpose, for a transformation); static or near-static frames for dialogue, with a foreground object layer; movement saved for reveals (pull-out reveal, push on an arrival, one crash zoom for the comic button); wordless process montages at about 1 cut per second with matched before/after frames; a white flash plus a title card for a time jump; a payoff held long in one shot.
- Pace: dialogue holds 1.5-3.5 s (payoff up to 7 s); montage 0.6-1.8 s; the bed always on, louder when no one talks.
- Editing layer: yellow word-by-word captions, centred; a white title card for time jumps.
- Do not copy: the real car and clothing brands, the van livery, the garbled phone UI, the redesign drift of side characters and houses, the live-action creator tag (it belongs to that creator, not to a brand video) *(judgement)*.
